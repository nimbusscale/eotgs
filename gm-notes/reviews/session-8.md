## 1. Summary

**Top 3 strengths (system-specific)**
1. **Player-authored, irreversible cost — Garland sacrifices the memory of his son.**
   In the spirit realm the GM workshops a "leave part of yourself here" cost live
   (833-848), and the player chooses to give up the memory of his first child, Dafydd
   — and the GM makes it stick: Garland no longer remembers him at all (859-862). Think
   Dangerously aimed straight at a PC's emotional core, irreversible, and *owned* by
   the player. This is the apex of the ledger's co-authorship + Hard-consequence
   strengths.
2. **The S7 carry-forward landed — a 7-9 Defy led with a real ugly choice.** Paxton's
   Defy +DEX to dodge two tacklers (7-9) resolves as a Make Them Choose: "you can still
   leap out of the way, but in doing so you'll end up knocking over one of these memory
   stones… You wanna knock it over or take the tackle?" (1596-1604). Exactly the fix the
   S7 review asked for — the cost is a choice the player owns, both branches make
   concrete fiction.
3. **Spotlight cadence held under a four-way split + intercut brawl.** The memory
   stones round-robin, then three threads, then a three-location fight, all cut on beats
   (e.g. 676, 863, 1060, 1323, 1607). GM mic share **49.4%**, players **50.6%** — the
   second straight session players out-talk the GM. The table explicitly endorsed the
   handling (2337-2344).

**Top 3 growth areas (ranked by impact)**
1. **The Loka theological standoff ran on repeat — a missed "make something happen"
   Move.** The argument cycled "he's lying / no, we're certain" without escalating
   (886-1055, 1357-1476). The GM self-diagnosed it correctly (2350-2367). Fix in §5 —
   cut the stall with a **Make Them Choose** (the warriors slip the child toward the
   Suonet *while* they argue).
2. **Consequence variety dipped to a condition-default in the upstairs brawl.** The GM
   caught himself — "doing a condition to you is kind of boring… trying to think of what
   else could be interesting" (1900-1903) — then did the condition anyway. Fix in §6.
3. **Encourage Exciting Risks in the info scene — still mostly a free briefing.** The
   Vaalo/Hiillos lore is handed over with no in-the-moment jeopardy (388-476). Same flat
   note as S5/S6/S7, though the Hiillos-as-objective at least points at a dangerous quest
   (423). Fix in §4.

**Quick wins for next session**
- When a social scene stops *evolving*, make a Move to force it forward — don't let it
  loop (the Loka fix, §5).
- In the upstairs brawl spot where you reached for a third condition, reach instead for
  **Take Something Away** or **Change the Environment** (§6).
- Frame one piece of the next lore-dump as a ticking risk before you hand it over (§4).

**Notable moments (why, in CA terms)**
- **Castor answers the Harlequin and leaps in as a gorilla (763, 1273).** The Dark
  Harlequin's whisper — "all you do is watch society" — is a precise tempt aimed at
  Castor's arc, and it pulls him from spectator to actor: he shapeshifts mid-leap to
  snatch Itu. This is the exact directed beat the S7 review asked for, and it's a Druid
  playbook move done right.
- **Loka snaps Roderic's sword (1543).** Roderic's Engage 9 chips the stone and reveals
  flesh beneath — *and* Loka snaps the blade in half. A **Take Something Away** as the
  7-9 cost: fiction-forward, irreversible, not a condition.

## 4. The five GM Principles

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks — Weak (this session's weakest, as in S5/S6/S7).** The
   Vaalo/Hiillos exposition is a free briefing (388-476). _Fix: frame a piece of the lore
   as a live, ticking risk before handing it over. As Holvi explains the children of
   Hiillos, **Foreshadow an Ominous Force:** "As he speaks you feel the floor-hum stutter,
   and Holvi stops, troubled — 'the children grow weak faster than I have ever known. If
   Carnforth has truly gone dark, the others will follow within the season. You would have
   to reach the cradle before the next one dims.'" Now the lore is a clock, not a lecture._
2. **Portray a Lively World — Strong.** The Slate schism, Amu's trauma-grief, the caste
   "garb," the pillar that turns out to be a very old person (1151).
3. **Think Dangerously — Strong.** Dafydd sacrificed (859), Roderic's sword snapped
   (1543) and Roderic thrown down the stairs (2115), conditions landing on PCs, and the
   tentacles breaking the ceiling at the cut (2146).
4. **Leave Things Open — Strong.** The entire memory-stone content is co-authored;
   Garland narrates his own vision; the spirit-realm ritual cost is workshopped live and
   Roderic is invited to author the risk (824-829).
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over — Good Ask, one baggy Gloss.** Excellent asks throughout the
   visions ("what would be important for Paxton to experience?" 103; "what does Dafydd
   look like?" 852). The Gloss miss is the Loka standoff, which should have been cut
   sooner — his own note (2350-2355), and §5.

## 5. GM Moves — usage, variety, and missed opportunities

**Used well**
- **Make Them Choose** — Paxton's tackle-or-knock-the-stone (1596); Amu's bargain to
  Garland (give the child and leave peacefully, refused, 1659-1662).
- **Take Something Away** — Garland's memory of Dafydd (859); Loka snapping Roderic's
  sword (1543).
- **Foreshadow an Ominous Force** — the cat-sized spiders closing on the stones (1098),
  then the tentacles through the ceiling at the session cut (2146).
- **Introduce Something New / tempt the Druid's arc** — Loka entering on "what powers
  are those?" (476); the Dark Harlequin's whisper aimed at Castor (763).

**Missed — the most important spot a Move should have fired but didn't: the Loka stall.**
The Roderic/Paxton vs. Loka argument cycles the same beats — "Moldrex lies / no, we are
certain" — across two long stretches (886-1055, 1357-1476) without the situation
*evolving*. A PbtA scene that stops producing new fiction is the cue to make a Move. _Fix
— cut the loop with a **Make Them Choose** built from the scene's own stakes: "While you
argue, two of Loka's warriors slip past toward the Suonet with the child cradled between
them — Loka means to have them gone before you finish your sermon. Keep working on him, or
break off and chase the child?"_ That converts a stalled debate into the exact lever
(win-the-argument vs. stop-the-child) he used well elsewhere this session. _Alternatively,
**Set Up an Immediate Danger** off Loka himself: "Loka's patience is gone — he plants
himself in the doorway and the stone of his fists grinds as they close. You have one more
sentence before he stops talking and starts swinging."_ Either makes the scene move.

## 6. 6- and 7-9 handling (the heart of PbtA)

_Strong and varied at the thread level; one localized dip to conditions in the upstairs
brawl._

- **Castor — Examine the charged scene (+WIS) → 7-9.** Answered truthfully (the vibes:
  Amu confronting the leader, warriors forming a perimeter, the child about to be taken,
  725-729), then the Dark Harlequin whisper lands as the soft Move (763). _Handled well —
  the Harlequin tempt is a textbook playbook beat aimed at his arc._
- **Garland — Engage the spider (+STR) → 7-9.** Trade blows; poison narrated up the arm
  *before* the player picks STR (1138). _Handled well — cause-first condition._
- **Roderic — Engage Loka (+STR) → 9.** Hits, chips the stone to reveal flesh, *and* Loka
  snaps the sword in half (1543). _Handled well — a Take Something Away as the 7-9 cost,
  irreversible and pure fiction._
- **Paxton — Defy +DEX vs. two tacklers → 7-9.** Make Them Choose (knock over a memory
  stone of unknown effect, or take the tackle; he takes the tackle, conditions DEX,
  1596-1604). _Handled well — the model 7-9 of the session; cashes the S7 rec._
- **Paxton — Compel the soldier (+CHA) → 7-9.** The Favor bargain: released only on a
  promise to keep Loka alive (2047-2051). _Handled well — a real string attached, not a
  flat success; varied social cost._
- **Roderic — Decree → 6-.** Cut off mid-decree by a punch to the face, take a condition
  (1785). _Handled well — a Hard-ish Move on the miss._
- **Garland — Engage the warriors upstairs → miss/partial → condition (1736), then miss →
  condition again (1903).** This is the dip. On the *second* miss the GM said aloud "doing
  a condition to you is kind of boring… trying to think of what else could be interesting"
  (1900) — and then did the condition. _Verdict: handled, but the consequence palette
  flattened where the GM himself wanted it not to._ _Fix — that 1898 miss was the spot for
  **Take Something Away** or **Change the Environment**: "Your wild swing tears Second
  Harvest from your grip — it skids across the floor, between you and the warriors. Lunge
  back for it and leave the child a step exposed, or stand over Castor bare-handed?" (Take
  Something Away + a built-in Make Them Choose). Or **Change the Environment:** "Your blade
  bites a support column — one of the petrified watchers — and it cracks and topples across
  the stair, sealing you and Castor off from Mira's circle." Either keeps the brawl kinetic
  without a third condition._

## 7. Conditions, consequences & fictional positioning

- **Conditions narrated cause-first, player choosing stat + manifestation** — Garland's
  spider poison (1138), Roderic's punch (1785). At 1739/1752 the GM reaffirmed correct
  procedure ("I never choose the stat, you choose the stat"). Good.
- **Consequence variety** — broad at the thread level (a player-authored memory
  sacrifice, a snapped signature weapon, a Favor debt, a Make-Them-Choose, the spirit-realm
  reveal) but defaulting to conditions/armor inside the upstairs brawl (§6).
- **Fictional positioning respected** — the GM correctly told Roderic he could *not*
  knock out the stone Loka with a punch and offered a condition-or-subdue instead
  (2107); steered Castor's "talk to the rocks" and the gorilla shapeshift through the
  right Moves (1275 — rolling for the *story beat*, not "do you turn into a gorilla").
- **One positioning slip toward too-safe** is the Loka stall (§5) — a scene that wanted a
  Move and got more dialogue.

## 8. Missed character moments

- **Castor (Druid — Drive: endanger yourself for wildlife/outcasts).** *Not* a miss in
  the scene — the GM did precisely what the S7 review asked: the Harlequin tempt pointed
  him at the child (763) and he risked himself as a gorilla to save Itu (1273). The miss
  is structural and recurring: the drive *still* didn't fire, and the table spent End of
  Session relitigating whether the child qualifies as an "outcast" (2282-2293). After six
  sessions, the evidence now points at the **drive text**, not the GM's setup. _Fix — with
  the player, re-anchor Castor's drive to the arc the table is actually playing: spectator
  → one who acts; reclaiming the human life he let pass. A drive like "act on the world
  instead of watching it — step in where the old Castor would have stayed a beaver" would
  have been claimable on the gorilla leap and on the memory-stone grief, and stops the
  per-session "does this count as wildlife/outcast?" debate._
- **Roderic (Paladin — test his Virtues).** Loka is the corrupt/rival authority leading
  his people to a dark god — well engaged. The one untaken beat: when Loka's flesh is bared
  beneath the stone (1543) he becomes *killable*, and Roderic chose not to — a Mercy beat
  the GM could have pressed harder. _When Roderic next has him exposed: **test his Virtue
  directly** — "He's flesh now, Lightbearer, and the child's fate hangs on the next breath.
  Lucifer's mercy or Lucifer's justice — strike him down to end it, or stay your hand and
  let him reach Moldrex?"_ Minor — Roderic largely self-tested.
- **Paxton (Immolator) — well engaged.** Unleash Radiance burned Loka's flesh (1577), the
  Favor bargain (2047). Drive unclaimed by the player's own call; the GM handled it well at
  End of Session (checking it won't become perpetually unclaimable, 2228-2264). No miss.
- **Garland (Fighter/Wizard) — best-served.** The Dafydd sacrifice, the ley-line mystery,
  and the executed drive *change* (the epiphany move) are a full arc beat in one session.
  No notable miss.
