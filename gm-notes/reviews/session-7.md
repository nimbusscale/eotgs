## 1. Summary

**Top 3 strengths (system-specific)**
1. **The S6 cadence fix landed — tight intercutting on beats.** Where S6 ran three
   ~30-minute vignettes back to back, S7 rotated in short segments and cut on
   cliffhangers: Paxton dragged into the mountain → cut (697); Roderic hauled out of
   the river → "cut to someone else" (1537); "cut back to Roger just to get everyone
   together" (1720). GM mic share also dropped to **47.6%** — the lowest of five
   sessions (players out-talked the GM for the first time). This directly cashes the
   S6 carry-forward.
2. **Foregrounding the grim choice *before* it lands — the barge rescue.** Paxton
   wades back and the GM leads with a clean **Make Them Choose**: "you think you can
   save one of them… either the woman and the child or the man, but you cannot say
   both" (211-212). Roy burns by Paxton's decision, and the corpse Paxton found
   earlier turns out to be the woman's lost son (233). This is exactly the refinement
   the S6 review asked for (foreground the choice, don't reveal the cost as
   aftermath).
3. **Portray a Lively World — the Slate.** Petrified ancestors fused into the walls
   and roofs of the town (1548-1550), worn-out-9-volt-battery stones (847-849),
   rock-grinding speech rendered as "vibes" and text-bubbles (968-978), vines that
   move/resist fire/grow huge, a newborn whose skin is smooth flesh among the stone
   (2109). A genuinely alien culture built from a few concrete sensory hooks.

**Top 3 growth areas (ranked by impact)**
1. **One soft 7-9 Defy — the trample reverted to a "worse outcome" reveal.** Roderic's
   8 on the warhorse charge (462) resolved as "there are more men ahead + you notice
   the lantern necklaces" (482-484) — the GM himself said "Claude continues to berate
   me that I'm not following this move directly" (475). No sacrifice, no ugly choice.
   Fix in §6 (and it was a doubly-missed Paladin Virtue beat — Garland teed it up at
   467).
2. **Encourage Exciting Risks — the tavern was a free briefing again.** The militiaman
   hands over the Roland lead with zero jeopardy (1157-1234). Same flat note as S5/S6.
   Fix in §4.
3. **Castor's drive still unclaimed, focus still thinnest (7.4% mic).** At End of
   Session he again can't point to a drive beat (2333); the Slate-as-outcasts angle
   sat right under his Druid drive and never became a decision. Fix in §8.

**Quick wins for next session**
- On a **7-9 Defy**, lead with the ugly choice/sacrifice (as you did on the barge),
  not a reveal — see the trample fix in §6.
- In an info scene, frame the lead as a risk before you hand it over (tavern, §4).
- Give Castor one directed beat that lets him claim "endanger yourself for outcasts"
  among the Slate (§8).

**Notable moments (why, in CA terms)**
- **The tomb collapses under Roderic (1456).** A clean Hard Move at a non-roll
  trigger: he reaches for the buried objective and the ground gives way, plunging him
  into the underground river — *Change the Environment + Take Something Away* (his path
  to the tomb is gone). It also delivered him to the convergence. Model unprompted
  Hard Move.
- **Mira's Tongues failure costs her human speech (1060).** On her 5 she gains the
  rock-grinding tongue but can now *only* speak that way — a *Take Something Away* cost
  that's pure fiction, not a condition. Exactly the varied 6-/7-9 texture the ledger
  rewards.

## 2. Continuity — am I improving?

**Open recommendations checked:**

- **[S6, new — top of the list] Intercut split threads at dramatic beats — ✅ adopted.**
  This was the headline growth area from S6 and it's the clearest win of S7. Segments
  were short and cut on beats rather than run to completion (697, 1537, 1720). Two
  out-of-game tangents still fell *during* cut-aways (the GPU chat ~714-786; the Twin
  Peaks chat ~1463-1535), but the structural habit changed.
- **[S5→S6 ✅] Point the world-break at a PC + foreground the choice before he
  commits — ✅ confirmed.** The barge rescue leads with the Make Them Choose (211-216);
  Roy's death is owned by Paxton's decision, not learned from the riverbank. The S6
  refinement is now demonstrated.
- **[S3→S6, ◐ trending up] Make a Move *at* a PC at non-roll trigger points — ✅
  largely landed.** The ambush springs with an arrow off Roderic's horse-armor and six
  men closing (420-422); the giant vine bats the Slate youth down at Paxton (577); the
  tomb collapses (1456); the spider erupts on Paxton's *unwanted attention* (644). No
  uncashed social threat this session (unlike S6's demand-for-Castor). The remaining
  softness is a *roll* consequence (the trample, §6), not a non-roll miss.
- **[S5/S6, flat] Encourage Exciting Risks in exposition-heavy scenes — ⬚ flat.** The
  tavern info-gather is a free briefing again (§4).
- **[S3→S6, ◐ ongoing] Re-anchor Castor's ill-fitting drive — ⬚ not yet.** Unclaimed
  again at End of Session (2333); thinnest focus again (7.4%), compounded by IRL
  distraction. The line is still unsettled (§8).

**Recurring strengths confirmed:** Portray a Lively World (the Slate), Leave Things
Open / collaborative authorship (the lantern co-authored at 22-51; Roland and the
necklace-cult improvised on the fly), varied 6-/7-9 consequences (Mira's voice, the
tomb collapse, Paxton's near-crumble).

**Recurring growth area — refined.** The dice handling regressed on exactly one roll
(the trample 7-9), after S6 had nailed the ugly-choice texture on the barge. Note it's
*one* roll, not a pattern — the barge Make Them Choose this same session was textbook.

## 4. The five GM Principles

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks — Weak (this session's weakest, as in S5/S6).** The
   tavern is a free briefing: the ex-militiaman volunteers the Roland lead and the
   spineright-necklace detail at no cost (1157-1164). _Fix: frame the lead as a step
   toward danger before handing it over. **"The man lowers his cup — 'They came through
   asking after a paladin by name. Whoever sent them has coin and a long reach, and the
   last man who went poking after that lantern necklace turned up in the marsh. You
   still want to know where their camp is?'"** Now the info points at a risky course
   instead of arriving free._
2. **Portray a Lively World — Strong.** The Slate (see §1).
3. **Think Dangerously — Solid.** Paxton to near-crumble (663-680), Roy dead
   irreversibly (211-233), Mira's human voice gone (1060), the tomb buried and Roderic
   plunged underground (1456). Pointed mostly at Paxton; Roderic's thread was light
   until the collapse.
4. **Leave Things Open — Strong.** The whole lantern/Hiillos seed was co-authored in
   the first five minutes (22-51); Roland, the necklace cult, and the Slate's "child"
   schism all grew out of improvisation the GM folded into canon.
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over — Good Ask, baggy Gloss at the end.** Strong asks throughout
   ("how does talking rock look on screen?" 954; "do they look more or less healthy?"
   540). The one drag is the closing stretch — reunion → settle-in → level-up →
   medicine/healing rules ran long after the scene's tension was already spent; a
   harder Gloss ("you settle in, the Slate party around you, level up between sessions —
   next time you set out for Carnforth") would have closed cleaner.

## 5. GM Moves — usage, variety, and missed opportunities

**Used well**
- **Set Up an Immediate Danger** — the ambush arrow + the six men closing on Roderic
  the instant his Scrutinize lands (420-422). The threat is telegraphed and springs at
  a non-roll trigger.
- **Change the Environment / Take Something Away** — the tomb collapse (1456): the
  objective is buried *and* the ground drops him into the river. One Hard Move that
  removes a goal and relocates the PC.
- **Introduce Something New / Foreshadow an Ominous Force** — the mutating vines
  ("it is almost like it is foreshadowing an ominous force," 596), the giant black
  widow, the worn-out second ring of Witch Stones. Arc threats planted in the world.
- **Take Something Away (social/identity)** — Mira's Tongues failure strips her human
  voice (1060). Fiction-first cost.

**Missed / under-used — the Paladin Virtue at the ambush.** Covered as a roll in §6,
because the softness is in how the 7-9 resolved. The Move that *should* have fired
there is **Make Them Choose** aimed at Roderic's Mercy — see §6 for the full
move-and-fiction.

## 6. 6- and 7-9 handling

_Mostly clean and varied. One genuinely soft 7-9 (the trample); the rest landed._

- **Paxton — Examine the barge to find the screams (+WIS, conditioned) → 7-9.** The GM
  bakes the answer (where the survivors are) into a **Make Them Choose**: save the
  woman+child or the man, not both (211-212). _Handled well — model 7-9: the cost is a
  real dilemma, foregrounded before it lands, and irreversible._
- **Roderic — Scrutinize the arguing man (+WIS) → 11 (10+).** Eyes of the Faithful
  reads "violence very soon, and he feels a little guilty about it" (398-401); the
  ambush then springs. _Handled well._
- **Roderic — Defy +STR, trample through the line on the warhorse → 8 (7-9).** This is
  the one soft spot. The delivered consequence — "there are more men ahead, you can
  barrel through them too, and you notice the lantern necklaces" (482-484) — is a
  worse-outcome reveal, not a sacrifice or ugly choice; the GM flagged his own unease
  (475). And Garland had handed him the perfect hook: trample a man wearing a holy
  symbol (467), i.e. a Paladin Mercy test. _Verdict: too soft; missed a Virtue beat._
  _Fix — lead the 7-9 with the ugly choice using the man who already fell. **Make Them
  Choose:** "The only clear gap is straight over the man your horse just knocked flat —
  put the hooves through him and you're clean away, or rein hard around him and the
  others close and drag you off the saddle. Which is it, Lightbearer?"** That forces
  his "never harm the helpless" vow into the open. Or **Take Something Away:** "You
  break the line, but a shaft buries in your warhorse's flank — it'll carry you clear
  of here and then it's spent. Ride it into the ground, or pull up and lose your
  lead?" Either makes the 8 cost something._
- **Paxton — Examine the vines, "are they a growing danger?" (+WIS, conditioned) →
  6-.** The GM withholds the read and lets the danger *be* the answer — the giant vine
  bats the Slate youth down the slope and Paxton must take a condition to save it or
  let it fall (577). _Handled well — correct 6- instinct (no free answer), and the
  save-it cost was narrated cause-first with the player choosing DEX + "sprained
  shoulder" (592)._
- **Paxton — Unleash Radiance to burn the vine (+CHA) → success, choose one.** He takes
  *unwanted attention* and burns his Spontaneous trait; the giant spider erupts (644).
  _Handled well — the cost the player picked drove the next threat._
- **Paxton — Defy +DEX to flee the spider → 6-.** "The spider jumps on you, its fangs
  sink in, doing another condition" (663); he conditions INT (poison, clouding vision),
  reaching four conditions and near-crumble, then is carried off into the mountain
  (691). _Handled well — a real Hard Move on the 6-: a condition that nearly drops him
  plus a *Throw Them Around* that relocates him into the Slate town._
- **Mira — Evoke Tongues → 5 (failure).** She gains the stone-tongue but loses human
  speech (1060). _Handled well — a fiction cost, not a flat condition._
- **Paxton — Ponder the lantern necklace (+CHA? read as INT) → 9 (7-9).** Vague but
  true: he recalls the Carnforth chaplain wore it (1893). _Handled well — incomplete
  truth that opens the cult thread._

## 7. Conditions, consequences & fictional positioning

- **Conditions narrated cause-first, player choosing stat + manifestation** — Paxton's
  sprained shoulder (DEX, 592) and poisoned vision (INT, 677) both done
  cooperatively after the GM narrated the cause.
- **Consequence variety stayed broad** — a condition to near-crumble, an irreversible
  death by player choice (Roy), a lost human voice (Mira), a buried objective +
  environmental plunge (the tomb), a burned playbook trait (Spontaneous). Conditions
  were *not* the default reach. Keep this.
- **Fictional positioning respected** — the GM correctly steered Castor's "talk to the
  rock people" through At One With The World as wordless vibes rather than inventing a
  bespoke roll (931-996), and let the fiction pick STR for the warhorse charge (449-456).
- The one positioning slip toward *too safe* is the trample 7-9 (§6) and the no-jeopardy
  tavern (§4).

## 8. Missed character moments

- **Castor (Druid — Drive: endanger yourself for wildlife/outcasts).** The Slate are a
  dying, deathless, birthless people — textbook outcasts — and Castor is the one PC who
  can feel the mountain "celebrating" (995) and speak their nature. Yet the scene never
  asked him to *risk* anything for them, so his drive went unclaimed again (2333).
  Engage it with a **playbook Move (introduce a wild spirit/creature in need)** pointed
  at the newborn: _"Castor — the little one with the smooth skin keeps drifting to the
  edge of the crowd, toward the dark where the big statues went. Nobody else seems to
  notice. The world-sense in you says it's not safe out there. Do you go after it?"_ Now
  the first Slate child becomes *his* outcast to endanger himself for — and that's a
  drive he can claim. (It also pre-seats the S8 fight over the child.)
- **Roderic (Paladin — test his Virtues; the helpless in his presence).** The ambush
  trample was the session's ready-made Virtue test, and Garland served it on a plate
  (467). Full fix is in §6 — lead the 7-9 with the Mercy choice over the fallen man.
- **Paxton (Immolator — have his fire burn something valuable; demand sacrifice).**
  Strongly engaged, not a miss — his fire took his own Spontaneous trait (631) and the
  barge cost an innocent life by his choice. The Drive ("act callously about death")
  drove the near-crumble climb. Keep this; the only refinement is the trait-loss could
  have stung more if named as a thing he *valued* losing.
- **Garland (Fighter/Wizard) — well-served.** The silent second ring carved in an
  unreadable tongue, the worn-battery bond, the campfire remnant (someone's been here,
  1108), and his stated intent to rebuild Mira's gate as a ritual (1122) are a Wizard
  mystery teed up for the arc. Mira's lost voice is his cost to carry. No notable miss.
