## Session 7: The Town of the Slate

**Date Played:** 2026-06-13

### Recap-Teaser

*Sir **Roderic Lightbearer** rides for the **Bonewall**, and he is riding back toward the worst night of his life.*
*Somewhere along that wall is the tomb where his company of paladins went in seeking a relic and came out as corpses — mutilated beyond knowing, the relic gone, and Roderic the only one to wake among them, remembering nothing.*
*Behind him he leaves a **Beaconhold** that no longer makes sense: a **Light** fractured into squabbling factions, scripture rewritten while no one was looking, the faithful vanishing from their beds without a struggle — **Primus Griswald** among them, the one man who ever believed Roderic innocent.*
*And where the hero **Sariel** once stood in marble, there is only a patch of dust no bust ever sat in.*
*Did the missing leave, or were they taken — and why does Roderic alone still remember a man the whole Church has forgotten?*

*Far to the south, old **Garland** and **Castor** stepped through a door made of song and stone — and they did not step through clean.*
*They leave the **Ashen Vale** boiling behind them: **Aldric** turning grasping and aggressive, the three great families arrayed against a talking beaver and the man who shelters him, and **Captain Vask** carrying word that Aldric somehow knows everything that happens in the vale.*
*Even the ravens are wrong — black shapes that are not quite ravens, creeping close to the warded **Witch Stones**, watching, tracking, reporting to something that should not be able to see this far.*
*Now **Mira**'s booming, borrowed tongue has torn open a portal onto the dead grey shale of the Bonewall, where a second ring of Witch Stones waits — and the three of them have crossed to the far edge of the world with no way home and no idea who built the door.*

*And in the marshes of the **Reaches**, **Paxton Lumnus** drags himself from the river while a barge burns at his back.*
*He has seen the first true face of the thing behind the failing wards — **Senna**, a Spinewarden he once served beside, healing scaled horrors with a scaled hand and reptile eyes, reborn not in **Lucifer**'s light but in the vision of **Moldrex**.*
*"You were reborn wrong," she told him, before the fire took her — and Paxton still does not know what Moldrex wants, what became of his own tower at **Hallowreach**, or why **Carnforth**, the northernmost Spine, has now gone fully and finally dark.*
*Three threads, three roads, and a single wall of bone where all of them are about to meet.*

### Summary

Three threads ran toward the Bonewall, and all three ended underground, in the same impossible town.

[[Paxton Lumnus|Paxton]] woke from his nap in the cold river to the screaming of people he had never meant to burn.
Wading back to [[Senna]]'s sinking barge, he found a man, a woman, and a child hidden among the cargo, and the fire would only let him save one side of that family.
He pulled the woman and her child from the window and left the husband, Roy, to the flames; the woman's son, he realized, was already the corpse he had found in the marsh.
He cradled no illusions about what his recklessness had cost.
The next morning, climbing toward dark [[Carnforth]], he met a young stone-skinned creature mourning at one of the [[Bonewall]]'s carved [[Bonewall|Watchers]], and followed it up a trail where the only vegetation — the gnarled grey vines — had begun to move, to resist fire, and to grow monstrously large.
A vine the size of a tree batted the creature down the slope; Paxton roped it to safety at the cost of a wrenched shoulder, burned the vines from a cave mouth, and was bitten near to death by a cat-sized black spider before the rock creature smashed it away and carried his failing body down into the mountain.

[[Castor]] and [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] spilled out of [[Mira]]'s portal onto a matching ring of [[Witch Stones]] high on the Bonewall — stones that hummed with the same earthen energy as Garland's own but were carved in runes he could not read, their voice worn down to the faintest whisper.
Two living statues and a small grey child scattered at their arrival, and a ring of stone-skinned people closed slowly around them, able to force out only single labored words: "child," "sequester."
Castor opened himself to the world and felt the mountain celebrating something new; Mira, reaching for the same connection, found she could now speak the grinding stone-tongue but could no longer be understood in any human language.
[[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]], meanwhile, fought free of an ambush at the edge of the [[The Reaches|Reaches]] — six men with lantern-symbol necklaces who had been sent, he learned, by someone named [[Roland]], specifically to keep the Lightbearer from reaching the [[Tomb of the Spinewright|Spinewright's tomb]].
Doubling back through the mountains, he found the tomb buried under a fresh rockslide, fell through collapsing ground into an underground river, and was hauled out by the same kind of rock creatures.

All three threads converged in a hidden town built from the petrified bodies of its own dead — the people who called themselves [[The Slate|the Slate]].
They nursed Paxton with moss, marveled at Roderic's metal armor, and through Mira's translation finally told their story: for as long as any of them could remember, none of the Slate had aged, none had died, and none had been born — until, very recently, a single child emerged from the mountain, the first in living memory, and the whole society had broken into celebration.
Roderic met Paxton for the first time and rejoined Garland and Castor; Garland privately recognized the sigil of Paxton's resurrection as the mark of what his youth had called the unborn, and said nothing.
The party settled in among the dancing Slate, agreeing to make for failing Carnforth first and the buried tomb after, while Garland turned his mind to the witch stones and the ritual that might one day send Mira safely home.

### Major Events

- Paxton waded back to Senna's burning barge after hearing screams he had not expected.
A family had been hidden among the cargo, and the fire would let him save only one part of it.
He pulled the woman and her young child free through the window and left her husband, Roy, to burn.
The dead body he had found earlier in the marsh, he realized, was the woman's lost son.
The needless deaths drove home that he could not keep spending lives — his own and others' — so cavalierly.
- Climbing toward Carnforth, Paxton followed a young stone-skinned creature and noticed the Bonewall's grey vines behaving wrongly — discolored, shifted, and far too large.
A monstrous vine knocked the creature down the slope; Paxton roped it to safety, wrenching his shoulder, then burned the strangely fire-resistant vines from a cave mouth.
A cat-sized black spider lunged from the rocks and bit him near to collapse before the rock creature crushed it and carried him down into the mountain to be healed.
- Castor came through Mira's portal in wind form and was struck by a vision of the valley as it was before the [[Beaconhold|Beacon]] was built — a rare moment of clarity recalling his life as a young carpenter in the early days of the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]], before the long centuries as a beaver erased it.
Shaken, he lost the wind form and reverted to human.
Garland followed and again felt the forgetting take hold, briefly reliving — but still unable to recall — the lost memory of the note he had once given Mira to carry to Aldric.
- On the Bonewall, Garland and Castor found a second ring of witch stones, twin to Garland's own but carved in an unreadable runic language and worn nearly silent.
Living statues and a fleeing grey child gave way to a ring of stone-skinned people who could force out only single words.
Castor used his bond with the world to sense the mountain's joy and goodwill; Mira reached for the same power and gained the ability to speak the grinding stone-tongue — at the cost of being unable to make herself understood in any human language.
A recent campfire near the stones marked that some human had passed this way not long before.
- Riding toward the Bonewall, Roderic was ambushed at the edge of the Reaches by six armed men feigning a quarrel, each wearing a necklace bearing the Spinewright's lantern symbol.
He scrutinized them, charged free on his warhorse, then traced them to their camp and overheard that they had been sent by someone named Roland to stop "the Lightbearer" from reaching the Spinewright's tomb — not to harm anyone else.
They feared his arrival at the tomb would put something in jeopardy.
- Roderic doubled back through the southern mountains to the Spinewright's tomb — the site where his paladin company had died and the portable lantern artifact had been lost — and found it completely buried under a fresh rockslide.
As he approached, the ground collapsed and dropped him into an underground river; a rock creature dragged him out and parted the stone like clay to reveal the hidden Slate town.
- All three threads reunited in the underground town of the Slate, built from the petrified bodies of their own dead.
The Slate nursed Paxton with moss and were fascinated by Roderic's armor.
Through Mira's translation they revealed that for as long as any of them could remember, none had aged, died, or been born — until, very recently, a single child emerged from the mountain, the first birth in living memory, sparking a celebration.
Roderic met Paxton for the first time; Garland silently recognized Paxton's resurrection-sigil as the mark of the "unborn."
- The party resolved their next steps: with the failing Carnforth tower now closer than the buried tomb, they will make for Carnforth first and return to the Spinewright's tomb afterward.
They settled in among the dancing Slate to rest, Paxton having been carried to the brink of death.
Garland devoted his attention to the witch stones, intent on reproducing Mira's long-distance gate as a ritual to send her safely home.

- Why has the [[The Slate|Slate]]'s long stasis — no births, deaths, or aging — broken now, and what does the first child in living memory signify? Is it an echo of the [[God of Renewal]]'s awakening?
- Why did the newborn Slate child flee from the party, and why do the rigid [[Bonewall|Watcher]] statues pursue it?

- What does the lantern necklace mean, worn by Roland's men and by reputation a [[Carnforth]] chaplain — a cult, a splinter order, or something tied to the failing wards?
- Do the [[Witch Stones]] form a network spanning the [[Ashen Vale]] and the Bonewall, written in an older or different language, and what secrets do the Bonewall stones hold?

### Questions Answered / Arcs Advanced

- The relic Roderic's company sought in the Spinewright's tomb is named: [[The Spinewright's Lantern]], a portable version of the great wards atop the [[Thirteen Spines]], whose flame pushes back [[Öuth Krelt]] for miles and purifies the land its light touches. It is missing; the company never recovered it.
- [[Senna]] did not die and return the way Paxton did — she was not killed and resurrected but transformed and "reborn" only in a metaphysical sense in the vision of [[Moldrex]], a change she accepted. Paxton's spontaneous resurrection is genuinely rare, divine, and unrelated to any ritual.
- The Bonewall wards and the Thirteen Spines are failing as a side effect of [[Sariel]]'s erasure from history, compounded by the party — the heralds of ruin — whose ruinous energies struck at that same unmaking.
- The three scattered threads converged: Paxton, Roderic, and the Garland–Castor–Mira group reunited underground in the town of the Slate, bringing the party back together (minus [[Edric Bloom|Edric]]) and joining Paxton to the group in play.

## Session 6: Three Roads to the Bonewall

**Date Played:** 2026-05-30
**In-Game Timeline:** The days before the heralds reunite, each moving alone through the changed world

### Recap-Teaser
> Three threads, three places, one wound widening.
> In a tunnel beneath Beaconhold a masked inquisitor wore a missing Primus's name to beg an alliance; at the Witch Stones a child sang open a door to the Bonewall and stepped through; on a burning barge in the marshes a dead friend rose with reptile eyes and named the thing that is remaking the drowned.
> The party is scattered along the Bonewall now — and the next time the dawn breaks, it will break over all of them at once.

### Summary
The reunion was still days off, and so each herald moved alone through the changed world, and each found the same rot wearing a different face.

In Beaconhold, [[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]] returned to a faith he no longer recognized.
The Light that had once stood unified in the city had fractured into bickering factions, and the rites and scripture he remembered no longer matched what the churches now taught.
A message summoned him to the old imperial tunnels beneath the city, signed by [[Primus Griswald]], the Primus who heads the Light's academies — the man who had recruited him as a boy and made him a knight-errant.
But the figure who emerged from the dark was not Griswald; it was the [[Corona Vigil]] inquisitor [[Inquisitor Voss|Voss]], whom Roderic had traded barbs with in the Aurelion vault, wearing the Primus's name as a lure.
Griswald was missing, the inquisitor confessed, and others of the faithful with him, vanished from their beds without a trace of struggle.
The Vigil remained pure, he insisted, but its old power and dread had thinned to almost nothing, and against his own orthodoxy he asked the half-heretic Roderic for help — to ride to the [[Bonewall]] and learn why the thirteen wards were failing.
Roderic, who could weigh the truth of a man's words, found no lie in him.
In that tunnel the old wound of Roderic's lost battalion finally surfaced: a company of paladins sent into the tomb of the hero who long ago stopped the darkness and raised the towers, to recover a relic they believed could let them push into the darkness on the far side of the Bonewall.
He remembers nothing of that night — only waking among his companions, all dead and mutilated beyond recognition, the relic gone; he is presumed the sole survivor, though the unidentifiable remains leave even that uncertain.
He was made an outcast for living through it, and Griswald alone had believed in him.
Later, in the church bureaucracy, Roderic pressed [[Vicar Lucis Gregory]] and learned worse news still — the missing were many, and on the desk where a bust of the hero [[Sariel]] had always stood there was now only a patch of undisturbed dust, as though the general of Lucifer's armies had never existed at all.
Gregory could no longer remember the man Roderic described, though Roderic remembered him clearly.

South in the [[Ashen Vale]], [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] and [[Castor]] sat to a Sunday dinner at the hidden [[Witch Stones]], where Garland now sheltered young [[Mira]] and helped her heal from the ruin that had wounded Crownvale's children.
The peace broke when riders came: [[Captain Vask]], head of [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]]'s guard, and [[Sergeant Iyer]], escorting the heads of the Vale's three great families, come to collect Aldric's runaway daughter and to put an end to the beaver troubles that had soured the lowlands.
Garland conceded Mira's return readily, and defended his old friend Castor and the beavers who had tamed the [[Ashen Flow]], but the parley curdled when Vask revealed that Aldric somehow knew everything that passed in the Vale — even that the beaver could speak.
Black ravens that were not ravens crept closer through the trees, one veering off the warded stones; and then Mira walked out of the shack speaking a booming, unknown tongue, and the Witch Stones blazed with their runes and tore open a portal onto the dead grey shale of the Bonewall, where another ring of stones waited.
She stepped through.
Garland recognized the gate-craft as a far stronger echo of magic he had once owned and lost; Castor became wind and rushed after her; Garland broke free of Vask, was tackled by Iyer, and with a desperate threat bent the sergeant to defy his own captain and grant him the chance to follow.
Both old friends crossed the threshold before it closed.

Far to the northeast, in the marshy [[The Reaches|Reaches]] below the Bonewall, the newest herald told his story.
[[Paxton Lumnus]], a dock worker's son turned Spinewarden, had been stationed at the failing spine of [[Bonewall|Hallowreach]] when its wards began to die.
When an elder's ritual demanded a life to renew the seal, Paxton volunteered, died, and was honored and entombed — only to wake at dawn in the mausoleum with the Light burning in his chest and a terrible certainty that the whole system of protection was flawed.
Bound for [[Aurelion]] to confront the church, he heard that the northernmost spine, Carnforth, had gone dark, and turned back toward the wall.
On the way he came upon a barge in the reeds, a body in the water, and scaled tentacled horrors that dragged at him from the marsh.
Inside he found [[Senna]], a fellow Spinewarden he had served with — but she knelt healing the creatures with a scaled hand and reptilian eyes, and she told him that she too had died and been reborn at a tower, but in the vision of [[Moldrex]], while he had been reborn wrong, in the vision of [[Lucifer]].
Paxton answered with fire, burning the barge down to the waterline and throwing himself into the river — and only as the screams rose behind him did he realize others had been hidden aboard, captives or stowaways he had never known were there, burning now because he had been too quick to spend his own life to count the cost to anyone else's.

### Major Events
- Returning to [[Beaconhold]], Roderic found the once-unified Light fractured into rival factions, its scripture and rites subtly altered from what he remembered.
- Lured to the imperial tunnels by a message bearing [[Primus Griswald]]'s name, Roderic met instead [[Inquisitor Voss]], who revealed that Griswald and other faithful had gone missing, that the Vigil's power had withered, and asked Roderic — despite doubting his purity of faith — to ride to the Bonewall and investigate the failing wards. Roderic confirmed the man told the truth and accepted the charge.
- The circumstances of Roderic's lost battalion were laid bare: a company of paladins sent into the tomb of the hero who long ago stopped the darkness and raised the towers, seeking a relic they believed could let them push past the Bonewall. Roderic woke amid his companions, all dead and mutilated beyond recognition, the relic gone — presumed the sole survivor, suspected of submitting to the darkness, and made a knight-errant by Griswald, who alone believed him innocent.
- In the church bureaucracy, [[Vicar Lucis Gregory]] confirmed that many of the faithful had vanished overnight and that scholars had found inconsistencies in scripture and history. Roderic noticed the bust of [[Sariel]] was gone from Gregory's office, yet the spot bore undisturbed dust, as though it had never stood there; Gregory could not remember Sariel even when described.

Castor became wind and followed; Garland broke Vask's grip, was tackled by Iyer, then bent the sergeant to defy his captain so he could cross. Both reached the Bonewall as the portal closed.
- Paxton recounted his death and resurrection at [[Bonewall|Hallowreach]] — volunteering for the ritual that renewed the wards, dying, and waking at dawn with the Light in his chest and the conviction that the wards are flawed. Setting out for Aurelion, he heard that Carnforth, the northernmost spine, had gone dark, and turned back toward the wall.
- In the marshes of the [[The Reaches|Reaches]], Paxton found [[Senna]], a Spinewarden he had served with, healing scaled marsh-creatures with a scaled hand and reptilian eyes. She told him she had been reborn "in the vision of [[Moldrex]]" while he had been reborn "wrong," in the vision of Lucifer. Paxton set the barge ablaze and threw himself into the river, killing Senna and her minions — only afterward realizing others he had not known were aboard had burned with them.

Combined with the letter from [[Rowan yn Greenholt|Rowan]] mentioning someone dressed in Aldric's livery near [[Ashbrook]] before the disaster, suspicion fell on the ambitious young lord.

Walking through the [[Ashen Vale]] toward [[Crownvale]], the party encountered a merchant named Jacob whose preserved fish had inexplicably rotted.
His young daughter spoke disturbingly of endings as natural and comfortable things.
[[Edric Bloom|Edric]] purchased a barrel of the ruined fish and a cart, planning to sell it as fertilizer.
The party debated whether the girl's words reflected the spreading influence of ruin on the minds of ordinary people.
[[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]] warned against reinforcing the philosophy of "new beginnings through destruction," fearing it empowered ruin further.

Arriving in [[Crownvale]], the party moved quickly.
[[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]], keeping his hood drawn to avoid recognition, purchased horses.
[[Edric Bloom|Edric]] distracted [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]]'s guards — including [[Sergeant Iyer]] — by buying them drinks at the tavern, where he pumped them for information.
He came away convinced Aldric was not involved in the ruin's release.
However, Edric experienced a disturbing vision of Sergeant Iyer's marital troubles and felt an almost irresistible compulsion to weaponize that knowledge — a whisper of ruin's influence that he barely resisted.
[[Mira]], Aldric's ten-year-old daughter, recognized Garland despite his disguise, and he slipped her a letter warning Aldric of the approaching danger.

A brief complication arose when the horse seller Laura accused Garland of theft — apparently confused or manipulated — but the guards recognized Garland and dismissed the claim.
The townspeople began turning against Laura, but Roderic intervened with a stirring blessing, urging compassion and attributing her confusion to the corrupting influence spreading across the land.
The party departed [[Crownvale]] on fresh horses, headed for [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]] to seek counsel from the [[The Light]] and the Wizard's College, knowing that Garland's spirit wall would not hold forever.

### Questions Answered / Arcs Advanced
- **Can Castor communicate with beavers?** — Not through verbal speech, but through gestures, body language, tail-slapping, and familiarity built over generations. He can convey urgency and general concepts but not nuanced ideas. (Established [[Session 1]])
- **What is the relationship between the old gods and the new gods?** — The [[Old Gods|old gods]] are forces of nature — uncaring, neither good nor evil, but indifferent to human suffering. The new gods, led by Lucifer, represent civilization, order, and care for people. Lucifer drove the old gods into shadow. The Wild Hunt and the force of Ruin are old gods. The old traditions call ruin "the Gentle Collapse" — a natural necessity, not a demon — suggesting the Luciferians may have imprisoned something that was never meant to be caged. (Established [[Session 1]])
- **What is the party's relationship with Count Marrow?** — [[Count Albrecht Marrow|Marrow]] hired them to retrieve the [[Seal of Unmaking]]. The party has decided not to return it to him, suspecting his motives. They plan to bypass [[Marrow County]] on their way to [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]]. (Established [[Session 1]])
- **Curse of Ruin advanced:** The party released the force of ruin, raced ahead of the cursed river, evacuated the beaver colony, and erected a temporary spirit wall. Ruin is spreading its influence across the land. The party now heads to [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]] for help.
- **Garland's Uncover the Truth advanced:** Garland chose this as his character arc, driven by suspicion that [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]] may be connected to the events at [[Ashbrook]]. Evidence mounts but Edric's investigation suggests Aldric himself is uninvolved.
- **Roderic's Redemption advanced:** Roderic's crisis of faith deepened when his healing was overwhelmed by ruin. His successful blessings in Crownvale offered a counterpoint — his faith can still move people.
