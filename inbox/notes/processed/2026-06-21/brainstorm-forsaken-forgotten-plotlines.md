## The Witch Stones, Mira, and the Forgotten Place

**Core concept:** The Witch Stones exist in between realities, the way the Xan-Kor sits within the Nodrum. Because they stand slightly outside linear time, the Xan-Kor's erasure cannot reach them. The stones are the world's witnesses: they still remember Sariel when scripture, busts, and clergy do not. The grinding Garland deciphered for centuries was the stones replaying history the world had already lost.

**Mira's attunement, two woven threads:**
- Canon already established: the ruin that harmed Crownvale's children drove her into depression, and it was then she began hearing the stones, whose magic started healing her. The wound is the doorway.
- New layer: when the Xan-Kor took Garland's memory of Mira at the Nodrum, that memory went to the forgotten place. A piece of Mira already exists there, which is why she hears the stones as words while Garland hears grinding. She has one foot in the forgotten place.

**Garland's regret beat:** Garland was the herald of forgetting. His curse spread the ruin that hurt Crownvale's children, including Mira. He forgot her once (Nodrum) and his curse wounded her. Reveal order: emotional gut punch first (the party, and Garland specifically, caused the harm), cosmic turn second (her wound is why she can hear, and why she may be the key to restoring Sariel).

**Portal mechanics idea:** Travel between stone rings passes through the forgotten place. Glimpses at first: a half-second of grey, the vale before Beaconhold, a tower being raised, a winged figure watching. Later, a misfire or deliberate ritual could strand the party there properly. Possible inhabitants: the Displaced, Sariel, maybe Edric (vanished beneath the Chryseum, which resonates with the stones).

**Restoration ritual shape (loose):** a place that remembers (the stone rings), a person who remembers (Roderic), a voice that can reach across (Mira), a body anchor (a surviving relic of Sariel, TBD).

**Counterweight/clock:** The Unraveling has reason to hunt and unmake stone rings one by one, since they are the last witnesses.

**Next session tease:** Introduce the Witch Stones as a potential avenue to reach the place where forgotten things go, and possibly recover Sariel.

## Castor: The Moldrex Theory

**Canon status of the curse:** Origin never established. Only known: ancestral land cursed, it picked on Castor in particular ("bad luck or revenge for a forgotten affront"), family barely affected, Dark Harlequin claimed the Laughing One did it (unconfirmed, possibly a lie). Timing sits in the Suppression era, the generation after Lucifer's ascension.

**The big swing:** Ascension has a cost. Shedding mortality means it lands on someone. Moldrex, before becoming the god of aberration and transformation, was someone Castor knew, possibly a childhood friend. When they ascended, their discarded humanity was dumped onto Castor, and the shape it took was the beaver. The curse wasn't punishment, it was a transaction, and Castor was the receipt.

**Why a beaver:** The friend's commentary on Castor. The dreamer of the pair became a god; the modest craftsman who never took the dreams seriously was remade as nature's small, diligent builder. The Dark Harlequin's taunt ("Why a beaver? Why not a lion?") becomes a riddle, and this is the Laughing One's "best joke": a god who couldn't stop being petty.

**Symmetry:** Moldrex builds with flesh, Castor builds with wood. Two halves of one severed person's craft. The discarded humble half may be the only thing that can unmake the god half.

**Dark Harlequin tie:** The Dark Harlequin is an emanation of the Laughing One (beheading doesn't kill emanations; the Xan-Kor is the template). Two emanations as the Comedy and Tragedy masks: lighthearted laughter and the Dark Harlequin. You can never kill one, only shift which mask is forward. Possible return vector: it manifests within objects (canon), so it could reappear inside something Castor crafts. It keeps visiting Castor because the Laughing One is watching the punchline ripen.

**Why Castor doesn't know:** Undecided. Options: centuries of beaver-brain buried it (reveal as recovery), or he never knew who his friend became (reveal as reinterpretation). Plan: have Dustin establish some backstory facts and build within those confines.

**Arc framing:** This can live under "the Forgotten" half of the arc title even though the God of Forgetting isn't involved. Seed slowly and deniably: a carving technique among Moldrex's aberrations Castor recognizes, converts using phrases from his childhood village, a name he can't place.

**Spinewright tie-in:** Castor the builder could be a piece of the puzzle in understanding the Spinewright's work (see Roderic section).

## Roderic: The Tomb, the Spinewright, and the Conspiracy

**The tomb hero is NOT Sariel.** Deliberate choice to avoid one figure being everything. New figure: **the Spinewright**, the mortal engineer-champion who physically raised the Thirteen Spines (Sariel handled the spiritual/ritual side). Buried with their instrument rather than grave goods.

**The relic:** The Spinewright's tool (hammer, horn, keystone, TBD). It can raise a ward tower, which means it can also unraise one. It has been missing since the massacre. A loaded gun on the mantel.

**What happened at the tomb (working theory):** A betrayal occurred inside. The bodies were mutilated as bookkeeping, not rage, destroyed so no one could count the dead. Someone walked out unaccounted for, carrying the relic. Roderic surviving was part of the design: a massacre needs a culprit, and a lone survivor with no memory is a ready-made scapegoat. He wasn't spared, he was cast.

**Griswald's true role:** Making Roderic a knight-errant was protection disguised as punishment, getting the scapegoat beyond the church's reach before a tribunal could finish the framers' work. (Name check: Primus Griswald is the vanished mentor; Vicar Lucis Gregory is the one with the missing Sariel bust.)

**The conspiracy within the church:** A hidden faction whose step one was stealing the Spinewright's relic via the tomb expedition, which they sponsored. Roderic's trauma was their founding crime. Working concept: a crusade movement that believes the Bonewall was never meant to be permanent and that the relic can open a path to march on Öuth Krelt and destroy Moldrex. Name TBD (Breaking Dawn, the Advance, or similar).

**New layer from this session:** The conspirators may have been spared the forgetting somehow. They remember Sariel, or remember enough, and see the lost angel as a chance to further their ends (ends TBD). This connects them to phase two.

**Phase two and Griswald's disappearance:** The framers waited years. The moment their operation on the wall begins, Griswald, the one man who shielded Roderic and may have kept investigating, vanishes overnight. What happened to him is deliberately unwritten. Options held open: taken, went to ground, or went to the wall himself ahead of Roderic.

**Roderic's collision at the Bonewall:** A church faction about to do something catastrophic with the relic taken from his dead, possibly led or aided by someone who walked out of that tomb and was believed over him, while he returns to the site of his trauma. Vindication versus stopping them.

**Held loosely:** A second survivor was floated (decorated, leading the rival mission) but Joe's framing keeps survivor identity ambiguous by design. The revenant/oathbound-dead idea was considered and set aside, not speaking to Joe currently.
