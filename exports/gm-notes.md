## Campaign Guide — GM Notes

See also: [[Curse of Ruin]], [[Old Gods and New Gods]], [[The Old Gods]]

### Campaign Theme

The world has been slowly dying for centuries — not dramatically, but through stagnation.
When mortal civilization turned away from the Old Gods and embraced the New, the natural cycles stopped.
Each era has been smaller than the last.
Nobody notices because nobody alive remembers anything different.

The party accidentally broke this stagnation by freeing Ruin.
They will restore Renewal.
When they do, the world wakes up — everything that was dormant stirs, everything that was frozen starts to move.
This is not a single crisis with a resolution — it is a fundamental change in the nature of the world.
The campaign is about living in that change, navigating it, shaping it where possible, and surviving it where not.

### The Awakening

When the world wakes up, everything stirs — not just the natural cycles, but the emanations in their prisons, the New Gods in their rivalries, and the mortals in their ambitions.
The ice that held everything in place is thawing.

The emanations are still powerful — diminished from their height, but far beyond anything the current era can easily handle.
The New Gods' institutions have been hollowed by centuries of stagnation.
The heroes who once sealed these forces away are gone — and in the case of [[Lucifer]]'s champion, literally erased from history.
[[The Xan-Kor]], in particular, is unsealing itself: by erasing the champion who defeated it, it is undoing the very victory that imprisoned it.
Reality is slowly catching up to the fact that the battle that put the Xan-Kor in its prison never happened.

The player characters are becoming the heroes this era needs — the first figures in centuries with the potential to contend with threats of this magnitude.
Not because they were chosen, but because they were there at every critical moment, and because Renewal is giving the world the capacity to produce greatness again.

### The Three Layers of Conflict

The campaign draws from three sources of tension.
These are not factions to be defeated — they are ongoing, overlapping layers of conflict that generate story arcs, hooks, and complications.
Any given session might touch one, two, or all three.

#### The Old Gods

Now that Ruin is free and Renewal stirs, the Old Gods' domains are reasserting themselves.
Their emanations — constructs like [[The Xan-Kor]], entities buried beneath standing stones, forces of nature taking form — are waking up.
Some mortals still keep the old ways — hedge witches, druidic traditions, rural communities that never fully converted — and their patrons are regaining influence after centuries in the margins.

**The nature of the threat:** The Old Gods don't hate humanity.
They don't notice humanity.
An emanation waking beneath the [[Ashen Vale]] is like an earthquake — it doesn't care about the town on top of it.
The Xan-Kor erases things from existence because that's what it does, not because it wants to.
The danger is impersonal, geological, elemental.

#### The New Gods

The world waking up destabilizes the New Gods as much as anyone.
Whatever rival lurks beyond the [[Bonewall]] tests [[The Light]]'s wards and fortifications along the range.
The Light's institutional foundations crack as the erased champion leaves gaps in their history and theology.
The New Gods are not unified — their rivalries with each other are as dangerous as anything the Old Gods might do.
The open question of how mortals became gods in the first place hangs over everything — especially now that Renewal makes new things possible again.

If [[Lucifer]] returns, that changes everything.
But so does Lucifer not returning.

**The nature of the threat:** Unlike the Old Gods, the New Gods have intentions.
Whatever rival waits beyond the [[Bonewall]] wants something.
Their conflicts play out through armies, churches, politics, and believers.
This layer is about war, faith, institutions, and power — divine conflict filtered through mortal structures.

#### The Mortals

The institutions that were supposed to serve mortalkind — churches, kingdoms, the old alliances — calcified during the stagnation into self-serving structures.
People follow [[The Light]] not because they chose it, but because it's all they've ever known.

When the world wakes up, that inertia breaks.
Renewal doesn't just affect gods and emanations — it affects people.
New ideas emerge.
Old grievances resurface.
Ambitious leaders see opportunity in the chaos.
Established powers cling to what they have.
Somewhere in the upheaval is the possibility that mortalkind could reclaim the agency they once had — not as subjects of gods, old or new, but as a force in their own right.

This is a renaissance and a crisis simultaneously.
The stagnation was suffocating, but it was stable.
What replaces it is alive but unpredictable.

**The nature of the threat:** Mortal conflicts are personal, political, and immediate.
Lords scheme for advantage.
Military commanders struggle to hold order.
Religious authorities enforce orthodoxy while the ground shifts beneath them.
Refugees flee dangers that didn't exist a month ago.
Trade routes become unsafe.
Alliances shift.

### 4. The secret and the offer

**The secret (the diplomatic lever).**
▶ **Itu is not the first to emerge — it is the first to survive.**
Others came before, stillborn, and **the town does not know. Only Aamu knows** (locked — not even Routa).
Aamu has hidden the failures out of **mercy, not coercion** (Moldrex is not holding it over them), protecting the people from despair.
The tragedy: the same protective silence that shields the town is what isolates Aamu enough for Moldrex's offer to take root.
The lever: if the party uncovers this, the move isn't "argue Moldrex is bad" — it's **bring the grief into the open, let Aamu not be alone with it**, competing with Moldrex on the actual wound. (Mira's creed cuts here: *the land remembers*.)

**The offer (Moldrex).**
Not a threat — a seduction, framed as a **divorce from the Old Gods**: the Slate are a people *of* the abiding order; Moldrex offers them out of it.
- **Control over birth** (Aamu's want) — make your own children, never again at the mercy of a mountain that keeps losing them.
- **Control over death** (Lohka's want) — never return to the stone; petrification stops being destiny.
Both ends of the lifecycle severed from the mountain. *The land endures / the land survives* — Mira's god — is exactly what Moldrex sells an escape from.

**The devolution (what the change actually is).**
The Slate's nature is to *harden* toward permanence; Moldrex's "evolution" is the exact inversion, dragging them **backward into soft biological life.**
Under the cracking stone is real flesh — they are devolving from enduring stone beings into perishable creatures, becoming *the revelers in the ephemeral* in the most literal sense.
**Mechanical/visual consequence:** the exposed flesh under broken stone is a **weakness the party can see and target.** The more transformed a warrior, the more flesh shows, the easier to wound — the "stronger, freer" Slate are the easiest to hurt.
▶ Staging: **Aamu not visibly changed** (still deciding, talkable, sympathetic); **Lohka and the loyal warriors** showing it — stone split at the joints, flesh beneath. Reads against [[Castor]]'s curse (§7: forced devolution vs. their chosen one).

> The **mutated vines are their own Bonewall phenomenon**, not part of the warriors' transformation. Keep them separate (§8).

### 6. Soft structure (beats, not rails)

A loose clock; players can move fast, slow, or sideways.

- **Beat 1 — Settle & Bond (the night).** Celebration continues; roleplay menu open (§7); Itu becomes real to someone. Plant Lohka and Aamu in scenes now so the next-day turn is a betrayal, not a stranger.

### 8. The combat (chosen, not forced)

The fight is **the warrior's refusal to die, made physical** — it happens because Lohka chooses it, not because the plot needed a brawl. Roderic gets his real encounter.

**Trigger:** the party talks **Aamu** down (via the secret, §4). Their stepping back removes Lohka's only road to immortality. **Lohka seizes the moment by force** and tries to take both Itu and the movement.

## The Spinewright — GM Notes

> **GM ONLY.** The player-facing entry is [[Spinewright]]; the relic is [[The Spinewright's Lantern]]. The public KB knows him only as "the Spinewright" — a foreigner and convert to [[Lucifer]] whose name is lost to time, and whose lantern bears the elder name Hiillos. This file keeps the rest: his true name, where he came from, what Hiillos really is, and the Slate connection.

See also: [[The Slate]] (and the GM note: The Slate), [[Bonewall]], [[Thirteen Spines]], [[The Spinewright's Lantern]], [[Carnforth]], [[Sariel]], [[Lucifer]]

### True name: Vaalo

The Spinewright's name was **Vaalo**.
It is lost now to the ordinary erosion of centuries — not torn out, the way [[Sariel]] was unmade.
This is the deliberate counterpoint to Sariel's erasure: one name violently removed from history and bleeding into the present, one name simply allowed to wear away the way all names do.
The Slate still hold it, because the Slate keep their past in stone rather than in words, and stone forgets more slowly than men.

*Pronunciation for the table: Vaalo = "VAH-loh."*
*Decision: not to be published; surfaced in play, most likely from the Slate.*

### Origin: Syvämaa, the Deep-earth

Vaalo was no son of the Imperium.
He came up from **Syvämaa**, "the Deep-earth" — an inner world at the core, a hollow beneath the world with its own firmament and a sun burning at the heart of the rock.
The true Light, in Vaalo's gospel, does not hang in the sky; it burns *below*.
What the Imperium worships overhead as Lucifer's radiance, Vaalo knew as a buried sun — and he came up out of the deep as a convert who married the two faiths together, raising the Bonewall's wards with the fire of the world's core in service to the Light of its sky.

*Pronunciation: Syvämaa = "SÜ-vuh-maa" — but it is a place, not a name anyone has to say aloud, so the hard word does no damage at the table.*
*This is the payoff for the hollow-earth idea: a future destination, an inner sky beneath the Bonewall the party could one day descend to.*

### Hiillos, the Ember

The relic is **Hiillos** — in Vaalo's tongue, "the kept ember / banked coal" (player-facing entry: [[The Spinewright's Lantern]]).
It is not a lamp of Lucifer.
It is a single coal banked from Syvämaa's inner sun, carried up and kept alive in stone the way the Slate keep their dead.
The wards atop the [[Thirteen Spines]] are the same elder fire, fixed and enthroned in towers; Hiillos is the portable ember of it.

At the heart of each great Spine beacon is the same humble thing: a banked ember, a living coal of the inner sun.
The bright beacon-flame the Imperium raised above it is *display* — the public glory of the Light, the fire that represents the Imperium — but the actual ward is the coal beneath, exactly as in Hiillos.
Hiillos is simply that mechanism made portable, stripped of the imperial fire-show.
This recolors [[Carnforth]]'s dead, dark beacon: not merely a flame let die, but an ember gone cold — the ward itself extinguished, with the glory-fire above it long since meaningless.

The Church absorbed the wards, renamed the maker a hero of Lucifer, and forgot the fire ever came from underground.

### Kehto, the Cradle

The [[Tomb of the Spinewright]] is named **Kehto** — "the Cradle" — in Vaalo's tongue.
To the Slate a tomb is not a grave: they do not die, they *settle* into stone, and Vaalo petrifying out on the wall's valley side — apart from any city, far from the mountain's heart — reads in their terms as a great sacrifice (see the GM note: The Slate, on petrifying outside the cities).
So Kehto is not where Vaalo died.
It is where he settled back into the stone that bore him — a return to Syvämaa, a cradle rather than a grave.

There is a deliberate rhyme to play on: the same rock that cradles the dead is birthing the living again.
Kehto names a return to the deep at the very moment the first Slate child in an age has emerged from that same deep — death-as-return and the cycle turning over, side by side.

*Pronunciation: Kehto = "KEH-toh."*
*Public KB hints "Kehto = cradle, not tomb" as an unexplained curiosity; the settling/sacrifice/return meaning is GM-only.*

### The Slate connection

The Slate also came up from Syvämaa (see the GM note: The Slate).
They are Vaalo's kin in the broad sense — the stone-people of the deep — which is why they alone still hold his name, and why their petrified-ancestor architecture and Hiillos's stone-kept ember share the same logic of life banked in rock.

Working hypothesis to develop in play: the Slate raised the Spines *with* Vaalo, or *for* him.
The lore the Light remembers credits imperial laborers who died by the hundred at [[Carnforth]], but the deeper, impossible stonework may be the Slate's.
This recolors the "Imperium's quarries" grievance in the Slate notes — the Imperium may have cut into a living people who were, through Vaalo, its own partners in raising the wall.

### What to hint vs. hide

Public / discoverable (already seeded in the KB):
- The Spinewright was a foreigner, not of the Imperium, a convert to Lucifer; his personal name is lost to time.
- The Lantern's elder name is **Hiillos**, imperfectly remembered to mean a kept ember rather than a lantern.

GM-only until found in play:
- The name **Vaalo**.
- **Syvämaa**, the inner world, the buried sun, the hollow-earth realm with its own sky.
- The Slate's origin in Syvämaa and their hand in raising the Spines.
- That Hiillos is a coal of the inner sun, and the elder Light burns *below*, not above.

### Open threads
- A descent to Syvämaa as a future destination — an inner sky beneath the Bonewall.
- Whether the inner sun is bound to an Old God of stone or the deep (ties to the Slate's open question about a stone god).
- How Vaalo died and came to the [[Tomb of the Spinewright]], and what his constructs and "trials of faith" were truly set to guard.
- Why a convert from the Deep-earth gave his fire and his life to the Light of the surface, and whether anything of that bargain still binds.


## The Slate

> **GM ONLY.** First contact happened in [[Session 7]] — the player-facing entry now lives at [[The Slate]] (and their town at [[The Slate Town]]). This file keeps the secrets the players have **not** learned: the Stagnation/Awakening framing, the Moldrex temptation, the birthing secret, the Imperium-quarry grievance, and the open mechanics below. Locked decisions and open questions are marked.

The live-play staging that draws on this lore lives in the GM note **Session 8 Prep — The Slate Town**.

See also: [[The Slate]], [[The Slate Town]], [[Bonewall]], [[Thirteen Spines]], [[Moldrex]], [[Öuth Krelt]], [[Senna]], [[Spinewardens]], [[Witch Stones]]

### What the party learned in Session 7
Through [[Mira]]'s translation, the party learned only the surface: that the Slate are living stone, are born from the mountain and petrify into their town's architecture in death, keep no records but their own dead, and that for as long as any of them can remember none had aged, died, or been born — until a single child emerged very recently, the first in living memory, sparking celebration.
The Stagnation/Awakening framing, the tie to the [[God of Renewal]] and the cycle restarting, and Moldrex's interest in them remain GM-only.

### Concept

A hidden people native to the [[Bonewall]]: humanoid rock-folk who emerge from the mountain itself, harden as they age, and petrify in place at the end of life.
Few outside the wall know they exist.
The strange carvings and statues that are sometimes found on the Bonewall are not artifacts.
They are Slate who have died.

They are also the truest embodiment of the Stagnation.
Their whole society stopped during the long freeze, and only now, with the Awakening, has the first new Slate in an age emerged.

### Origin and Nature

The Slate come from the mountain itself.
They are not settlers of the Bonewall, they are an expression of it.
They are born of a rocky kind of flesh, a mixture of stone and something living, and emerge already formed rather than being raised from infancy.

Emergence is tied to the Awakening (see [[Old Gods and New Gods]]).
As the world wakes, the mountain can produce new Slate again, after an age in which it produced none.

*Open:* the exact mechanism of emergence is still undecided, whether they rise spontaneously from the living rock, only under certain conditions, or have some form of reproduction.

### Syvämaa and the Spinewright (locked)

The Slate's deeper origin is **Syvämaa**, the Deep-earth — the inner world at the world's core, with its own buried sun (see the GM note: The Spinewright).
They are stone-people of the deep, kin in the broad sense to **Vaalo**, the foreigner the Light remembers only as the [[Spinewright]].
This is why the Slate alone still hold his lost name: they keep their past in stone, and stone forgets more slowly than men.

Working hypothesis to develop in play: the Slate raised the [[Thirteen Spines]] alongside Vaalo, or for him — the impossible deep stonework the Light credits to long-dead imperial laborers at [[Carnforth]] may in truth be theirs.
This sharpens the "Imperium's quarries" grievance below: the Imperium may have cut into the very people who, through Vaalo, helped raise the wall it sheltered behind.

Vaalo's own resting place reads in Slate terms too.
The [[Tomb of the Spinewright]] is named **Kehto**, "the Cradle" — not a grave but a settling, a return to the stone, and (petrified out on the wall, apart from any city) a sacrifice in their reckoning.
Play the rhyme: the same deep that cradles Vaalo's dead is birthing the first Slate child in an age.

The door to Syvämaa sits in the town itself: at its lowest point a great half-sealed **Descending Stair** vanishes into a deep amber glow rising from far below, a warmth felt more than heard.
Keep it shut for now — it plants the descent to the Deep-earth (Castor's curiosity, Mira's faith, the buried-sun/stone-god question) without opening it.
Keep the whole Syvämaa thread GM-only for the present; only **Suonet**, "the Veins" — the Slate's own fast-travel network of glyph-carved conduits linking fixed nodes north to Carnforth and south to Kehto, which the party nickname the Speedways — is surfaced to players, as their road through the wall.

### The Stagnation

During the long Stagnation the Slate froze completely.
No new Slate emerged, none aged onward, none petrified, none died.
Their society did not decline, it simply stopped, and they withdrew into their caverns and stayed there.
Where humanity stagnated by slow degrees and never noticed, the Slate are the freeze in its purest form.

One consequence to keep consistent: every petrified Slate, both in the cities and out on the dead shale, predates the Stagnation.
The cities are ancient and complete.
No new ancestor has joined their walls in an age, until now.

### The First Child

The Awakening has reached the Slate.
For the first time in an age, a new Slate has emerged, a child.
In their world this is the single most significant event in living memory, the first proof that the freeze has broken and their cycle can turn again.

**Plot hook:** the players come across this child out on the wall, or near it.
How they handle that encounter sets their standing with the Slate going forward.
It is a first contact, and a fragile one.

**Stakes:** the child is precious and unprecedented, the future of a people that had stopped having one.
That also makes the child exactly what Moldrex's agents would most want to claim or corrupt.
It can become a flashpoint between the party, the Slate, and the forces beyond the wall.

**The birthing secret (locked).**
The child — named **Itu**, "the Seed" — is not truly the first to emerge.
It is the first to *survive*.
Others came before, stillborn, and the town does not know it.
Only **Aamu**, the Keeper of Emergence, knows — not even Routa — and Aamu has hidden the failures out of mercy, not coercion, to spare the people from despair.
The stillborn forms are kept in the **Gallery of the Lost**, a quiet side-niche off the Cradle-Hollow, a row of small, unfinished forms in low niches, easy to miss and the one silent place in the town.
The tragedy is that the same protective silence that shields the town is what isolates Aamu enough for Moldrex's offer to take root: no one knows the weight Aamu carries, so no one can talk them out of it.
The diplomatic lever is therefore not to argue Moldrex is evil but to bring the grief into the open, so the keeper is no longer alone with it.

### Lifecycle and Petrification

When young, the Slate are nimble.
Their stone-flesh is hard but still flexible, and they move and bend as the living do.
As they age they grow larger, heavier, and harder, until movement slows and then stops.
They do not die of old age the way mortals do.
They petrify.

Petrification is a choice and an act, not merely an ending.
An elder settles where they mean to remain.
Within Slate society this is how their cities are built: the petrified become the architecture, ancestors literally forming the walls and halls of the places the living inhabit.
Where a Slate chooses to settle, and whether they are permitted to, carries weight and is a likely source of internal politics.

With the first child, the cycle that stopped is beginning to turn again.
In time, elders may begin to petrify once more.
The Awakening is restarting the whole loop, not adding a single life to a frozen one.

### The Dead, Sacrifice, and the Misread Statues

To petrify in place, within a Slate city, is the ordinary and honored end.
To petrify outside those places, alone and far from the mountain's heart, means something else.
It marks either a great sacrifice or a tragedy, a Slate who gave themselves, or who was lost where they should not have been.

This is what outsiders occasionally stumble on: a strange, finely formed statue out on the dead shale, with no record of who made it or why.
They are looking at a person, and at an expression of something far larger than a carving.
Tragedy or reverence, depending on the death.

> **GM note — the breadcrumb is already planted.** The player-facing mystery is seeded in [[Bonewall]]'s Description: travelers occasionally find a strange, finely formed statue weathered into the shale, framed purely as an unexplained curiosity of the wall with no hint of the truth. The reveal — that these are dead Slate — lives only here.

*Open:* how the Slate handle their dead, and whether a petrified Slate can ever be recovered or brought back, is undecided.
There may be a desire to retrieve those lost outside, with real complications around whether that is possible, permitted, or wise.

### Society

*Mostly open.* What is established: the Slate live in underground cities within or beneath the [[Bonewall]], hidden from the surface world.
There may be several such cities rather than one.
They have internal politics and tensions, which makes them a potential place for the players to explore, with complications that interweave with the main story rather than sitting apart from it.

### Hooks and Connections

**Moldrex and the temptation of change.**
The Slate live inside the very wall that holds back [[Moldrex]] in [[Öuth Krelt]].
Moldrex's pull is willing transformation and the seduction of becoming something new.
For a people whose destiny is to harden into stillness, that is the one temptation built to bite.
Moldrex can offer a Slate the single thing nothing else can: escape from petrifying into silence.

The mechanism is already in motion.
As the wards weaken and the Spines fail, Moldrex's agents are infiltrating the wall.
There has always been some quiet contact between the few who remain on the Bonewall, mostly the [[Spinewardens]], and what lies beyond it, and some have been seduced by Moldrex's ideology.
[[Senna]] is the established example.
The Slate are simply a new and richer target.

There is a thematic rhyme worth playing on: the Awakening offers the Slate real renewal, the first child and the cycle turning, while Moldrex offers a counterfeit, escape from stillness through transformation.
Real change and false change arrive at the same moment.

**The offer, made concrete.**
Moldrex's pitch is not a threat but a seduction, framed as a divorce from the Old Gods: the Slate are a people *of* the abiding order, and Moldrex offers them out of it, at both ends of the lifecycle.
- **Control over birth** — make their own children, like the surface folk, never again at the mercy of a mountain that keeps losing them. This is **Aamu's** want, born of the stillbirths only they know of.
- **Control over death** — never return to the stone; petrification stops being destiny. This is **Lohka's** want, the head warrior nearing petrification, who is not despairing but *wanting*.
Both are exactly what [[Mira]]'s emerging creed names sacred — *the land endures, the land survives* — so the rival faction becomes the literal doctrinal enemy of her faith: the rootless, the revelers in the ephemeral.

**The rival faction (embedded inside the town).**
This is not raiders from beyond the wall but Slate of the town itself, recruited by the offer.
- **Aamu**, the Keeper of Emergence, is the leader and the sympathetic core — reachable, because the wound is grief, not zeal, and they have the standing to redefine what the Slate are.
- **Lohka**, the head warrior, cannot lead the movement alone (no cachet), so they back Aamu; talk Aamu down and you do not stop Lohka, you *trigger* them, because their only road out of dying just walked away.
- **Sora**, Lohka's lieutenant, and the younger loyal warriors give the movement a second voice and the body of any fight.

**The devolution — what the change actually is.**
The Slate's nature is to harden toward permanence; Moldrex's "evolution" is the exact inversion, dragging them backward into soft biological life.
Under the cracking stone exterior is real flesh — they are devolving from enduring stone beings into perishable creatures, becoming the revelers in the ephemeral in the most literal sense.
The exposed flesh under broken stone is a visible weakness: the more transformed a warrior, the more flesh shows, the easier they are to wound — the "stronger, freer" Slate are the most vulnerable.
Keep this separate from the **mutated Bonewall vines**, which are their own phenomenon of the failing wards, not part of the warriors' transformation.
This devolution rhymes against [[Castor]]'s curse: the Slate *choose* the unwanted softening that was *forced* on him.

**The Imperium's quarries.**
The ruined Imperium quarry camps on the Bonewall read very differently if the mountain is a living people.
Whether the Imperium knew, traded, exploited, or simply cut into them unaware is open, but it is a historical grievance the Slate may remember even when no human does.

- Whether a petrified Slate can be recovered or revived, and the customs around the dead

### Appearance Notes (for image reference)

Life stages to keep consistent across images:

- **Child.** Smallest, smoothest, most supple, the palest bone-white, clearly new stone. Visibly distinct from the weathered older forms. The first of an age.
- **Young.** Lean and nimble, stone-skinned but supple, surfaces closer to smooth slate. Pale bone-white and light grey matching fresh Bonewall shale.
- **Aged.** Larger, heavier, more thickly armored in hardened rock, surfaces cracked and mineral, greys deepening toward weathered shale.
- **Petrified.** Fully still and statue-like, the form fixed mid-gesture or seated, weathered like the oldest near-black hollows of the wall. Reads to outsiders as a carving.

Palette ties to the established [[Bonewall]] look, bone-white through ashen grey to near-black, so the Slate read as the mountain given a body.
