# Chasing Adventure — GM-Craft Reference (for session review)

This is a condensed, coaching-oriented digest of the **Game Master** half of
*Chasing Adventure* by Spencer Moore, assembled specifically so the
`/review-session` command can evaluate GM play without re-scanning the full
18k-line `knowledge/chasing_adventure_reference.md`. When a ruling is ambiguous
or a move's exact wording matters, fall back to that canonical file.

Everything here is about **running this system** — Powered-by-the-Apocalypse
craft as Chasing Adventure expresses it. Use it to keep feedback system-specific
and to supply concrete, in-system alternatives whenever a moment was fumbled.

---

## The conversation loop (Play Order)

Chasing Adventure is a conversation governed by a cycle. Knowing the loop is how
you spot a *missed* move — a missed move is almost always a point in the loop
that got skipped.

1. **GM makes a Move** (hard or soft, following the Principles) to set up the
   situation. Usually ends with *"What do you do?"*
2. **Players describe their response** — what does it look like when they act?
3. **Is a Move triggered?** If the fiction matches a Move's trigger, follow the
   Move. If not, return to step 1 (the GM just keeps narrating / makes another
   Move).
4. **Is a roll involved?** If not, resolve and loop. If so, roll what the Move
   says. Freely remind PCs they can **Cooperate** and/or **Push Themselves**.
   If the stat rolled carries a condition, they roll at Disadvantage but gain 1 XP.
5. **Resolve by result:**
   - **10+** — follow what the Move says (usually the good outcome).
   - **7-9** — follow the Move; the GM enacts the cost/choice, **usually a Soft Move**.
   - **6-** — if the Move specifies what happens, follow that. **If the Move
     doesn't say, or says "in addition to what the GM says," the GM makes a Hard Move.**
6. If a condition was inflicted, **the PC chooses which stat** it lands on and
   **describes how it manifests** — but only *after* the GM narrates the cause.

**The single most common review finding in this system:** a 6- that was resolved
as a soft consequence (or no consequence). On a 6-, absent move-specified text,
the answer is a **Hard Move** — something that changes the fiction and can't be
easily undone, played out *fully* before the PC gets to react.

## The five GM Principles

Evaluate each every session. For each, the coaching question is in *italics*.

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks** — Nudge players toward actions that aren't quite
   safe but are awesome or important enough to try anyway. Best expressed when a
   player asks about the world or what they can do, or when they Examine/Ponder.
   Information you hand out should *point toward* a risky, exciting course of
   action ("It's only weak to its own beam — you'll have to provoke one and
   reflect it back"). *Am I framing info as an invitation to act dangerously, or
   letting them play it safe?*
2. **Portray a Lively World** — Vivid, dynamic, specific sensory detail; a few
   sharp details beat a generic summary. NPCs feel real: they pursue their own
   goals, react to changed circumstances, retreat when losing, set ambushes when
   hunted. Portray sweeping situations in down-to-earth ways (the plague is a
   coughing beggar and the smell of pyres, not an abstraction). *Are my NPCs
   acting on their own wants, or only reacting to the PCs?*
3. **Think Dangerously** — Nothing in play is truly safe; be willing to break or
   hurt things, sometimes permanently. PCs only die when they choose to, so don't
   pull punches *with them* — but consequences must make sense and severe harm
   must be foreshadowed (no ambushes or poison "from nowhere"). *Am I hitting hard
   enough on 6- and failed consequences, or softening them?*
4. **Leave Things Open** — Let the PCs surprise you. Keep prep mutable; when a
   player does something unplanned that fits the fiction, embrace it (even retcon
   to improve the game). Incorporate player questions, ideas, and hopes into the
   narrative. *When a player went off-script, did I build on it or steer them back?*
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over the Boring Stuff** — Treat the session like a TV show:
   cuts, timeskips, the occasional flashback. Skip week-long journeys and shopping
   ("a week passes; you arrive" / "spend the Wealth and move on") — but check with
   players before skipping, in case they had something planned. *Am I spending real
   time on scenes that aren't producing tension, discovery, or character?*

## GM Moves

GM Moves have **no fictional trigger, roll, or fixed outcome** — the GM picks one
(or more) that fits what happens. You make a Move when:

- the players look to you to see what happens,
- the players ignore a clear threat,
- the players roll a **6-** on most Moves, or
- a PC becomes exposed, vulnerable, or similar.

**Hard vs Soft:** A **Hard Move** changes the fiction in a way that can't easily
be undone (an ally seriously wounded, a treasure stolen, an enemy escaping); its
consequences play out *fully before* the PCs are asked "what do you do?" A **Soft
Move** lets the PCs intervene before the consequence fully lands, and often
telegraphs a future Hard Move. One Hard Move often leads into a Soft Move and
vice-versa.

For each Move below: what it is, and — for review — the **look-for** cue (where it
tends to be missed) and the kind of in-system alternative to suggest.

- **Hit the Ground Running** — Skip setup/downtime; drop straight into action.
  Used opening a scene, after a timeskip, or starting a chase; often paired with
  another Move. *Look for: scene openings and post-timeskip moments built up
  slowly instead of starting mid-action.*
- **Inflict Conditions** — Hurt the PCs; tell them to mark one or more conditions.
  **Always narrate the cause first**; the player then picks the stat and how it
  manifests. Match severity to the situation; forewarn severe harm. *Look for: 7-9
  Engage where both sides should trade conditions; 6- where a condition fits;
  pulled punches on physical/emotional harm.*
- **Throw Them Around** — Knock down, tie up, hurl through a window, drag
  underwater. Keep action kinetic. Forceful enemies often do this *while*
  inflicting a condition. *Look for: static "standing still" combat; grapples;
  moments an NPC could reposition a PC to change the tactical picture.*
- **Change the Environment** — Collapse the tunnel, set the building alight,
  teleport them; or shift the *social* environment (the court turns when the
  Thief's noble identity is exposed). Move them somewhere new, or alter/destroy
  where they are. *Look for: scenes parked in one static place; 6- where an
  environmental shift would create a fresh problem.*
- **Foreshadow an Ominous Force** — Show an Ominous Force's effect in the world:
  an event it's building toward (still stoppable) or aftermath the PCs stumble
  into. PCs should generally be *aware* of the Forces so they can respond. *Look
  for: quiet beats and scene transitions where a reminder of a looming threat would
  hold tension; environmental hints that a Force is advancing.*
- **Set Up an Immediate Danger** — *The most common Soft Move.* Describe something
  bad about to happen *right here, right now* but not yet landed. Can they stop it
  in time? *Look for: combats that jump to consequences without first telegraphing
  the threat; danger that should have been described before dice hit the table.*
- **Make Them Choose** — Put the PCs' desires in conflict: save the prized sword
  or the companion? Attack the lair or guard the king? Ask what they'll sacrifice.
  If they find a way to get everything, celebrate — but don't make it easy. *Look
  for: two cared-about things at risk at once where you could force a
  prioritization; 7-9 where an ugly choice beats a flat cost.*
- **Take Something Away** — Equipment, allies, opportunities — temporarily or
  permanently. Be careful with things core to a PC (a Wizard's implements);
  telegraph very significant losses via Soft Moves first. *Look for: 6- where
  losing gear/advantage reshapes the fiction; an NPC who could lift something
  during a distraction.*
- **Have It Backfire** — Twist what the PCs did. **Not** because they're inept —
  due to freak accident, unforeseeable consequence, active sabotage, or an enemy's
  frightening power (the dragon swallows the fireball and grows stronger). *Look
  for: plans that went too smoothly; successes that logically should have ripple
  effects.*
- **Sometimes, Give Them What They Want** — When there's no interesting
  consequence, or they've earned it, just say yes (maybe with strings). The go-to
  when things are safe. *Look for: over-use that drains tension by giving things
  too freely — and, conversely, correct use that kept pacing from bogging down.*
- **Introduce Something New** — A new NPC, ally, item, location, faction, or fact
  that changes the situation; harmful, helpful, or merely complicating. *Look for:
  stale or stuck scenes a new element would re-energize.*
- **Slow Down for a Bit** — Give a breather: peaceful towns, campfire laughter,
  no urgent threat. Make it clear there's no immediate danger and offer rest; maybe
  ask what's here. These treasured beats make later losses land harder. *Look for:
  relentless pacing with no downtime; post-intense moments where a pause would let
  character beats breathe.*
- **Ask Them** — Ask a player a question about the world/their character and let
  them answer it (the Druid knows how treants are born; the Thief knows the guild).
  Doesn't replace Ponder/Examine, but can deliver such a result. Use for the
  *broad* world, not the immediate "what do you see?" *Look for: things you stated
  that could have been a question; relationships/lore a PC could have defined.*

### Playbook GM Moves (this party)

More specific versions of the general Moves, tuned per playbook. Use these to
catch **missed character moments** — when the fiction brushes a PC's playbook and
nothing happened. Map by current playbook:

- **Castor — Druid:** introduce a wild spirit/creature in need; tempt their
  Instinct while in animal form, usually toward trouble; offer a chance to
  Commune with the spirits.
- **Roderic — Paladin:** endanger the helpless in his presence; test his
  commitment to his Virtues; introduce a corrupt or rival authority.
- **Paxton — Immolator:** have his fire burn something valuable (to him or
  another); offer an intimate or enchanting moment with someone important; demand
  further sacrifice.
- **Garland — Fighter / cross-class Wizard:** *(Fighter)* offer a chance to
  Demolish something important; have an NPC notice his prowess and challenge, hire,
  or manipulate him; give a chance to scare or intimidate (with later
  consequences). *(Wizard)* introduce a magical threat or mystery; offer a costly
  or dangerous place of great magical power; have an NPC fear or admire his powers.

> Playbooks evolve — confirm each PC's current playbook against `kb/pcs/` before
> leaning on this list, and treat custom/reskinned playbooks (e.g. Paxton's
> Immolator reskinned as the Light) by their underlying playbook.

## Core player Moves — triggers & outcomes

To judge whether the *right* Move triggered, check the **fictional trigger** and
the player's **actual intent**, not the label they reached for. (A player says "I
Engage" but means to slip *past* the guards — that's **Defy**, not Engage.)

- **Defy** — *act despite an imminent threat or obstacle.* Roll +STR (endure/power
  through), +DEX (speed/finesse), +INT (quick/out-of-box thinking), +WIS (mental
  fortitude/sharp senses), or +CHA (charm/intrigue/grace) — **let the fiction pick
  the stat.** 10+: the threat doesn't come to bear. **7-9: it only abates if the PC
  makes a costly sacrifice or ugly choice — the GM says what.**
- **Engage** — *openly fight an enemy up close.* Roll +STR. 7-9: you and the enemy
  trade blows, usually a condition each. 10+: trade blows but choose one — avoid/
  withstand their attack, inflict an extra condition, or take something from them.
- **Let Fly** — *attack a clear target from afar.* Roll +DEX. 10+: clean hit,
  inflict a condition. 7-9: inflict a condition but either end up in a dangerous
  position or take Disadvantage Forward (your choice).
- **Ponder** — *consider what you know about a subject.* Ask one question, say how
  you'd know, roll +INT. 10+: detailed useful truth. 7-9: vague/twisted/incomplete
  truth. Allies have Advantage first acting on it.
- **Examine** — *closely investigate a charged situation.* Ask one question, roll
  +WIS. 10+: answer + truthful follow-ups. 7-9: answer the one question truthfully.
  Allies Advantage first acting on it.
- **Scrutinize** — *study someone in an intimate/prying conversation.* Roll +WIS.
  10+: 2 Insight; 7-9: 1 Insight (+1 if they Favor you, even on 6-). Spend Insight
  1-for-1 to ask their player (incl. the GM) any question about thoughts/feelings/
  motives — answered truthfully.
- **Compel** — *use favor, payment, promises, or threats to get someone to act.*
  Roll +CHA. NPC 10+: they do it; 7-9: only if you do something in return / Favor
  them after. (PC-target rules differ — see canonical.)
- **Cooperate** — assist a companion's act to grant Advantage, but **both** of you
  eat any cost/consequence. Multiple helpers stack Advantage. Declared before the
  roll, not after.
- **Push Yourself** — put everything into one act: roll with Advantage, then take a
  condition from the strain that **ignores Armor**.
- **Settle In** — rest as a group: GM **advances the Ominous Forces**, anyone with
  5+ XP Levels Up, then conditions clear and Armor refreshes. Only when they
  *intentionally* describe taking a break, not every in-fiction night.
- **Crumble / life untenable** — marking the last non-Armor condition (or narrative
  reasons: willing sacrifice, broken vows) puts a PC out of action; they choose to
  Lock a condition, change Playbook, or Die, then the GM advances the Forces and
  says when they're back.
- **End of Session** — as a group: did we discover something new and important;
  newly visit or significantly alter a memorable location; overcome a notable
  obstacle/enemy/challenge? +1 XP each "yes," +1 if a PC accomplished their Drive.

## Conditions & consequences — the system's texture

- **Narrate the cause first; the player chooses stat + manifestation.** A condition
  handed out as raw bookkeeping ("take a condition") skips the cooperative beat.
- Vary consequence *types* beyond conditions: lost/broken equipment, a changed or
  destroyed environment, lost Favor, a new danger, time passing (Forces advance),
  collateral damage, an ally endangered, an opportunity gone.
- **7-9 is where this system lives.** Reach for **interesting costs and genuine
  dilemmas**, not a default condition. On a **7-9 Defy specifically**, lead with an
  **ugly choice or hard bargain**; treat "worse outcome" as the backup. **Both
  branches of the choice must create concrete new fiction** — if one branch is
  shrugged off, it was too soft.
- When a player **declines** the ugly choice on a 7-9 Defy, that means **the threat
  does not abate** — escalate, don't let it settle into a comfortable stalemate.
- Consequences should be **proportional and logical** within the fiction, and
  severe harm **telegraphed** beforehand (Think Dangerously, not arbitrarily).

## Fictional positioning

- Respect what's physically/narratively possible: a normal sword can't wound a
  dragon; executing a tied-up captive doesn't call for Let Fly; smashing a door
  with no time pressure needs no roll.
- When a risky action fits **no** Move, either demand a **Defy** against the
  associated danger or **make a GM Move** — don't invent a bespoke roll.
- Let the fiction dictate the **Defy stat**; don't default to the same one.
- Confirm the player's **fictional intent** before locking in a Move.

---

## Spotlight

- "Having the spotlight" is being the focus of the current narrative moment. The
  GM guides it and **switches it generously** — especially at dramatic beats (right
  after something awesome, or after a PC lands in trouble).
- Be generous with the spotlight; when one player dominates, actively pass it to a
  quieter one.
- The objective talk-time numbers (`scripts/spotlight_breakdown.py`) are a *floor*,
  not the spotlight itself — the GM always tops mic time. What matters is whether
  each PC got *narrative focus and meaningful decisions*, and whether the spotlight
  moved at the right moments.

## What this system asks of the GM (keep feedback here)

Review feedback should target **Chasing Adventure / PbtA craft**, not general
roleplaying or table logistics. In scope:

- Making (and choosing hard vs soft) GM Moves at the right trigger points.
- Honoring the 6- → Hard Move rule and the 7-9 ugly-choice texture.
- Triggering the right player Move for the fiction and intent; fictional positioning.
- Inflicting conditions cooperatively; varied, proportional, telegraphed consequences.
- Living the five Principles; managing spotlight; engaging playbook hooks.
- Advancing Ominous Forces on Settle In / 6- and foreshadowing them.

Out of scope (note briefly if it bears on the above, but don't coach it as a
system failing): interpersonal table dynamics, scheduling, whether to split the
party, generic "describe more" advice untethered from a Principle or Move, or
player behavior. *If a critique would apply equally to D&D or any narrative game,
it probably isn't the system-specific note this review is for — sharpen it to the
CA Move/Principle in play, or drop it.*
