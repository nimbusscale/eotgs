# Session 3 — GM Review

_Reviewed 2026-06-28. Chasing Adventure GM coaching. Transcript: session-3.txt._

> **Context:** This is the table's **first Chasing Adventure session** (migrating from
> Grimwild/Dungeon World/Fate), so it doubles as a live rules tutorial. Per the review
> brief, rules-teaching time is **measured, not moralized**, and feedback is held to
> CA/PbtA craft specifically. It's also the **baseline** review — there's no prior
> session to diff against. Present PCs: Garland (Jay, Fighter+cross-class Wizard),
> Edric (Ken, Bard), Castor (Dustin, Druid). Roderic (Ramsey) was absent.

---

## 1. Summary

**Top 3 strengths (system-specific)**
1. **Collaborative "Ask Them" worldbuilding.** The herald-impact prompt (lines 207-228)
   handed authorship to the players and produced fiction the GM didn't write — the man
   who's forgotten his family, the house quietly falling apart, nobody believing the
   victims. Textbook *Ask Them* + *Leave Things Open* + sweeping-force-shown-down-to-earth.
2. **Portray a Lively World.** Aurelion's faded gold, Dorn's twice-mended cloak (4:21),
   the rock-rat society with its own wants (Tufa's litters, her disdain for naming her
   young, 1802), and the *Introduce Something New* of the demoted guards all read as a
   world pursuing its own business, not just reacting to the PCs.
3. **Leave Things Open / play to find out.** The entire mine route was unprepared and
   embraced ("I didn't prepare... I'm planning to find out what's happened," 2362).
   Player-invented vantages (Edric's Dorn flashback, 762-799) were folded into canon, and
   an improvised rock-rat encounter became a lasting campaign thread.

**Top 3 growth areas (ranked by impact)**
1. **Think Dangerously — no Hard Move ever landed.** Consequences stayed uniformly soft;
   Armor and Battle Momentum absorbed nearly every hit, so danger never changed the
   fiction in a way the party couldn't undo. The 6- and the established threats were
   available and weren't cashed in hard (see §6).
2. **State the threat *before* the roll.** The GM twice rolled first and explained the
   stakes after (Tufa, 1658: _"I probably should have told you this... everybody should
   make the threat clear"_). When he *did* front-load it (the door dive, 2425-2442), the
   decision got crisp and tense. Make it procedure.
3. **Spotlight distribution in long solo scenes.** The ~37-minute Castor mine/Tufa
   sequence (3:18-3:55) left Edric and Garland as spectators ("I'm going down the
   hallway, I don't need to see a beaver rock love fest," 1715). Cut away at the
   off-screen beat to give the quiet players a decision.

**Quick wins for next session**
- Before any **Defy**, say the danger and what failure costs out loud, then roll.
- On the next clean **6-**, make a *Hard* Move (something that can't be walked back).
- Address PCs by **character name** ("Castor," "Garland"), not "Dustin/Jay/Ken" — the
  teaching context explains the slip, but the principle is worth re-forming early.

**Adventure hooks opened**
- Castor's possible offspring with **Tufa** the rock rat, and whether his druid-curse is
  heritable (flagged explicitly at End of Session, 2829-2835).
- The undead deeper in the tunnels — _"people that have been dead for a long time, but
  they still walk"_ (1744).
- The party's tunnel infiltration is now **blown** — a guard knocked out, the door breach
  heard; heat rising in Aurelion.
- The **lost Imperium gold-mining magic** bound up in the substrate rock (182) — a
  magical mystery dangling for Garland-the-Wizard.
- Aurelion's **crackdown on bards/performers** (Jimmy arrested, his guitar smashed, 4:23;
  Severin "despises storytellers") — pressure aimed straight at Edric.

**Notable moments (why, in CA terms)**
- **The herald-impact ask (207-228).** *Ask Them* feeding *Leave Things Open*: the world's
  decay got concrete and player-owned in three sentences.
- **The Tufa 7-9 (1641-1855).** A model 7-9 Defy: a genuine *ugly choice* (lie with the
  rock rat for the intel, or she screams and brings the colony), both branches creating
  real fiction, with a permanent consequence (broken celibacy vow) that became canon.
- **Dorn demoting the slacking guards (2336-2342).** *Portray a Lively World*: an NPC
  acting on his own goal (securing the wall against a perceived threat), which doubled as
  a soft *Set Up a Future Danger* — the easy guards just became hard ones.

## 4. The five GM Principles

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks — Mixed.** The mine route was framed as a real gamble ("not
   without risk, who knows what's down in the mines anymore," 542) and the herald-ask
   invited investment. But because threats often weren't named before the dice (see §6),
   several risks weren't *informed* risks. _Fix: name the danger pre-roll so players choose
   to leap knowing the drop._
2. **Portray a Lively World — Strong.** Cited above (Dorn, the guards, Tufa's rat society,
   the faded city). NPCs wanted things of their own.
3. **Think Dangerously — Weakest.** No Hard Move landed all session; Armor/momentum soaked
   the hits. _Fix in §6._
4. **Leave Things Open — Strong.** Unprepared mine embraced, player vantages canonized,
   Tufa improvised into a lasting thread.
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over the Boring Stuff — Good on "Ask."** Lots of "how would you know?"
   (720), "what's your armor look like?" (1962), "paint the scene" (1904), and the travel to
   Aurelion was glossed cleanly. The "Gloss" half occasionally lost ground to rules tangents
   rather than to fiction.

## 5. GM Moves — usage, variety, and missed opportunities

**Used well**
- **Ask Them** (207, 762, 1027) — the most effective Move of the night; turned exposition
  into player-owned fiction and relationship history.
- **Introduce Something New / Foreshadow** — Tufa, the walking dead deeper in (1744), the
  smashed-guitar crone, Dorn's return all seeded threads.
- **Set Up an Immediate Danger** (the rats dropping from the ceiling, 1914-1919) — the
  system's most common soft Move, used correctly to open the fight.

**Missed / under-used**
- **Hard Move on the failed Examine.** Castor's 6- on Examine in the swarm (2060) drew a
  single throat-bite condition — fine as far as it goes, but the swarm was the moment to go
  *hard*: _"a rat clamps onto Castor and drags him into a burrow — he's gone from the light;
  what do the rest of you do?"_ (*Throw Them Around* + *Change the Environment*), or
  _"the racket reaches the guards early — you hear boots,"_ collapsing the dungeon's two
  halves into one problem.
- **Make Them Choose** never fired, despite ripe setups. When Castor was lured toward the
  beaver-musk burrow while the party wanted to *push on past* the rats, that's two desires
  in tension — you could have forced it: _"the smell's that way, the exit's the other; the
  longer you linger the more rats wake — which is it?"_
- **Take Something Away / Have It Backfire** went untouched. Garland's *ongoing* immunity
  buff (921) and the party's torches were both leverage you could have stripped on a miss to
  raise the stakes (a torch knocked into a pit = instant *Change the Environment*).
- **Cash in the Tufa threat.** You established that a scream brings the whole colony (1660),
  then never used it — even after the noisy door-slam (2457) and the running fight. A
  **golden opportunity** to *Foreshadow an Ominous Force* (the colony stirring) or to make
  the back-half chase genuinely dangerous went by.

## 6. 6- and 7-9 handling (the heart of PbtA)

- **Castor — Defy +CHA (disadvantage), vs Tufa (1641).** Intent: charm intel out of the rock
  rat. **7-9, handled well.** Led with a real *ugly choice* (lie with her or she screams for
  the colony), both branches concrete, lasting consequence (broke his celibacy vow, seeded
  the offspring arc). The one flaw the GM named himself: the **threat wasn't stated before
  the roll** (1658), so the choice surprised rather than informed. _Verdict: handled well,
  telegraph late._
- **Castor — Examine +WIS (1) → 6- (2060).** Intent: learn how to drive the rats back. GM
  correctly knew 6- = GM move ("doesn't matter, I get to make a move," 2065) and inflicted a
  throat-bite condition (lost voice) — narrated cause-first, player chose the manifestation
  (2076). But it's a **soft** outcome where the swarm invited a Hard Move. _Verdict: too
  soft for a 6- in a swarm._ (Alternative in §5.)
- **Edric — Let Fly +DEX (2096).** Intent: shoot the rat behind him. **7-9.** GM offered the
  correct menu (dangerous position *or* Disadvantage Forward), Edric chose, and the
  "dangerous position" was rendered as his leg stuck in a hole — a fine bit of fiction.
  Minor rules wobble: brief confusion over whether *Forward* lasts the scene (2107-2113);
  Forward = next roll only, Ongoing = scene. _Verdict: handled well._
- **Edric — Defy +DEX (disadvantage) to dive through the door (2418).** **6- / fail, handled
  well.** This time the stakes were spelled out cleanly *first* (2439-2442: don't make it
  through *and* they know) and the consequence — slammed into the door, alarm raised, bounced
  back down the hall — was a real, fiction-changing result. The model for how to frame the
  others. _Verdict: handled well — and the template for fixing #2 above._
- **Garland — Engage +STR, rats (2011) and the guard (2731).** **10+/strong partials.** Clean
  use of the Engage menu (avoid harm / extra condition / take something), and momentum
  mechanics taught correctly (one momentum per instance of harm, 2031). Consequences were
  proportional but **always absorbed by Armor**, which is the root of the "no bite" feel —
  not wrong, but it's why nothing this session felt dangerous.

## 7. Conditions, consequences & fictional positioning

- **Cause-first conditions: good.** Castor's lost voice (2076) and Garland's wounded arm
  (2177) were both narrated cause-first with the *player* choosing the manifestation — exactly
  the cooperative beat. One rote slip ("take a condition, your choice," 1937) where a quick
  cause-line would have kept it fictional.
- **Variety of consequences: thin.** Almost every consequence was a condition (mostly soaked
  by Armor). The system wants you to reach past conditions — lost/broken gear, a changed
  environment, an ally endangered, time passing/Forces advancing, an opportunity gone. The
  collapsing supports (1874, 2457) were the one environmental consequence and they were
  cosmetic; make the next collapse *separate the party* or *seal the exit*.
- **Fictional positioning & intent: a real strength.** You consistently asked "what's your
  goal?" before locking a Move (Castor's rat convo 1614-1640, Garland's chop 2723-2726) and
  let the fiction pick the Defy stat rather than defaulting — Garland choosing to *endure*
  the falling rats with +STR instead of dodging +DEX (1921-1934) is exactly right. You also
  let Castor correct the fiction (squatting Garland → reachable rat, 2673-2676) and rewarded
  it. Keep this.

## 8. Missed character moments

- **Edric (Bard — Drive: "endanger or sacrifice something for the sake of your art").** The
  crone splintering the *arrested performer's guitar* (4:23) brushed Edric's entire identity
  — his trade being criminalized in Aurelion, a fellow artist's instrument destroyed — and it
  was played for a laugh, then dropped. A directed beat would have lit him up: _"Edric — that
  splintering is a luthier's guitar, your craft, outlawed in this city. What does that stir?"_
  or a *Perform/Compel* hook off the arrested bard. He told you at close he never engaged his
  drive (2969); this was the on-ramp. Also: his being known to Dorn and the Seneschal (299)
  was established and never pressured into a scene.
- **Garland (cross-class Wizard).** His recovered-but-incomplete magic and the *lost gold-
  mining magic of the Imperium* (182) and the *walking dead* (1744) are exactly the "magical
  threat or mystery" the Wizard playbook Move points at — and none of it hooked onto Garland.
  He spent the session as a sword. Even one beat — his *Second Harvest* or his half-remembered
  spellcraft reacting to the substrate-magic or the undead — would have engaged the Wizard
  half you took the trouble to draw out at 1027.
- **Castor (Druid) — done right, keep it.** The beaver-in-heat musk luring him into the burrow
  is the Druid playbook Move ("tempt their Instinct while in animal form, usually toward
  trouble") executed perfectly, and it's what generated the session's best 7-9. This is the
  template for the other two playbooks.
- **Drives, table-wide.** All three present players ended the night saying their handed-out
  drives don't fit (Garland wants to rewrite his, 2906/2933; Castor finds "protection" too
  generic, 2940-2963; Edric never engaged, 2969). That's not a failing — it's a **setup**:
  next session, frame an early situation that pulls each PC toward a sharper, self-chosen
  drive, and name it when they hit it ("that — that's the drive"). Carry-forward.
