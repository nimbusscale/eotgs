## 1. Summary

**Top 3 strengths (system-specific)**
1. **Spotlight — the sidelined PC got a full solo scene.** The session *opens* with the
   GM self-diagnosing that Castor "was just there" at the S4 cocoon (238-240) and
   resolving to fix it. He then hands Castor his own thread: communing with renewal
   (1118-1124), the Dark Harlequin temptation that drives straight at his curse and the
   Laughing One (1188-1247), the protector vision (1271-1298), and a wolverine fight.
   Castor topped player mic time (22m). This is the S4 spotlight strength **confirmed
   again**, now under the harder test of a party split.
2. **Consequence variety beyond conditions.** Roderic's Defy +WIS against the
   Harlequin's vision came up 7-9 and the cost was *a lingering doubt about Severin and
   Marrow's true motives* that the GM explicitly refused to dissipate (2353-2354) — a
   social/psychological consequence, not a default condition. This is exactly the
   non-condition texture flagged thin in S3/S4.
3. **Portray a Lively World — still the bedrock.** The Dark Harlequin pursues his own
   goals throughout (sits with an apple he won't eat, tricks Castor into touching the
   cocoon for information, puppets a dead paladin's corpse for a joke, 2071-2079); the
   grizzled vault veteran reasons his own way to standing aside ("we don't have time to
   fight both of you, I'd rather take my chances with you," 1099); the Chryseum bedlam
   is rendered as specific ruined humanity — jugglers with religious artifacts, the
   grieving, the maimed (1973-1981).

**Top 3 growth areas (ranked by impact)**
1. **A golden opportunity left as pure ambience — the tortured paladin.** While Castor
   builds his dam, an Order of the First Dawn paladin is tortured to death within
   earshot by Harlequins hunting *him* ("I want the beaver," 1892), the screams slowing
   to silence (1897-1899). It made **zero demand** on Castor — he narrated "look what I
   built" (1905). This is the session's clearest missed Move and it sat on his Druid
   drive. Fix in §5/§8.
2. **The bypassed-Dorn beat — a Paladin pull the GM noticed and dropped.** The party
   walks past First Aureate Dorn being overwhelmed by a dozen Eclipsed soldiers
   (909-931); the GM literally says "I was just looking at your virtues to see if
   there's anyone you would [help]" (931) and then lets it pass. At End of Session he
   correctly grades that Roderic *missed his drive* by leaving Dorn "to die basically"
   (2643). Make it a beat in the moment. Fix in §5/§8.
3. **Encourage Exciting Risks was weak — the wrap-up framing drained stakes.** The
   infiltration resolved through bluffs and a stomp-fight nobody was at risk of losing;
   the one real jeopardy (Castor's third condition) came from a creature he chose to
   fight. With the session deliberately compressed, failure rarely had teeth. Fix in §4.

**Quick wins for next session**
- When the players **ignore a clear threat** (the torture next door, Dorn going down),
  make a Move *at* the PC — a *Set Up an Immediate Danger* or *Make Them Choose* — even
  with no roll on the table. That is a Move trigger in its own right.
- Keep doing the non-condition 7-9 (the lingering doubt was the best beat of the night).
- Tempt the **Druid's Instinct** when Castor is in animal form and "hopped up" — that's
  a free playbook Move you walked past (see §8).

**Notable moments (why, in CA terms)**
- **Castor turns the Harlequin's taunt into consent (1242-1247).** "I'll have you know
  my life as a beaver has been studious and fulfilling… I think it's quite the noble
  creature." / "And that is the punchline." / "That's a very good joke, and that's what
  Castor goes and touches." A *Portray a Lively World* NPC and a player meeting it with
  in-character defiance — exposition turned into a character decision.
- **Roderic's Defy +WIS doubt (2268-2354).** Danger named before the roll, an
  interesting non-condition cost on the partial, and the GM held the line on it
  persisting. Model 7-9 handling.

## 4. The five GM Principles

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks — Weak.** The "prologue wrap-up" framing made the
   infiltration feel safe; the guard-bluffs and the vault stomp-fight carried little
   risk of real loss. _Fix: give the bluff or the door a cost with teeth. **Set Up an
   Immediate Danger** on Marrow's mechanism: "The lock's a slow tumbler — Marrow needs a
   count of twenty and the first Eclipsed blades are already on the ramp. Hold them off
   blind, or split your attention and risk the door jamming half-open. What do you do?"_
2. **Portray a Lively World — Strong.** The Harlequin's self-driven agenda, the veteran
   guard's own logic, the desiccated pre-Imperium chamber, the textured ruin-crowd.
3. **Think Dangerously — Mixed, trending up.** A PC condition landed and a man died
   irreversibly — both improvements. But the death made no demand on the PC who could
   hear it. Fix in §5.
4. **Leave Things Open — Strong.** The collaborative opening and Roderic's nuanced
   theology were embraced and canonized; the Follower swap was waved straight in.
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over — Good.** Strong Asks built the scene (what Roderic respected
   *and* feared about the Order, 724-745; what Garland remembered of Crest Aurelion,
   758-772). The Gloss (the Fiat to leave Castor behind, 593) was the right call for a
   deliberately compressed session.

## 5. GM Moves — usage, variety, and missed opportunities

**Used well**
- **Introduce Something New** — the Dark Harlequin at the cocoon, the heretic-branding
  inquisitor, and the corpse-puppet all re-energized stuck moments.
- **Foreshadow / Have It Backfire** — the dead paladin rising as a puppet (2071-2079)
  is a clean "the threat you walked past is now in your way" beat.

**Missed / under-used**
- **Set Up an Immediate Danger + Make Them Choose — the big one, at the paladin
  torture.** Castor hears a man tortured to death within earshot by enemies hunting
  *him*, and it never becomes his problem (1889-1905). His Druid drive is *endanger
  yourself for wildlife or outcasts* — this is the hook. Press it: _"The Harlequin's
  voice gets closer — 'The beaver came down here, check behind the branches' — and a
  hand starts pawing at the desiccated vines you just stacked over the door. Below, the
  paladin's screams have gone wet and slow; he won't last. You can hold the barricade
  and stay hidden, or break for him — not both. What do you do?"_ Or, if you want it
  purely as a threat that lands: _"You can keep damming, but every branch you pull free
  makes noise, and the next scream cuts off mid-breath — they've found something better
  to do than look for you. Do you keep your head down, or is that enough?"_ Either turns
  a passive montage into a drive-charged decision.
- **Make Them Choose — the Dorn bypass.** You spotted it ("looking at your virtues,"
  931) and let it go. Cash it: _"Roderic — that's First Aureate Dorn going down under a
  dozen Eclipsed blades, the man whose city you slipped into. Severin's father needs you
  too, and you can't reach both in time. Walk on and Dorn is finished. What does a
  Lightbearer do?"_ That engages his drive (endanger yourself to protect) *and*
  foreshadows Dorn's fall instead of leaving him as set-dressing.

## 6. 6- and 7-9 handling

_No 6- occurred this session. The partials were handled cleanly — this is the area
that improved most._

- **Castor — Engage +STR as wolverine, at Disadvantage (conditioned) → 7-9 (1395).**
  Trade blows: he claws the Harlequin and takes his **third** condition. Narrated
  cause-first (teeth sink in), player chose stat + manifestation (DEX, a "wonky
  wolverine foot," 1430-1435). _Handled well — and it's the session's one PC condition,
  putting him in real jeopardy. Exactly the texture S4 lacked._
- **Roderic — Defy +STR to bowl the order soldiers downhill → partial (1768).** He lands
  the move but a sword clashes his leg; cost absorbed by an armor point. Right Move
  (act despite an imminent threat), proportional cost. _Acceptable — slightly soft in
  that armor is a free buffer, but fine for a non-lethal stunt. If you wanted more bite:
  "You bowl them over, but the one on the flank gets a clean line — take the condition
  to your leg, or take it to your shield arm and lose the bash next round. Your call."_
- **Roderic — Defy +WIS vs the Harlequin's vision → 7-9 (2261).** Danger named first;
  cost = a persistent doubt about Severin/Marrow's honesty that the GM refused to clear
  (2353). _Handled well — model non-condition 7-9._ The later Scrutinize 13 (2412)
  earning its resolution is legitimate, since the player rolled specifically to chase it.
- **Garland — Compel +CHA (threat, Advantage) → success (1095); Engage (Advantage) → 11
  (2177); Roderic — Decree +CHA → 10 (2026); Engage (Advantage) → re-rolled high (2339).**
  All resolved correctly — effects chosen, NPCs given sensible reactions. _Handled well._

## 8. Missed character moments

- **Castor (Druid — Drive: endanger yourself for wildlife or outcasts).** The tortured
  paladin (1889-1905) is the textbook drive hook — a helpless man being killed within
  reach — and it passed without a beat. This is the single biggest miss of the session.
  Engage it with the *Set Up an Immediate Danger / Make Them Choose* in §5: make the
  screams *his* decision, not background audio. Even a one-line Ask — _"Castor, that's a
  man dying ten feet away and they're killing him to find you. Does the beaver keep
  building, or does something in you move?"_ — would have put his drive on the table.
- **Castor (Druid playbook — tempt his Instinct in animal form, toward trouble).** As a
  wolverine he's "hopped up… a growly little guy stuck in a corner" (1451-1453) — a
  ready-made moment to tempt the Instinct toward overreach: _"The wolverine in you
  doesn't want this thing to *leave* — it wants to chase it up the dark stairs and
  finish it. Do you let the animal off the leash?"_ A free playbook Move that also
  re-anchors his "what is my mitigated curse capable of" arc.
- **Roderic (Paladin — Drive: endanger yourself to protect; "endanger the helpless in
  his presence").** The Dorn bypass (909-931). You noticed the Virtue hook and dropped
  it; the End-of-Session grade (2643) shows you knew. Make it the *Make Them Choose* in
  §5 next time the fiction offers a helpless ally in his sightline.
- **Garland (Fighter/Wizard) — fine, no notable miss.** His kit drove the infiltration
  and the vault fight; this was a support session for him by design, well-integrated.
