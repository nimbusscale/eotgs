## Altreth

**Type:** Ruins
**First Visited:** [[Session 0]]

### Description
A city-state that once clung to the side of Mount Ripea.
It was a prosperous metropolis whose wealth came from rich veins of ore carved from the mountain's depths.

### The Fall of Altreth
Legend holds that when a trading caravan arrived after a particularly long winter, they found the city already dead.
Altreth had not fallen to fire or flood, but to a deeper failure: the bedrock beneath it had decayed, and the mountain itself slumped over the city like a lifeless body.

Nearly all of Altreth was buried beneath stone and rubble.
Only one structure remained untouched: the great church in the central square.
Upon its altar rested a single [[Whiteglass]] disc — the [[Seal of Unmaking]].
Those who recovered the relic reportedly aged with unnatural speed and soon died.
Their homes fell to ruin, and even their names were forgotten.
Only a faint memory of Altreth and the caravan remains.
The surviving accounts named the whiteglass disc the [[Seal of Unmaking]] after the fate of those who recovered it.
The relic vanished after the initial recovery and eventually surfaced in [[Ashbrook]].

### Connected Locations
- Mount Ripea — The mountain Altreth was built upon

### Events Here
- [[Session 0]] — Established as the legendary origin of the [[Seal of Unmaking]]

## Ashbrook (Eld Ashara)

**Type:** Ruins
**First Visited:** [[Session 0]]

### Description
Once a town high in the [[Cinderwall]], near one of the few mountain passes.
Originally a thriving settlement where [[Garland yn Greenholt]]'s relatives lived.
Now a cursed ruin known as Eld Ashara, aged centuries in moments by the spreading curse.

The spring beneath the town feeds the Upper [[Ashen Flow]], carrying the curse downstream toward the [[Ashen Vale]].

Before the fall, residents spoke of the town "building itself" and there was talk of renaming it to something grander.
The water tasted unusually sweet, though fish didn't linger in the shallows.

### Notable Features
- Location of the reemergence of the God of Ruin
- The [[Seal of Unmaking]] was recovered from here by the party, unwittingly releasing what it contained
- A spring beneath the ruins now carries the curse into the [[Ashen Flow]]
- The old stonework near the spring was reportedly changing on its own before the fall

### Connected Locations

- [[Ashen Flow]] — The river originating from the spring beneath the ruins
- [[Ashen Vale]] — The valley downstream, threatened by the curse

### Associated NPCs

- [[Garland yn Greenholt]] — Had relatives living here

## The Ashen Flow

**Type:** Wilderness (River)
**First Visited:** [[Session 0]]

### Description
A river that flows from the [[Cinderwall]] mountains through the [[Ashen Vale]].
The giant lake that feeds the river is miles away, well beyond the borders of the Ashen Vale.
While the water runs clear from the lake, it grows murky and unpleasant as it comes downstream.
Yet by the time it reaches the Ashen Vale, the water is clean — as pure as fresh rainwater.

### [[Upper Ashen Flow]]
The upper portion originates from a spring beneath [[Ashbrook]].
It is fed by several other rivers and springs from the [[Cinderwall]].
The waters are wide, dirty, and perilous.
Since the curse, this section carries the aging curse downstream.

### [[The Great Beaver Dam]]
Along the river, just on the border of the [[Ashen Vale]], stands a beaver dam.
It has existed as long as anyone remembers and is meticulously maintained, never grown rotten.
The dam blocks the Upper Ashen Flow, forming a lake.
It filters the water — below it, the Lower Ashen Flow runs clean into the vale.

This dam was [[Castor]]'s society and home during his years as a beaver.
He was enamored with beaver society and how they lived, and it was he who first settled here for the sake of the beaver Aspen and helped a modest dam grow into the great one that tamed the river.

The dam currently holds back the cursed water from [[Ashbrook]], but the dam and the beavers maintaining it are aging rapidly under the curse's influence.

Since the curse passed, the beaver community has fractured much like the [[The Light|Light]].
Led by Aspen's ambitious descendant [[Tamarack]], the beavers have begun damming the lower [[Ashen Flow]] beyond their old home at [[Beaver Lake]], cutting into orchards and farmland and becoming pests — which has soured the Vale's great families against the colony and brought armed men demanding the beavers be broken.

### [[Lower Ashen Flow]]
The clean, calm portion of the river that runs through the [[Ashen Vale]].
Filtered by the great beaver dam upstream.
Feeds the fertile farmland of the vale.

### Connected Locations
- [[Ashbrook]] — Spring beneath the ruins feeds the upper flow
- [[Cinderwall]] — Source of tributaries
- [[Ashen Vale]] — The lower flow runs through the vale
- [[Crownvale]] — At risk if the dam breaks

### Associated NPCs
- [[Castor]] — The beaver dam was his home and community

### Events Here
- [[Session 0]] — The cursed water from Ashbrook is being held back by the beaver dam; the dam and beavers are aging rapidly

## Ashen Vale

**Type:** Region
**First Visited:** [[Session 0]]

### Description
A fertile valley enriched by volcanic ash from the [[Cinderwall]].
A border region of the Kingdom of [[Beaconhold]].
Home to [[Garland yn Greenholt]] and [[Castor]].
Once an agrarian region during the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]] era.
Fiefdoms were granted to war veterans like [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] after [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]]'s conquest, though largely left to self-govern.

### Notable Features
- Fed by the [[Ashen Flow|Lower Ashen Flow]], which runs clean after being filtered by the great beaver dam upstream
- Fertile farmland enriched by volcanic ash

### [[Greenholt]]

[[Garland yn Greenholt]]'s fiefdom, granted after [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]]'s conquest and largely left to self-govern.
Garland serves as its accidental lord, with his grandson [[Aldric Garlandsson]] now ruling from [[Crownvale]].

#### Witch Stones

The [[Witch Stones]] — a hidden ring of rune-carved standing stones also known as the Shepherd's Teeth — stand somewhere on Garland's land.
They are the source of Garland's magic and, as recently revealed, can open a portal to a matching ring of stones on the [[Bonewall]].
Garland speaks of the Ashen Vale openly, but guards the secret of their location and does not volunteer information about the stones themselves.

### The Families
The Vale is held by a handful of powerful landowning families who act as its unofficial fiefdom under [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]], the most powerful among them.
All of them are [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]]'s blood, generations down; to them he is half myth, and only Aldric's line kept the direct knowledge — so Garland faces a crowd of his own descendants who do not really know him.
Three family heads led the delegation to the [[Witch Stones]], all aggrieved by the spreading beavers:

- **Sela Thornby** — orchardist matriarch and de facto head of the prominent families, with decades of her trees drowning in beaver-flooded ground. She leads the delegation, is formidable, and is reachable by reason — under the anger is grief, not cruelty; the one Garland and [[Castor]] can actually talk down.
- **Doral Beck** — the miller, whose wheel depends on the Lower [[Ashen Flow]] the beavers keep diverting. Not cruel, just frightened for his living — the neutral, tragic face who didn't want to come but had no choice.
- **Wat Hollis** — younger and hungry, the one setting traps along the Ashen Flow. He smells bounty and pelts in the chaos and pushes hardest to see Castor handed over and the colony broken — the escalator who can turn a tense parley ugly.

### Connected Locations
- [[Beaconhold]] — The kingdom to the north that the vale borders
- [[Crownvale]] — Seat of power within the vale
- [[Cinderwall]] — The mountain range to the south, source of the vale's fertile ash
- [[Ashbrook]] — Former town in the [[Cinderwall]] above the vale, now cursed ruins ([[Eld Ashara]])
- [[Ashen Flow]] — The river that flows through the vale
- Greenholt — [[Garland yn Greenholt]]'s fiefdom, home to the [[Witch Stones]]

### Associated NPCs
- [[Garland yn Greenholt]] — Long-time resident, accidental lord of Greenholt
- [[Castor]] — Resident, connected to the beaver dam on the [[Ashen Flow]]
- [[Aldric Garlandsson]] — Rules [[Crownvale]]

### Events Here
- [[Session 0]] — Established as homeland of Garland and Castor; the curse threatens to reach the vale if the beaver dam fails
- [[Session 6]] — At the hidden Witch Stones, [[Captain Vask]]'s delegation and the three great families came to retrieve [[Mira]] and end the beaver troubles; Mira opened a portal to the [[Bonewall]] and stepped through, with Garland and Castor following — and Vask revealed that Aldric somehow knows everything that happens in the Vale

## Aurelion

**Type:** City
**First Mentioned:** Pre-session notes

### Description
The capital city of [[Marrow County]], once a crown jewel of the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae]] famed for its gold production.
The Imperium possessed magical or technological means to extract gold from substrate rock — knowledge lost since the Imperium's fall.
When the gold ran out, Aurelion declined into relative unimportance, leaving it vulnerable to [[Beaconhold]]'s expansion.
[[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] fought in the war that brought Aurelion under Beaconhold's control.
Seat of [[Count Albrecht Marrow]]'s power and center of the county's administration.
[[The Nodrum]] is located not far from the city.
The surrounding countryside shows decline: thin fields, sparse orchards, and a once-grand imperial road now lightly traveled.
The city employs dog catchers to deal with a stray dog problem.
The party is wanted in Aurelion — posters accuse them of subverting the county and stealing goods on behalf of [[Count Albrecht Marrow]].

### [[Crest Aurelion]]
[[Count Albrecht Marrow]]'s castle and seat of power for [[Marrow County]], perched atop the city hill like a vault rather than a castle.
Contains a massive vault with diminishing gold reserves and a secret passage connecting it to the count's private chambers, discovered by [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] during [[Beaconhold]]'s conquest centuries ago.
[[Beaconhold]] conquered Crest Aurelion by forcing the capitulation of a Morrow ancestor who ruled it.

### [[The Chryseum]]
The cathedral of [[The Light]] in the city, presided over by [[Luminary Severin Morrow]].
Features a massive gold dome that dominates the skyline — the last prominent reminder of the city's golden past.
The interior features a massive [[Whiteglass]] stained-glass window radiating clean light, rows of pews, soaring vaults of white gold and candlelight, and a mysterious swirling pattern on the nave floor.
The church was built over an older, pre-Luciferian temple — a hidden spiral staircase beneath the swirling floor pattern leads down to the ancient Shrine of Renewal, activated by renewal-aligned power.
A church dungeon lies below — a sunken courtyard lined with cells on three sides, accessed via a mezzanine walkway and stairs, connecting to the tunnel network through multiple doors.
The [[Order of the First Dawn]] serves as the Chryseum's paladinic order.
The Chryseum district is accessible via the underground tunnel network.

#### [[The Shrine of Renewal]]
An ancient, pre-Luciferian chamber hidden beneath the Chryseum, accessible via a concealed spiral staircase activated by renewal-aligned power.
The space is organic rather than constructed — shaped by forces older than the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]], older than everything the party has encountered save [[The Nodrum]] and the [[Witch Stones]].
The chamber predates the Chryseum, the Imperium, and the Luciferian era.
The vines and roots are petrified and desiccated but show faint signs of recovery when a herald is present — small cracks appear and then heal.
At its center rests a translucent cocoon containing the faintest suggestion of a sleeping form — the dormant God of Renewal.
The cocoon radiates faint warmth and contains what appears to be a figure in a fetal position within a translucent membrane.
The cocoon inspires an instinctive, parental urge to protect it in all who behold it.
Those with herald connections feel an interlocking resonance in this place.
Residual renewal energy within the shrine can partially counteract the effects of ruin.
When all six heralds gathered, the cocoon opened and the chamber transformed from desiccated ruin to a verdant, living environment filled with renewal energy.

- **The Cocoon** — A translucent cocoon at the chamber's center containing the sleeping God of Renewal; the form within is never clearly resolved, just a curve and shadow suggesting dormant but living presence
- **Hidden Entrance** — Concealed beneath the swirling pattern on the Chryseum's nave floor; activated by placing hands on the floor with renewal-aligned power, producing a greenish glow

## The Bonewall

**Type:** Region (Mountain Range)
**First Visited:** [[Session 0]]

### Description
The eastern arm of the L-shaped mountain border, contrasting with the wet and volcanic [[Cinderwall]] to the south.
Its climate is dry and cold, windswept, with far less snow than the Cinderwall despite the chill.
It feeds the [[Eastern Rivers]] through deep springs and aquifers.
What sets it apart is the stone itself, and the fact that it was not always this empty.

**Sight.**
The Bonewall is built of pale shale, bone-white where it is freshly broken and weathering down through ashen greys to near-black in its oldest, most sheltered hollows.
It is brittle stone that splits and flakes into loose plates and shards, and it erodes into shapes the eye reads as skeletal: ridges running like exposed ribs along the spurs, knuckled outcrops, and smooth hollows worn into the cliffs like empty sockets.
The land behind the first slopes is deep and broken, a country of ravines, blind valleys, and stacked outcroppings where sightlines are short and travel turns maze-like.
The only stubborn growth is a hardy woody vine, grey-brown and near-leafless, sprawling in thick knotted tangles across the shale and choking the mouths of ravines, like old grapevine swollen to a wilder scale.

**Sound.**
Everything underfoot crunches and clatters, loose shale plates sliding and breaking with each step, so no one crosses the Bonewall quietly.
Above that runs the wind, which never fully stops, moaning through the ravines and the hollow shells of ruined structures.
Beneath both is a deep silence: no birdsong, no insect drone, none of the ordinary noise of a living landscape.

**Smell.**
The defining note is dust — dry, chalky, mineral, the smell of powdered stone and old plaster, with a cold flinty edge where fresh rock is split.
It settles on the tongue and dries the throat, and the wind keeps it moving.
Just as distinctive is what is absent: no loam, no rot, none of the green or wet smell of living things.
Travelers often say the Bonewall smells like a sealed tomb.

**A wall that was lived on.**
The Bonewall was never true wilderness.
Under the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]] it was settled and sustained, its harsh ground made livable by imperial engineering and magic.
There were no great cities, but there were towns, quarrying camps cut into the shale, waystations strung along the roads, and remote sites given over to magical research and to supplying the [[Thirteen Spines]].
When the Imperium fell, the support that held all of it in place fell with it, and the wall emptied with startling speed.
What remains is a ghost country: abandoned settlements, worked-out quarries, and the derelict shells of imperial works, standing dry and dust-choked where the people simply walked away.

**The roads.**
A few roads are still kept open — the supply lines [[Beaconhold]] and the [[Spinewardens]] rely on to provision the Spines that remain manned — and they are the exception.
Most of the old imperial roads are abandoned and failing, their pale paving plates heaved and scattered, half-swallowed by shale and vine.
Along them sit the bones of the old order: toppled mile-markers, the foundations of dead waystations, and the empty husks of border checkpoints.
The ancient military road still runs precariously along the base of the wall toward [[The Reaches]].

Travelers out on the dead shale occasionally come upon a strange, finely formed statue — a figure with no record of who carved it or why, weathered into the rock as if it had always been there.
No one can say who they are meant to be, and they remain one of the wall's unexplained curiosities.
Those who cross the wall call them the Watchers.

### [[The Thirteen Spines]]
Thirteen towers built along the Bonewall during the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]] era.
They rise from the mountain slopes like spines along a ridge — tall enough to peer over the crest of the Bonewall, but built on the slopes facing [[Beaconhold]] rather than at the very summit.
The towers serve as both physical fortifications and magical wards, maintained by [[The Light]] to hold back forces of darkness on the far side of the mountains.

Each Spine has its own local nickname based on its appearance, history, or reputation — names given by the soldiers and scholars who have garrisoned them over the centuries.
The formal [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]]-era designation for the tower network may exist in old records but has fallen out of common use; everyone simply calls them the Spines.
The towers are garrisoned by the [[Spinewardens]], a military order of [[Beaconhold]] sworn to maintain the wards.

## The Reaches

**Type:** Region (Marsh Delta)
**First Visited:** [[Session 6]] — site of the barge encounter; the town of [[Havens Reach]] sits within it

### Description
The marshy delta at the kingdom's northeastern edge, where the [[Eastern Rivers]] drain off the [[Bonewall]] and spread into a vast, low country of water and reed before emptying into the [[Solvium]].
It separates the port of [[Havens Reach]] from the rest of [[Beaconhold]] and runs inland until it meets the dead grey shale of the Bonewall, where the wet, living marsh stops in a single abrupt line.

If the Bonewall is the kingdom's dead and frozen edge, the Reaches is its opposite: low, wet, and teeming, a place that never stopped being alive.
It is also a place that keeps what falls into it.
The bog preserves and ferments rather than clears away, and a thousand years of settlement, runoff, and old power have steeped in it.

**Sight.**
A flat, open country under a wide sky.
Slow channels of brown water wind between banks of tall reed and marsh grass, past hummocks of peat and tussock and islands barely above the waterline.
Mist sits on the water at dawn and dusk.
Pale columns rise from one flooded stretch, the remains of a drowned temple far older than the Imperium, standing half-sunk where the ground gave way.
Everywhere are the bones of old habitation: overgrown villages, abandoned camps, foundations and streets half-claimed by water and weed.
Cutting through all of it in a single deliberate line runs the raised stone road, carried over the channels on old bridges, the one straight and certain thing in a landscape with no edges.
At the far inland horizon the Bonewall stands grey and lifeless above the green.

**Sound.**
Water is constant: lapping, trickling, the suck and gurgle of mud underfoot and under hull.
Over it runs the hiss of wind through reeds and a dense chorus of living things, frogs, insects, and waterbirds in numbers the rest of the kingdom has forgotten.
After the silence of the Bonewall it is almost overwhelming.
Threaded through the chorus are sounds that do not belong to it: something heavy shifting in the reeds out of sight, a call with no name, a stillness that falls where it should not.

**Smell.**
Heavy, wet, and full.
Rich rotting vegetation and stagnant water, the iron tang of bog, the sweetish preserved smell of peat, and the sulfur whiff of marsh gas where mud is disturbed.
Where camps burn peat for fuel the smoke hangs low and clings.
It is the smell of a place fermenting, the exact opposite of the Bonewall's dry dust, and travelers who have known both never confuse them.

### The Road and the Bridges
Almost all travel out of [[Havens Reach]] is by boat, across the [[Solvium]] or along the one navigable shipping channel through the marsh.
By land there is a single road, the artery running between [[Beaconhold]] and the port.
It is the only way goods move overland between the kingdom and its northeastern harbor, which makes it one of the most important and most vulnerable stretches of road in all of Beaconhold.
A few old stone bridges, Imperium-built and among the few things in the Reaches still deliberately maintained, carry the clear road over the channels on the final approach to the town.
Cut the bridges or lose the road, and Havens Reach becomes an island.

### The Drowned Temple
Among the ruins stands a pillared temple to the water gods, older than the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]] and built in a style that is not the Imperium's own.
Lucifer's Imperium raised its own architecture wherever it spread, but here, much as at [[The Nodrum|the Nodrum]], it found something already standing and claimed it rather than building anew.
What remains is a mixing of two ages of stone, the older bones of the temple overlaid with later Imperium work, now half-drowned where the delta swallowed its ground.
The powers it honored were not gentle ones: gods of water that drown and keep, the deep that takes things down and does not give them back.
It is the oldest visible thing in the Reaches, and the one the local boatfolk give the widest berth.

### Creatures and the Practitioners Who Court Them
The Reaches is known, to the few who know it at all, as a place that breeds strange things.
The steeping of runoff, old magic, and whatever the bog has held for centuries seems to change what lives in it, and the marsh has a reputation for predators and stranger creatures found nowhere else.
That reputation draws its own kind of people: hedge-wizards, naturalists, and less reputable scholars who come to study, harvest, or court what the marsh produces, living in isolated huts and towers out among the channels.
Where their study ends and their making begins is not always clear, and some of the marsh's stranger inhabitants may owe their existence to it.

### The Layered Dead
Unlike the Bonewall, which froze and emptied cleanly, the Reaches has been lived in over and over.
For a thousand years, settlements, camps, and fishing villages have sprouted on its richness and then been drowned, abandoned, or swallowed back, each leaving its layer.
People keep coming to live off the marsh, and the marsh keeps taking it back.
The result is a country thick with half-sunk ruins in every state of decay, from Imperium foundations to villages lost within living memory.

### The Source of Garland's Magic
The Witch Stones are the source of Garland's magical power.

His great-granddaughter [[Mira]] also hears the stones, but as actual words rather than the grinding and moaning of the earth — a more direct connection that is turning her into something like a cleric of the stones.

### The Portal to the Bonewall
At the hidden ring in the Ashen Vale, Mira commanded the stones to tear open a stable portal onto the dead grey shale of the [[Bonewall]], where a matching ring of Witch Stones stood.
She and her pursuers stepped through, and the portal closed behind them.
This is the only such portal seen so far; whether the stones connect to still more rings, or form a wider network, is unknown.
The [[Shrine of Renewal]] beneath the Chryseum in [[Aurelion]] evoked the same ancient resonance Garland felt at the Witch Stones, hinting at some connection between these ancient sites.
Garland would be very interested in investigating other Witch Stones if they exist elsewhere.
