## Session 3: Beneath the Golden City

**Date Played:** 2026-02-21

### Recap-Teaser
> Deep in the Nodrum, the Triune delivered a truth no one wanted to hear.
> The corruption is not in the river.
> It is not in the soil.
> It is in you.
> You are the heralds of ruin, and everywhere you ride, the world rots a little faster.
> But the sisters offered more than despair.
> They spoke of a counterpart: a God of Renewal, long absent, whose return might restore the balance shattered when the Seal of Unmaking was broken.
> And they pointed the way.
> A forgotten shrine, buried beneath the holiest cathedral in Marrow County — the Chryseum.
>
> Then came the laughter.
> A Harlequin named Jimmy confessed that the Laughing One has been watching since Ashbrook.
> The scraps of Aldric's livery, the paranoia, the feeling of being followed.
> All of it was entertainment.
> And the Dark Harlequin, that thing with the checkered face and the mouth full of teeth, sent Garland a vision of children daring each other to leap from the parapets of Aldric's castle.
> It wanted him to turn back.
> But old Garland pressed forward, even as something precious began to slip from his memory like water through cupped hands.
>
> Now the road leads to Aurelion.
> The seat of Count Marrow's power, where Luminary Severin preaches that storytellers are the enemy and the Aureate enforce his word in tarnished golden armor.
> Somewhere beneath that cathedral lies a shrine to a god the church buried and forgot.
> Four plague-carriers, walking into the most heavily guarded city in the county, seeking a forbidden temple beneath the feet of a priest who would see them silenced.
> What could possibly go wrong?

### Summary
The road to [[Aurelion]] offered the party their first true glimpse of what they carry.
A farmer stood blank-eyed on a dusty path, unable to remember his own wife and child while his farmhouse sagged and splintered behind him.
The neighbors could not make anyone believe the truth of what had happened.
The party rode past in grim silence, recognizing themselves as the cause — [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] the herald of forgetting, [[Castor]] of crumbling foundations, [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] of souring tales, and [[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]], traveling separately, of failing wards.

[[Aurelion]] rose before them: the golden dome of the Chryseum still catching light above a city long past its golden age, [[Crest Aurelion]] perched on the hill like a vault rather than a castle, and a countryside of thin fields and abandoned mines.
Wanted posters bearing all four of their likenesses lined the road, accusing them of subversion and theft on behalf of [[Count Albrecht Marrow]].
[[Castor]] shifted into a dog to hide his face, but [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] proposed a different approach entirely — the old mines beneath the city, whose tunnels [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] had learned about during a drunken evening with [[Captain Eisen Dorn]].

They entered the mines and descended into darkness, [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] conjuring light and wards while [[Castor]] shifted into beaver form to scout.
At a fork in the tunnels, [[Castor]] caught the unmistakable scent of a beaver in heat — impossibly out of place underground — and followed it to a burrow where he struck a bargain with a rock rat named [[Tufa]], trading his long-held vow of celibacy for intelligence about the tunnels ahead.
[[Tufa]] warned of undead deeper in the passages and of rock rats that ambush anyone carrying light.
Undeterred, the party pressed on with torches lit, ready to face whatever came — and face it they did: rats dropped from the ceiling, wounding [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] through his ancient armor and biting deep into [[Castor]]'s throat, stealing his voice to a ragged whisper.

Pressing on, the party discovered a new wooden door guarded by Aureate soldiers — and then watched from the shadows as [[Captain Eisen Dorn|First Aureate Eisen Dorn]] himself arrived to replace the idle guards with his best soldiers, warning of a threat to the city.
[[Edric Bloom|Edric]], invisible under [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]]'s spell, failed to slip through behind Dorn and alerted the new guards.
[[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] conjured an illusion of glowing rock rats to split the sentries, allowing [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] to finally dart through.
When a rock rat attacked from behind, [[Castor]] grappled it off [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]], who then stepped from the shadows, conjured a halo of light behind his head, and knocked an elite Aureate guard unconscious with a single blow from [[Second Harvest]] — his ancient sword that ignores armor.
All three stumbled through the door to rejoin [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] on the far side, battered but alive, one step closer to the shrine of Renewal beneath the Chryseum.

## Session 2: Heralds of Ruin

**Date Played:** 2026-02-07

### Recap-Teaser
> Ashbrook lies in ruin. Centuries of decay unleashed in moments upon a town that was alive just days ago. You rode hard through the Cinderwall, bargained with shapeshifters under a hunter's moon, and raised a desperate ward of spirit and song across the waters of Beaver Lake. But barriers crack. And already the signs are spreading: a merchant's stock rotting on the cart, a child whispering sweetly of endings, a soldier's discipline fraying like old rope. Something is seeping into the bones of the land, and it is not content to follow the river.
>
> Now the road to Beaconhold stretches too long. The spirit wall won't hold forever, and the cursed waters pool higher with every passing hour. So you ride for the Triune, three halfling sisters and hedge-wizards of strange geometric arts, who Garland once consulted about Castor's curse. They work out of a place called the Nodrum, an ancient facility built at the nexus of several ley lines, deep in the heart of Marrow County. And therein lies the danger. Count Marrow's soldiers, the Aureate, patrol these roads, and the Assayer Sovereign, Mayliss Vane, is expecting a delivery you have no intention of making. Worse still, whispers travel fast in this county. Whispers of creeping ruin: crops failing, fish rotting, bridges groaning under their own weight. And whispers of a different kind. Luminary Severin Morrow, the Count's own son and head priest of the Chryseum, has turned his pulpit against bards, storytellers, and anyone who trades in the currency of words. Those who have the ear of the people are being silenced. Wanted signs are nailed to tavern doors. For a party traveling with a bard, these roads just got a good deal more dangerous.
>
> And old Garland rides with an uneasy knot in his gut. The Triune helped him, yes. But their questions about Castor were always a little too eager, their curiosity a little too sharp. He kept the druid's identity secret for a reason. And now he's about to walk Castor through their front door.

### Summary
The party detoured from the long road to [[Beacon Hold]], riding instead to [[The Nodrum]] — an ancient stone facility built into the side of a mountain at the nexus of several ley lines, deep in [[Marrow County]].
Along the way they witnessed signs of ruin spreading far beyond the [[Ashen Vale]]: rotting merchant stock, failing crops, and the Aureate dragging performers from taverns under [[Luminary Severin Morrow]]'s decree against storytellers.
[[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] and [[Castor]] agreed that Castor would pose as one of Garland's many grandsons, keeping the druid's true nature hidden from [[The Triune]] — three halfling sisters and hedge-wizards of geometric arts who had previously helped Garland stabilize Castor's curse.

At the Nodrum, Aurea greeted Garland warmly and the Triune attempted to examine [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] with their rotating platforms and lenses, but the machinery seized when it touched him.
Venn observed that Edric stood at the overlap of many circles — old gods, new faith, curses, and counties — a shared space that was either very safe or very dangerous.
Delta explained that ruin was never meant to exist alone among the [[Old Gods]]; it had a counterpart in Renewal, and without that balance, centuries of ruinous energy had been released all at once.
Edric wondered whether the answer lay not in resealing ruin but in restoring renewal.

Tremors shook the facility and the party pressed deeper, past malfunctioning clockwork guardians, into an antechamber library where Garland discovered the Nodrum was once called the Nexarium — a prison built to contain [[The Xan-Kor]], a legendary construct of the God of Forgetting capable of erasing things from existence by removing them backward through time.
The Triune's wards began failing; they confessed they had secretly siphoned energy from the curse Garland blocked on Castor to power what they called the Aegis Mechanism — their attempt to repurpose the Xan-Kor.
The party's arrival, carrying ruin's influence, had destabilized everything.

Garland refused to let Castor be re-cursed and instead teleported to retrieve the [[Seal of Unmaking]]'s case, losing his recent memory of his great-great-grandchild [[Mira]] in the transit.
The party helped the Triune redirect the ley line energy into the [[Whiteglass]] seal, pushing the Xan-Kor back to a faint outline — but the ritual went awry.
Ley lines passing through Edric and [[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]] emerged corrupted, reality itself shifted, the [[Bonewall]]'s ancient protective wards began to crumble, and a pivotal historical victory by Lucifer's champion was retroactively undone.

By morning, ruin had struck at the core of each party member: Roderic's door lock crumbled in his hands, Garland's books became indecipherable, Edric's dreams drained of inspiration, and Castor's presence caused the clockwork servants to break down.
The Triune delivered their research over breakfast and declared that the party themselves were the heralds of ruin — carrying the curse wherever they went.
A scrying of [[Beaver Lake]] confirmed it: the spirit wall still stood but blocked nothing, the cursed water simply gone.
The ruin traveled with the party, not through the waterway.

The Triune directed them toward a shrine to the God of Renewal beneath the Chryseum in [[Aurelion]], where they might find clues to locating the heralds of renewal and restoring balance.
The party departed the Nodrum, and Garland discovered another piece of [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]]'s green-and-gold livery deliberately planted on a branch.
Castor tracked acrobat footprints to a nearby village, where the Aureate were beating a performer in an orange scarf strikingly similar to Edric's yellow one.

Roderic invoked his authority as a knight of [[Lucifer]] and forced the soldiers to release the man, who introduced himself as Jimmy — a Harlequin, a follower of the Laughing One.
Jimmy revealed that the Harlequins had been watching the party since [[Ashbrook]], delighting in their unwitting role as heralds of ruin, and confessed that the Aldric livery was a low-effort trick to exploit Garland's paranoia.
The Dark Harlequin — their monstrous leader — sent Garland an uninvited vision of Mira and other children in the tower of Aldric's castle, daring each other to leap from the parapets.
Edric glimpsed the Dark Harlequin itself inside a barrel: a figure with checkered face paint, a fool's cap, and a mouth full of inhuman teeth that laughed silently before vanishing.
The party resolved to press on toward the Chryseum, hoping to find a path to renewal before ruin consumed everything they carried it toward.

## Session 1: The Race Ahead of Ruin

**Date Played:** 2026-01-24

### Recap-Teaser
> Behind them, Ashbrook crumbles to dust. Before them, a cursed river
> races toward the Great Beaver Dam — and beyond it, everything they love.

### Summary
The party stood on the mountainside above the ruins of [[Ashbrook]] — once called [[Eld Ashara|Ashbrook]], the Place of Unmaking — watching the town collapse into decay behind them.
They had removed the [[Seal of Unmaking]] from its altar beneath the town's spring, and in doing so released the imprisoned force of Ruin into the headwaters of the [[Ashen Flow]].
The cursed water now raced downstream toward the Great Beaver Dam, and beyond it, the [[Ashen Vale]] and [[Crownvale]] — home to people [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] and [[Castor]] cared deeply about.

Desperate to outrun the corruption, the party rode hard through the [[Cinderwall]] mountains.
Along the way, they were stopped by a pack of [[Dire Coyotes|dire coyotes]] — shapeshifters blessed by the Wild Hunt, one of the [[Old Gods|old gods]].
The coyote leader demanded the Seal, claiming the party was carrying ruin through their hunting grounds.
Through persuasion and bardic storytelling, [[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]] and [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] convinced the pack to let them pass, though the price was steep: a horse, which [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] soothed with an illusion spell before surrendering it to the hungry pack.

Riding through the night, the party reached [[Beaver Lake]] by morning.
They split their efforts: [[Castor]] shifted into beaver form and convinced the colony to evacuate downstream, wielding a stick — a sign of authority in beaver society — and slapping his tail urgently to convey the danger.
[[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]], assisted by [[Edric Bloom|Edric]]'s bardic melody, erected a spirit wall across the river to filter the ruin from the water.
The barrier held, though imperfectly — the corruption began pooling at the barrier site, creating a new concentration of ruinous energy that would eventually overwhelm the ward.
Meanwhile, [[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]] attempted to heal the exhausted horses by calling upon Lucifer's light, but the proximity of ruin overwhelmed his divine power.
The horses withered and aged before his eyes, deepening his crisis of faith.

With no mounts and the party exhausted, [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] recalled an abandoned fisher's hut on the lakeshore where they could rest.
During the night watch, the hut's foundations crumbled — whether from age or the creeping influence of ruin — and [[Castor]], standing watch in giraffe form, felt a growing doubt about his own craftsmanship.
As the party prepared to leave, [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]] spotted a scrap of fabric bearing the livery of his grandson [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]], snagged in the reeds by the lake — fresh, as if someone connected to Aldric had been there recently.
Combined with the letter from [[Rowan yn Greenholt|Rowan]] mentioning someone dressed in Aldric's livery near [[Ashbrook]] before the disaster, suspicion fell on the ambitious young lord.

Walking through the [[Ashen Vale]] toward [[Crownvale]], the party encountered a merchant named Jacob whose preserved fish had inexplicably rotted.
His young daughter spoke disturbingly of endings as natural and comfortable things.
[[Edric Bloom|Edric]] purchased a barrel of the ruined fish and a cart, planning to sell it as fertilizer.
The party debated whether the girl's words reflected the spreading influence of ruin on the minds of ordinary people.
[[Sir Roderic Lightbearer|Roderic]] warned against reinforcing the philosophy of "new beginnings through destruction," fearing it empowered ruin further.

Arriving in [[Crownvale]], the party moved quickly.
[[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]], keeping his hood drawn to avoid recognition, purchased horses.
[[Edric Bloom|Edric]] distracted [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]]'s guards — including [[Sergeant Iyer]] — by buying them drinks at the tavern, where he pumped them for information.
He came away convinced Aldric was not involved in the ruin's release.
However, Edric experienced a disturbing vision of Sergeant Iyer's marital troubles and felt an almost irresistible compulsion to weaponize that knowledge — a whisper of ruin's influence that he barely resisted.
[[Mira]], Aldric's ten-year-old daughter, recognized Garland despite his disguise, and he slipped her a letter warning Aldric of the approaching danger.

A brief complication arose when the horse seller Laura accused Garland of theft — apparently confused or manipulated — but the guards recognized Garland and dismissed the claim.
The townspeople began turning against Laura, but Roderic intervened with a stirring blessing, urging compassion and attributing her confusion to the corrupting influence spreading across the land.
The party departed [[Crownvale]] on fresh horses, headed for [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]] to seek counsel from the [[The Light]] and the Wizard's College, knowing that Garland's spirit wall would not hold forever.

- Why was someone wearing [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]]'s livery at [[Beaver Lake]] and near [[Ashbrook]] before the disaster?
- Is the spreading influence of ruin affecting people's minds — making them accept decay and endings as natural?
- Can the spirit wall hold long enough for the party to find a solution at [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]]?
- What happened to the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]] that even with the Unmaker sealed and Lucifer ascendant, the empire still fell?
- Is [[Edric Bloom|Edric]]'s disturbing compulsion in the tavern a sign that ruin is specifically targeting the party?
- Does [[Count Albrecht Marrow|Marrow]]'s son in the church hierarchy mean the church in [[Marrow County]] cannot be trusted?
- Can the [[Seal of Unmaking]] be used to reseal the force of ruin, and if so, how?

### Questions Answered / Arcs Advanced
- **Can Castor communicate with beavers?** — Not through verbal speech, but through gestures, body language, tail-slapping, and familiarity built over generations. He can convey urgency and general concepts but not nuanced ideas. (Established [[Session 1]])
- **What is the relationship between the old gods and the new gods?** — The [[Old Gods|old gods]] are forces of nature — uncaring, neither good nor evil, but indifferent to human suffering. The new gods, led by Lucifer, represent civilization, order, and care for people. Lucifer drove the old gods into shadow. The Wild Hunt and the force of Ruin are old gods. The old traditions call ruin "the Gentle Collapse" — a natural necessity, not a demon — suggesting the Luciferians may have imprisoned something that was never meant to be caged. (Established [[Session 1]])
- **What is the party's relationship with Count Marrow?** — [[Count Albrecht Marrow|Marrow]] hired them to retrieve the [[Seal of Unmaking]]. The party has decided not to return it to him, suspecting his motives. They plan to bypass [[Marrow County]] on their way to [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]]. (Established [[Session 1]])
- **Curse of Ruin advanced:** The party released the force of ruin, raced ahead of the cursed river, evacuated the beaver colony, and erected a temporary spirit wall. Ruin is spreading its influence across the land. The party now heads to [[Beaconhold|Beacon Hold]] for help.
- **Garland's Uncover the Truth advanced:** Garland chose this as his character arc, driven by suspicion that [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]] may be connected to the events at [[Ashbrook]]. Evidence mounts but Edric's investigation suggests Aldric himself is uninvolved.
- **Roderic's Redemption advanced:** Roderic's crisis of faith deepened when his healing was overwhelmed by ruin. His successful blessings in Crownvale offered a counterpoint — his faith can still move people.
