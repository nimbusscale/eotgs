## 1. Summary

**Top 3 strengths (system-specific)**
1. **Spotlight management — a real jump from S3.** The GM explicitly and repeatedly
   passed the spotlight across the three-PC break ("I'm going to move the spotlight
   to Garland real quick," 577) and actively pulled the quiet PC in ("Castor, what
   do you want to do while this is happening," 1027; "Castor, I haven't really asked
   you anything... you want to do anything?" 2260). This directly fixes the S3
   spectator problem. GM mic time also dropped 58.5% → 53.7%.
2. **Intent-first fictional positioning.** "What do you want to happen here — if
   everything went perfect, what would it look like?" (1105) is the system's core
   framing, and the Garland sword-heave negotiation (1099-1135) is a model
   positioning correction — the table worked out what was *fictionally* happening
   (heaving the sword into Roderic's reach, not a Let Fly attack) before touching
   dice. The whole "describe the outcome, not the action" principle got named and
   adopted at debrief (2596-2607).
3. **Portray a Lively World.** Brenn and Severin are NPCs with their own theology
   and agency, not exposition dispensers; the Harlequin who picks his *own* lock and
   bolts mid-scene (637-668) is an NPC pursuing his own goal; renewal manifests
   down-to-earth (moss in the floor-cracks, torches shifting from warm to white
   light, 1740-1749).

**Top 3 growth areas (ranked by impact)**
1. **Think Dangerously — the clean 6- went soft *again*.** Roderic's Engage at
   disadvantage came up **6-** (735) and the result was: pushed back into the cell,
   keys grabbed, door not locked — fully reversible, no condition, and he's still
   "holding his own... could probably end all four pretty easily" (3058). A miss in
   a 4-on-1 brawl is the textbook Hard-Move trigger. Fix in §6.
2. **The break had no stakes — golden opportunities sat uncashed.** Established and
   never used: the Corona Vigil inquisitor watching Roderic's arrest (1:59), the
   ruinous mob forming above (2:55-2:56), guards who flee to raise the alarm
   (3283), the Order of the Eclipsed Sword outside. The table *and GM* named the
   problem at close ("lack of repercussions for the prison break," 2725). Fix in §5.
3. **The ~35-min renewal/ruin debate ran as near-pure exposition with no Move.**
   No roll, no Ask, no Make Them Choose, despite a live timer (the crowd battering
   the doors) the GM kept mentioning but never pressed, and despite it being a
   direct test of Roderic's Paladin Virtues. Fixes in §5 and §8.

**Quick wins for next session**
- On the next clean **6-**, play a Hard Move *fully* before "what do you do?"
- When a player **declines** the 7-9 ugly choice, the threat **does not abate** —
  escalate; don't settle into "a middle ground" (it happened at 996).
- Put the **door-timer to work**: when a scene has a stated clock (the mob at the
  doors), spend it — make it a Make Them Choose, not background noise.

**Notable moments (why, in CA terms)**
- **Garland's spellbook at the cocoon (4:19-4:22).** Player-initiated, but the GM
  built on it richly — the lost Witch-Stone knowledge "almost able to pierce
  through," pages "a little stronger than they once were." This is the *Wizard*
  playbook hook (a place of great magical power) finally landing, addressing S3's
  under-served-playbook note.
- **The sword-heave (1099-1135).** Clean fictional-positioning coaching: the GM
  surfaced the difference between an attack and just getting the weapon in range,
  and let Garland keep his real intent.

## 4. The five GM Principles

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks — Weak this session.** The break was framed as
   low-risk and played out that way; nobody leapt at anything dangerous because
   nothing *was* dangerous. _Fix: put a live threat in the room — **Set Up an
   Immediate Danger** with a clock: "Boots are coming down the stairwell — you can
   hear the keys jangling. You've got maybe a count of ten before the guard rounds
   the corner and sees the open cell. What do you do?" Now the lockpicking is a race,
   not a formality._
2. **Portray a Lively World — Strong.** NPC agency (Brenn/Severin's own theology, the
   Harlequin picking his own lock and bolting) and renewal manifesting physically.
3. **Think Dangerously — Weakest, flat from S3.** No Hard Move, no condition on any
   PC, the 6- softened. _Fix: on Roderic's Engage 6- (735), play the Hard Move fully —
   **Inflict a Condition + Throw Them Around:** "They wrench your arm behind you and
   slam you into the bars — mark a condition — and shove the cell door shut with you
   on the wrong side of it."_
4. **Leave Things Open — Good.** The herald-identity question stayed genuinely open
   (Severin "I do not offer proof, only faith," 4209), and Garland's player-driven
   spellbook beat was embraced and canonized.
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over — Mixed.** Strong "Ask" on intent ("what do you want to
   happen," 1105). The "Gloss" half is where the session bled: the prison-key
   sequence and the renewal debate both ran far past their tension (the GM diagnosed
   this himself, 2546, 2678). The debate especially wanted a cut or a clock.

## 5. GM Moves — usage, variety, and missed opportunities

**Used well**
- **Take Something Away** (957) — shattering the guard's sword on Garland's Engage
  partial was the session's one varied, non-condition consequence. More of this.
- **Introduce Something New** — Brenn and the Order of the First Dawn, Severin's
  arrival, the cocoon all landed as situation-changing reveals.

**Missed / under-used**
- **Foreshadow an Ominous Force / Set Up an Immediate Danger — the big one.** The
  break sat inside a city tipping into ruinous chaos, with an inquisitor who'd just
  watched Roderic get arrested and a mob forming overhead, and none of it pressed on
  the scene. The moment Roderic's fight bogged into a stalemate, you could have
  *Set Up an Immediate Danger*: _"The dungeon door at the top of the stairs booms —
  the ruinous mob's found the stairwell, and the lantern-masked inquisitor from your
  arrest is with them. You've got maybe a minute before this room stops being
  yours."_ That turns the barricade-and-search from a chore (which the table flagged
  as forgettable) into a race, and cashes threats you'd already planted.
- **Make Them Choose — never fired, despite two ripe setups.** (1) Garland's 7-9
  sneak keyed the ugly choice on an escaping Harlequin he had zero investment in, so
  it didn't bite. Tie it to a friend instead: _"You can stay hidden, or break cover
  to stop the escapee — but the only way to reach him in time is to expose yourself
  right as a guard turns toward Roderic's cell. Stay down and they stack on Roderic;
  move and they see you. Which?"_ (2) The debate's stated timer was a ready-made
  choice: _"Brenn won't be argued into trust, and the doors won't hold either —
  'Stand with my order and hold the inquisitors, or go down to the temple now and
  leave us to die holding the line. You can't do both.'"_
- **Test his Virtues (Paladin playbook) — left on the table.** The whole Severin
  scene is Roderic being asked to abet freeing a god his Church imprisoned — a direct
  collision with his Purity/Honor vows. Make it *his* crisis with the Paladin move
  **Test their commitment to their Virtues:** _"Severin, what you're describing — the
  Church sealed this god for a reason, and you want me to help undo it. Roderic, your
  Purity vow says you don't traffic with the unclean. Do you hold the line your order
  drilled into you, or do you trust this heretic's renewal over your own scripture?"_
  That turns 35 minutes of exposition into a decision only Roderic can make.

## 6. 6- and 7-9 handling

- **Roderic — rejoin +CHA → 8 (7-9).** Eventful venture; consequence was the capture
  that set up the scene. _Handled fine._
- **Castor — Defy +DEX to grab the keys → great success (10+).** Threat named before
  the roll (794); player narrated the result. _Handled well — the template._
- **Garland — Defy +DEX to sneak down (7-9, 604).** Ugly choice = an escaping
  Harlequin he could stop or ignore; he ignored it, the escapee made a distraction,
  "you're much more in danger of being spotted now" (662). _Acceptable but toothless
  — the choice keyed on something Garland didn't care about._ Fix: key the ugly
  choice to a friend so both branches cost him something he wants — _"The only way to
  reach the escapee in time is to break cover right as a guard turns toward Roderic's
  cell. Stay hidden and they stack on Roderic; move and they spot you. Which?"_
- **Roderic — Engage +STR at disadvantage → 6- (735).** _Too soft — missed Hard
  Move._ The result (pushed back into the cell, keys grabbed) is fully reversible and
  inflicts nothing. On a 6- in a 4-on-1, play a Hard Move fully first. **Inflict a
  Condition + Throw Them Around:** _"They wrench your arm up behind you and slam you
  into the bars — mark a condition — and shove the door shut with you on the wrong
  side of it. What do you do?"_ Or **Set Up an Immediate Danger:** _"One guard breaks
  off and bolts up the stairs bellowing for the watch — the alarm's going out to the
  whole Cresium now, and the others still have you pinned."_
- **Roderic — Defy +STR to break the grapple at disadvantage → 7-9 (983).** Ugly
  choice = break free only by maiming/killing a guard; Roderic declined. The GM then
  let it settle into "a middle ground... you keep grappling" (996-1004). _Wrong
  resolution: when the player declines the 7-9 ugly choice, the threat does **not**
  abate — escalate._ **Inflict a Condition + advance the threat:** _"You won't
  cripple a man to get loose — so you don't get loose. They bear you down under their
  combined weight, mark a condition from the crush, and one of them peels off to ring
  the alarm bell on the wall. What do you do?"_
- **Roderic — Decree +CHA → 13 (full success, 1169).** Clean: chose two effects,
  Harlequins held in their cells. _Handled well._

## 7. Conditions, consequences & fictional positioning

- **No condition was inflicted on a PC all session** (Garland carried one in from
  S3). Combined with the soft 6- and the declined-7-9 stalemate, that's why nothing
  felt dangerous — the table said as much.
- **Variety:** thin but slightly improved — the shattered sword (*Take Something
  Away*) and the escaping Harlequin (a new complication) were the only non-soft
  beats. Reach further on the next miss: an alarm raised, a barricade that buys time
  *but seals your own exit*, the inquisitor at the door.
- **Fictional positioning: a genuine strength.** Intent-first asks throughout; the
  sword-heave correction; letting the fiction pick the Defy stat (Garland choosing
  STR to heave the heavy sword vs. DEX, 1120-1124). Keep this.
- One judgment call worth keeping: ruling that Roderic *can't* Scrutinize the
  heralds because none are "morally wrong" (2098) was a defensible read of his
  custom Eyes-of-the-Faithful — and it correctly handed him the real answer (they
  believe they're right). Fine as played.

## 8. Missed character moments

- **Roderic (Paladin — Virtues: Honor, Purity, Mercy; Drive: endanger yourself to
  protect).** The Severin/Brenn debate *is* the Paladin playbook move "test his
  commitment to his Virtues" + "introduce a rival/corrupt authority" — a heretical
  paladin order asking him to help free an imprisoned god. It stayed thematic and
  never became a Move. Crystallize it: _"Severin is asking you to help unmake a
  prison the Light spent an age building. That cuts against your vow of Purity,
  Roderic — does helping them break the vow and cost you its boon, or have you
  reasoned your way to this being the Light's own will?"_ That makes the long talk
  *his* crisis and gives the Virtue mechanics teeth.
- **Castor (Druid — Drive: endanger yourself for wildlife or outcasts).** A dungeon
  full of falsely-jailed performers ("all these bards and performers," 539) is a
  cell of **outcasts** — a live drive hook, and the cells were even *deteriorating*
  in his ruinous wake (2509). He sat the debate out; one Ask — _"Castor, these caged
  performers are exactly the kind the world throws away. Does the beaver want to
  open every door in here?"_ — pulls him in on his own terms.
- **Garland (Wizard) — done right, keep it.** The cocoon spellbook beat is the model:
  when a PC reaches for their playbook identity, build on it generously. He drove it,
  but you met him fully.
