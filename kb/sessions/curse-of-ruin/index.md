# Curse of Ruin

**Sessions:** 1–5
**Theme:** Make Things Right
**Status:** Resolved (consequences ongoing)

## Major Events

- The party retrieved the Seal of Unmaking from Ashbrook, unwittingly releasing the God of Ruin and reducing the settlement to ancient ruins in moments; Garland's great-grandchild Rowan perished in the collapse
- Racing the cursed river through the Cinderwall, the party bargained with dire coyote shapeshifters, evacuated the beaver colony at Beaver Lake, and erected a spirit wall across the Ashen Flow
- At the Nodrum, the Triune revealed that ruin had a counterpart in the God of Renewal, long dormant since Lucifer's suppression of the old gods threw the natural cycle out of balance
- The Triune declared the party were the heralds of ruin, carrying the curse in their bodies rather than in the water; ruin targeted each herald's core identity: Roderic's protection, Garland's knowledge, Edric's inspiration, Castor's craftsmanship
- A ritual to contain the Xan-Kor at the Nodrum succeeded but corrupted ley lines and reality itself; the Bonewall's ancient wards began to crumble and a pivotal historical victory by Lucifer's champion was retroactively undone
- The Harlequins revealed they had been manipulating events since Ashbrook for the Laughing One's entertainment; the Aldric livery was a planted trick to exploit Garland's paranoia
- The party entered Aurelion through abandoned mine tunnels to avoid wanted posters bearing all four of their likenesses
- Castor traded his vow of celibacy to the rock rat Tufa for intelligence about the tunnels; Garland revealed the history of Second Harvest, his legendary sword
- Roderic was captured by the Aureate and imprisoned beneath the Chryseum after sending intelligence about the old gods' return to Beaconhold
- Dawnwarden Brenn shared her theology: Lucifer's suppression of ruin caused renewal to go dormant, leading to centuries of stagnation where civilizations crumbled and nothing rose to replace them
- Luminary Severin Morrow revealed the Morrow family as secret worshippers of renewal; he, Count Marrow, and Brenn are the heralds of renewal
- The party discovered the Shrine of Renewal beneath the Chryseum, an ancient pre-Luciferian chamber containing the dormant God of Renewal in a translucent cocoon
- Castor communed with the God of Renewal; the presence recognized him and confirmed his role as a protector of the balance
- The Dark Harlequin claimed the Laughing One was responsible for Castor's original beaver curse and attempted to recruit him; Castor drove it off as a wolverine
- Garland and Roderic infiltrated besieged Crest Aurelion using Garland's centuries-old knowledge of secret passages and convinced the reluctant Count Marrow to join them
- A Corona Vigil inquisitor branded Roderic and his companions heretics in the Aurelion vault
- Roderic beheaded the Dark Harlequin after it puppeteered a murdered Order of the First Dawn paladin's corpse at the Chryseum
- All six heralds gathered at the Shrine of Renewal; the cocoon opened, releasing the God of Renewal and transforming the chamber into a verdant, living space
- Edric Bloom vanished mid-step beneath an invisibility spell in the tunnels under the Chryseum; his fate remains unknown

## Epilogue

The cocoon opened, and the world breathed. Within the Shrine of Renewal, desiccated stone gave way to moss and root, torchlight burned clean and white, and six heralds stood in a circle where ruin and renewal moved through them like a tide finding its level. The God of Renewal stirred from its long dormancy, and for one fragile moment beneath the Chryseum, the old balance held. But balance is not peace. Above them, Aurelion still smoldered. First Aureate Eisen Dorn fell defending Crest Aurelion from the Order of the Eclipsed Sword, and with him went the tempering hand that had kept the city's soldiers steady through weeks of chaos. A reckoning followed in the days after: Luminary Severin Morrow, Count Albrecht Marrow, and Dawnwarden Brenn stood before the Corona Vigil's inquisitors and the remaining officers of the Eclipsed Sword, and what emerged was not absolution but a grudging truce. The Chryseum would remain a house of both the Light and Renewal, with Severin permitted to shepherd the awakened god under the Vigil's watchful eye. But the inquisitors had larger concerns pulling them away. Strange reports had begun filtering in from Beaconhold and beyond: gaps in the Church's own records, names missing from liturgies that had been unchanged for centuries, histories that no longer quite agreed with themselves. Something was pulling at the threads of the Church's own foundations, and the Corona Vigil could not afford to linger in Aurelion chasing heretics while those threads continued to unravel beneath them. They withdrew, and Roderic, who had been branded a heretic in the heat of the vault, found that no formal charges followed him home. The schism growing within the Light was too deep and too confused for anyone to prosecute a knight whose only crime was standing on the wrong side of a theological question that the Church itself could no longer answer.

The Ashen Vale bore the scars of its brush with ruin long after the heralds departed. Garland's spirit wall still stood at Beaver Lake, a monument to a crisis that had moved on without it, but the Great Beaver Dam told a different story. Weakened by the ruinous energy Castor had carried through the colony, the dam had partially collapsed, and the beavers who once maintained it had changed. Some refused to return to the old site. Others had spread downstream into the vale itself, damming irrigation channels, felling orchard trees, and flooding fields that farmers had worked for generations. The people of the vale, who had lived alongside the beaver colony for longer than most could remember, began to speak of them not as neighbors but as pests. Traps appeared along the lower Ashen Flow. Meanwhile, Aldric Garlandsson moved quickly to fill the vacuum that crisis always creates. His guards patrolled farther from Crownvale than before, his builders repaired roads and bridges with conspicuous generosity, and his name was spoken in villages that had never answered to anyone. The borders of Marrow County and the authority of Beaconhold's distant crown meant less with every mile of road Aldric's men maintained. Count Marrow, nursing a quiet anger at the ruin that had been brought to his doorstep and the indignity of needing rescue from his own castle, watched Aldric's expansion with cold attention and noted the Greenholt name behind it. That old Garland had walked through his vault, had seen his diminished gold, and had left with knowledge of his secret passage sat poorly with a man whose power depended on the appearance of strength. In Crownvale itself, a lingering apathy had settled over the town like fog that would not burn off. People went about their work, but the urgency was gone. A cult of whispered endings had taken root among those who remembered the merchant's daughter speaking so sweetly of collapse, and similar murmurings surfaced along the southern reaches of Marrow County, where ruin's passage had left the soil tired and the people quieter than they should have been.

And then there was Edric. He had vanished mid-step beneath an invisibility spell in the tunnels under the Chryseum, swallowed by the dark without a sound. The party searched. Garland's magic found no trace. The tunnels yielded nothing but old stone and silence. When the cocoon opened with only three heralds of ruin present, the pattern held, but barely, and everyone in that chamber felt the absence like a missing tooth. Edric Bloom, the bard who carried ruin in the souring of stories, who wore a faded yellow scarf bearing the symbols of fallen kings, was simply gone. Whether he walked out of the tunnels into some other darkness, or whether the darkness reached in and took him, no one could say.

Far to the east, along the spine of the Bonewall, the people of Beaconhold and the shining towns had always slept easy beneath the Thirteen Spines. The great towers rose from the mountain slopes like sentinels, their ward-lights visible for miles on clear nights, a constellation of protection strung along the border between the known world and whatever waited beyond. Thirteen lights, steady and unchanging, burning as they had burned for as long as anyone alive could remember. But in the weeks after the cocoon opened, travelers on the eastern roads began to notice. First one light, then another, flickering where they had never flickered before. And then, on a still night with no cloud and no wind, the northernmost Spine went dark. The soldiers garrisoned there sent word that the wards had simply stopped responding, as though the power that sustained them had been quietly withdrawn. The official reports blamed age, or neglect, or the lingering aftershocks of the corrupted ritual at the Nodrum. But the people who lived in the shadow of the Bonewall did not read reports. They counted lights. And the number, for the first time in living memory, was twelve.

## New Questions & Hooks

- What is the God of Renewal now that it has awakened, and how will it affect the broader world?
- Was the Laughing One truly responsible for Castor's original beaver curse?
- What happened to Roderic's lost battalion, and was his faith truly to blame?
- The Thirteen Spines are going dark along the Bonewall; what is pressing against the wards from Öuth Krelt?
- Something is missing from the Light's own traditions, histories, and hymns; what was lost, and can it be found?
- The beavers of Beaver Lake have become a growing problem in the Ashen Vale; can the rift between the colony and the vale's people be healed?
- Aldric Garlandsson is consolidating power beyond Crownvale, encroaching on the borders of Marrow County and Beaconhold
- Count Marrow harbors resentment toward the party and is watching Aldric's expansion with cold attention
- Cults of whispered endings have taken root in Crownvale and southern Marrow County
- First Aureate Dorn is dead; who now keeps order in Aurelion?

## Notable NPCs Introduced

- **Aldric Garlandsson** — Garland's grandson; ruler of Crownvale and would-be petty king of the Ashen Vale; never appeared on screen but loomed over the arc as a suspected conspirator before the Harlequins revealed the livery was a planted trick
- **Mira** — Aldric's ten-year-old daughter; recognized Garland despite his disguise
- **Sergeant Iyer** — Sergeant-at-arms in Aldric's guard in Crownvale
- **The Triune: Aurea, Venn, Delta** — Three halfling sisters and hedge-wizards of geometric arts operating from the Nodrum
- **The Dark Harlequin** — Monstrous leader of the Harlequins; capable of projecting visions and manifesting within objects; beheaded by Roderic
- **Tufa** — A mother rock rat in the mine tunnels beneath Aurelion
- **Dawnwarden Brenn** — Leader of the Order of the First Dawn; herald of renewal
- **Luminary Severin Morrow** — Head priest of the Chryseum; herald of renewal; son of Count Marrow
- **Count Albrecht Marrow** — Ruler of Marrow County; herald of renewal; commissioned the retrieval that started it all
