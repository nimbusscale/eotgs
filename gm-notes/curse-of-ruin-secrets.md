# Curse of Ruin — GM Secrets

See also: [[Curse of Ruin]]

## The Shape of Things

**What the party believes:** They accidentally freed a god of ruin.
The curse is spreading via the river.
They need to stop the curse before it reaches civilization.

**What's actually happening:** The river curse is a ruse — dramatic, visible, something heroes can "solve."
The real vehicle for the plague is the party themselves.
They've become unwitting Heralds of Ruin, spreading decay through every interaction, every act of heroism, every moment of connection with others.

**The cruel irony:** The more they try to help, the more they spread ruin.
The more heroic they are, the wider the plague reaches.

## The God of Ruin

**Titles and Epithets** (true name unknown/unknowable):
- **The Unmaker** — Luciferian term, pejorative
- **The Gentle Collapse** — old honorific, still used by hedge traditions
- **Lord/Lady of Endings** — formal old religion term
- **The Patient Dark** — poetic name
- **Terminus** — scholarly term
- **The Release** — used by those who honor endings
- **The Final Kindness** — whispered by those who tend the dying

Still wounded, still potentially sympathetic.
Centuries of imprisonment in whiteglass — the substance of preservation — has changed them.
What was once gentle release has become something rawer, less controlled.
The plague spreading through the Heralds may be unintentional — an overflow of long-suppressed power, not malice.

**Potential angles:**
- The god doesn't know they're spreading plague through the Heralds, and would be horrified to learn it
- The god knows but can't stop it — their control is shattered
- The god is too disoriented to understand what's happening at all
- The plague is the god "remembering" their purpose, but doing it wrong after so long
- The god's wounds make them unstable — sometimes the old gentleness surfaces, sometimes something harsher

**What the god might want:**
- Acknowledgment that endings are necessary, not evil
- A place in the cosmic order again
- To understand what happened to them
- To find Renewal (if they remember)
- Simply to rest, to be left alone, to stop hurting

**The tragic possibility:** The god of ruin isn't the villain.
They're a victim whose trauma is now causing harm they never intended.
Healing them might be how you stop the plague — not defeating them.

## Those Who Honor Ruin

Ruinous cultists aren't cackling villains.
They come to the god of ruin from different places:

**The Grieving** — People who lost everything and found peace only in accepting that loss is natural.
"When my children died, only the Gentle Collapse offered comfort.
Not promises of reunion, not platitudes.
Just... permission to let go."

**The Exhausted** — People tired of the endless Luciferian labor of preservation.
"Every day we shore up walls, mend what's broken, fight the tide.
I'm so tired.
The Patient Dark offers something the Light never does: an end to the struggle."

**The Philosophical** — Those who believe the natural order has been disrupted.
"The world is constipated with things that have outlived their purpose.
The Unmaker isn't evil — they're necessary."

**The Converted** — Once victims of ruin who came to see it as revelation.
"The fire took my home, my shop, my life's work.
And in the ashes I finally saw clearly.
I had been holding on so tight.
The Release showed me I could let go."

**The Desperate** — Those who want something specific to end: a tyrannical ruler, a corrupt institution, an abusive relationship, their own suffering.
They come to Ruin not from philosophy but from need.

## The God of Renewal

**Titles and Epithets** (true name unknown/unknowable):
- **The Beloved** — in relation to Ruin, used by those who know the old stories
- **The Green After Fire** — poetic name
- **The First Shoot** — old honorific
- **The Dawn After** — scholarly term
- **Hope** — sometimes, simply
- **The Returner** — used by those who await the awakening
- **The Second Chance** — whispered by those seeking redemption

Dormant. Waiting.
When Ruin was sealed, Renewal went into a kind of hibernation — a cocoon state, waiting to emerge when their counterpart was free again.
The sealing of Ruin broke something in Renewal too; without endings, there can be no true beginnings.

**Current state possibilities:**
- Physically cocooned somewhere — a sacred grove, a hidden shrine, beneath the earth
- Beginning to stir as Ruin's power flows again
- Still dormant but their followers sense the change coming
- Dreaming, and those dreams are bleeding into the world in strange ways

**What Renewal's awakening might mean:**
- The cure to the plague (Renewal balances Ruin)
- A new complication (Renewal is also changed by centuries of dormancy)
- A reunion that could heal both gods — or go terribly wrong
- A restoration of the old balance that threatens the Luciferian order

**Followers:** Think Celtic/druidic aesthetic.
They've kept the old ways in secret — hedge witches, hermits, rural communities that never fully converted, families with long memories.
They know Renewal sleeps and have been waiting for the awakening.
Some operate openly in the wilds; others hide within cities.

## The God of Trickery

The mastermind.
Not evil — none of the Old Gods are.
A force of chaos, mischief, change, disruption.
They embody the truth that plans go awry, that certainty is illusion, that the universe has a sense of humor (and it's not always kind).

**Possible names/epithets:**
- The Laughing One
- Lord/Lady of Misrule
- The Crooked Path
- The Motley
- The Unexpected
- The Reversal

**Nature:** Amoral, not immoral.
The Trickster doesn't serve good or evil — they serve change, disruption, the puncturing of certainty.
They lie, but sometimes their lies reveal deeper truths.
They cause chaos, but sometimes that chaos breaks stagnant systems.

**Why they orchestrated this (possibilities):**
- The cosmic order has calcified; the Trickster is introducing chaos to a system that's become too rigid
- They genuinely believe freeing the imprisoned gods is right — they just can't do it straightforwardly
- They're bound by their essence: even when they want to do something good, they must work through misdirection
- They find the Luciferian order unbearably boring
- They have their own wound — perhaps they weren't sealed because no one took them seriously enough
- They want to see what happens; they're genuinely curious
- They owe Ruin or Renewal something from before the sealing

**The river ruse:** Classic Trickster work — give the heroes something obvious to fight while the real game plays out invisibly.
The Trickster knows how heroes think.
They need villains, threats, clear objectives.
So the Trickster gave them one.

**Possible relationship to events:**
- Directly manipulated Marrow (appearing as advisor, vision, mysterious benefactor)
- Set up the circumstances for Marrow to find them, making it seem like Marrow's own idea
- Has agents who did the actual work while the Trickster watched
- Was the one who ensured the party specifically would be hired
- Set up the "seal" to infect whoever broke it, knowing eventually someone would

## The Harlequins

Followers of the Trickster.
Not a formal cult — more like a loose network of those who've been touched by the Laughing One's influence.

**Aesthetic:** Motley, masks, bells, patchwork.
Classic harlequin imagery that can range from theatrical and almost playful to deeply unsettling.

**What they do:**
- Spread chaos, disruption, change
- Puncture certainties, expose hypocrisies
- Test people — are you as virtuous as you claim?
- Manipulate, misdirect, arrange circumstances
- Sometimes genuinely help people — but never straightforwardly

**Possible encounters:**
- A Harlequin who seems to help the party but steers them toward greater spread of the plague
- A Harlequin who knows what the party has become and finds it hilarious
- A Harlequin who wants to tell the party the truth — but only in riddles
- A Harlequin who is themselves a pawn, not knowing who they truly serve
- A Harlequin who has doubts about the Trickster's plan

**Tone options:** Can be played light (mischievous, theatrical, almost likeable) or dark (sinister, alien, wrong laughter).
Mix both for unsettling effect.

## The Heralds of Ruin

When the party broke the seal, they didn't just free the god of ruin — they became carriers of that power.
The plague doesn't spread through water or air.
It spreads through them.

**The mechanism (keep vague):** Don't over-explain how the plague spreads.
It happens through interaction, through connection, through the party being present in people's lives.
The more meaningful the interaction, the stronger the infection.
Saving someone's life is more contagious than passing them on the street.

### Each Herald's Burden

The cruel design: each Herald spreads ruin that mirrors their greatest virtue.

**Roderic — Herald of Failing Wards**
His gift is protection.
His presence makes people feel safe.
What he spreads: Protections fail; walls develop cracks; wards flicker; locks stick then break; faith wavers; certainties crumble; guardians falter.
The irony: People seek his protection, and he genuinely protects them — but he leaves behind a world where protection is harder for everyone.

**Garland — Herald of Forgetting**
His gift is knowledge, wisdom, the long view.
What he spreads: Memories blur; people forget important things; knowledge becomes uncertain; understanding fades; history becomes muddled.
The irony: He seeks to pass on knowledge before his death, and those he teaches slowly lose the ability to retain what they've learned.

**Edric — Herald of Souring Tales**
His gift is story, reputation, the ability to shape perception.
What he spreads: Stories twist; reputations decline; deals go bad; words fail; communication breaks down.
The irony: The storyteller who trades in words leaves behind a world where words can't be trusted.

**Castor — Herald of Crumbling Foundations**
His gift is building, community, the deep connection between creature and home.
What he spreads: Foundations crack; buildings settle wrong; communities fragment; nature unbalances; homes become unhomely.
The irony: The builder whose beaver-nature drives him to create — he leaves behind a world where nothing holds together.

## The Herald Legend

There's a legend about Heralds who come before an age of ruin.
Edric knows it — this is Ken's territory to define when the time comes.
The party will initially assume the legend refers to external threats.
The dawning realization that they are the Heralds is the emotional gut-punch of this arc.

**When to introduce:** Drop hints that the legend exists before revealing its relevance.
Edric might tell it around a campfire, thinking it's just a story.
NPCs might reference it when strange things happen.
Let the players start to suspect before confirmation.

**What the legend might contain (for Ken to develop):**
- Four figures (or a number that matches the party)
- Each brings a different kind of ruin
- They come in the wake of something freed/awakened
- They don't know what they are
- There's probably something about how they can be stopped, cleansed, or redeemed — but interpretations vary

## The River Ruse

The curse on the river is real — but it's a deliberate misdirection.
The Trickster (or their agents) set it up to give the party something to fight.

**Why it works:**
- Heroes need villains, threats, clear objectives
- The river curse is all of those things
- While the party focuses on the dam, the real threat spreads invisibly
- Every person they meet while "saving" the valley gets infected
- Their heroism becomes the vector

**What happens when they "solve" it:**
- They probably can solve it — save the dam, stop the water, protect the valley from that threat
- They'll feel like heroes
- And then strange things start happening to everyone they helped
- The plague is already everywhere they've been

**The Trickster's fingerprints:**
- The river curse is too convenient, too theatrical
- A Harlequin might have been seen near the spring
- The curse behaves strangely — almost like it's putting on a show
- Someone might point out that curses don't usually work this way

## Count Marrow's True Motivations

See also: [[Count Albrecht Marrow]]

A believer in Renewal, operating within Luciferian society.
His family has kept old traditions secretly — or perhaps framed them as "acceptable" Luciferian mysticism while actually serving older purposes.
He wants to awaken Renewal, believing this will heal something broken in the world.

**What Marrow knows (possibilities):**
- Knows about Renewal, knows (roughly) where they sleep
- Knows the Seal of Unmaking held a god
- Believed breaking the seal would somehow facilitate Renewal's awakening
- May or may not know about the Trickster's involvement
- Doesn't know about the Heralds or the plague spreading through the party

**How Marrow was used (possibilities):**
- The Trickster appeared to him directly — as a vision, a mysterious advisor, a voice in dreams
- The Trickster manipulated circumstances so Marrow would "discover" the plan himself
- Marrow found old family records that were actually planted or altered
- The Trickster worked through intermediaries and Marrow never knew there was another player

**Marrow's position now:**
- He got what he wanted (the seal broken, Ruin freed, Renewal stirring)
- He probably didn't want the plague, the chaos, the beaver dam crisis
- He may be trying to manage the situation, limit damage, stay hidden
- He may be horrified and looking for ways to fix things
- He may be doubling down, believing it will all be worth it when Renewal wakes

**Potential grievance with the party:** Keep this in your back pocket.
If you need to complicate things, Marrow has some history with one of the characters — a past slight, a grudge, a debt unpaid.
Not defined until you need it.

**Possible Marrow beats:**
- The party discovers who hired them and confronts him
- Marrow reaches out, claiming he can help them
- Marrow becomes an unexpected ally against the Trickster when he realizes he was played
- Marrow betrays them (if he has reason to)
- Marrow's position collapses as the church investigates the crisis

## The Church of Lucifer — What They Know

See also: [[Church of Lucifer]]

**At various levels:**
- Common faithful: The Old Gods are myths, demons, or "pagan superstitions." The Light protects.
- Local clergy: There are dangerous forces that the wards hold back. Don't ask questions, trust the rituals.
- Regional leadership: The old powers are real but contained. The church's sacred duty is maintaining containments.
- Highest levels: Some may know the full history — the deliberate sealing, the suppression. They believe it was necessary. Perhaps some have doubts.

**Potential responses to current events:**
- Send inquisitors to investigate the curse, possibly targeting the party
- Attempt to reseal the god of ruin (repeating the original wrong)
- Declare the party heretics or plague-carriers (ironically correct)
- Some reformers within the church might question whether the old ways were right
- Political factions might try to use the crisis for their own advancement

**Tension with Roderic:** A Luciferian paladin discovering the truth about his faith's history.
What does he do with that knowledge?
This is Ramsey's story to tell, but the material is there for drama if he wants it.
