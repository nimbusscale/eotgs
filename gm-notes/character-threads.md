# Character Threads

GM-facing planning notes for each PC — useful context for session prep and encounter design.
These are not player-facing; playable hooks live in `kb/story-arcs/character/`.

## Castor

- [[The Triune]] secretly siphoned energy from his curse to power [[The Xan-Kor|the Aegis Mechanism]] — a betrayal of trust; deploy when dramatically appropriate
- The [[God of Renewal]] recognized him and embraced him with deep warmth — a nostalgic connection stronger than any other herald experienced
- His herald curse — the herald of crumbling foundations — causes physical structures and objects to decay, break, and turn to dust; the irony is that he's a builder whose nature drives creation
- The beaver dam has partially collapsed; scrying revealed no ruin in the water — the curse travels with the party, not the river
- **Player-flagged arc direction:** Castor is entering a "mid-life crisis" — renewed wanderlust and curiosity about human life after lifetimes as a beaver, plus a drive to test the limits of his mitigated curse, which he increasingly sees as a gift rather than an affliction; he hungers again for both the highs and lows of human living (player note, downtime after the [[Curse of Ruin]])
- His stewardship of the [[Beaver Lake]] colony is failing: the beavers rebel against the "old ways," some may know he was the bringer of ruin, and his sticks of authority no longer command — his personal stake in the vale's broader beaver problem
- **The Moldrex theory (GM, the direction I'm taking — coordinate with Dustin):** [[Moldrex]] and [[Castor]] are tied together. Moldrex was someone Castor knew, who ascended to godhood *during Castor's lifetime* (this is why [[Moldrex]]'s player-facing entry now places his ascension after Lucifer, in the Imperium era, rather than during the Godstorm). Ascension has a cost: shedding mortality means the discarded humanity lands on someone, and Moldrex's fell on Castor and took the shape of the beaver. The curse was a transaction, not a punishment — Castor was the receipt.
- **Natural vs. unnatural evolution (the balance):** read Castor as a *natural* evolution — a remaking by the wild that, mitigated, he increasingly treats as a gift — against Moldrex as the *unnatural* evolution, the god of aberration who drags the living backward into wrong forms (cf. the Slate devolution in the GM note *The Slate*). The two are halves of one severed craft: Moldrex builds with flesh, Castor with wood. This gives the thread an endpoint — the discarded humble half may be the only thing that can weaken or unmake the god half, so undoing Castor's curse and contending with Moldrex become the same problem.
- **The Laughing One's claim may hold:** the [[Dark Harlequin]]'s taunt that the [[Laughing One]] cursed him need not be a clean lie — the Laughing One may have arranged or savored the joke even if Moldrex's ascension is the mechanism ("a god who couldn't stop being petty"). Leave the exact truth unknown; it's a riddle to play, not a fact to settle.
- **The Dark Harlequin as Castor's recurring foil (Session 8):** keep building the Dark Harlequin–Castor relationship and bring it back in Session 8. As an emanation of the Laughing One it survived its beheading (now reflected in the player-facing [[Old Gods]] entry as the two-masks Comedy/Tragedy framing); a natural return vector is *within something Castor crafts*, since it manifests inside objects. The alternate, lighter Comedy face is available to surface in Session 8 as a contrast to the Dark Harlequin.
- ⚠️ Keep Castor out of the **forgetting** thread (Garland and Mira own that — see the *Session 8 Prep* note §7). His lane is the devolution/aberration mirror to Moldrex, not erasure.

## Garland yn Greenholt

- Previously consulted [[The Triune]] about [[Castor]]'s curse but kept the druid's true identity secret
- Known as "Lord Greenholt" in the [[Ashen Vale]] — widely recognized and must disguise himself to move unnoticed
- The Aldric livery mystery resolved as a Harlequin trick, but broader questions about [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]] remain
- His herald curse — the herald of forgetting — caused knowledge to be lost and people to forget things in his proximity; its active spread ended when renewal was reborn (the rebirth stripped the heralds' ruinous influence), but the losses it already worked persist and have not returned
- **Garland–Iyer favor (strategic angle):** the bond itself is recorded on [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]]'s `## Favors` section (its source of truth) — what's GM-private here is the lever: [[Sergeant Iyer]]'s open defiance of [[Captain Vask]] before the families and soldiers will bring trouble down on him from the captain and from [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]], and Garland's life-sworn debt to him can be pulled either way as that fallout unfolds
- His spellbook responded to residual renewal energy at the [[Shrine of Renewal]], growing slightly stronger — the first real hope that knowledge stolen by the forgetting curse could be recovered
- The loss of [[Rowan yn Greenholt]] and the fall of [[Ashbrook]] remain emotionally unresolved
- **Player backstory hook:** ~100 years ago something drew Garland from the vale on a long journey; he was responsible for the death of a friend, then brought the man's widow and child home and married her, founding the second family from which [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]] and [[Rowan yn Greenholt|Rowan]] descend — what called him away and how the friend died are left open for the GM to develop
- **Downtime:** returned to the vale to assess and mitigate ruin's lingering effects and be a comforting presence, watching but steering wide of [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]]; escorted [[Castor]] as far as the vale, then parted ways
- **Renewed Witch Stone connection:** on returning to the [[Witch Stones]] he felt a bond different from his centuries-built one — it returns *magic* (surfacing as ritual magic) but not the *knowledge* the forgetting curse took
- **The reluctant apprentice (Mira):** [[Mira]], Aldric's daughter, has declared herself his apprentice and hears the stones as words rather than earth-sounds — an emerging cleric of the Witch Stones with a possibly deeper connection than Garland's; the player loves the irony of Garland being forced to teach lessons he himself needs to relearn. To be expanded in upcoming session notes. (Mechanically the player is eyeing a ritual-magic level and an "obtain an asset: apprentice" level, but those are not locked in.)
- **Why Mira hears the stones — the forgotten place (GM, locked):** The [[Witch Stones]] sit in an in-between, partly outside normal time — the same interdimensional register as [[The Xan-Kor|the Xan-Kor]]'s prison beneath [[The Nodrum]]. When Garland's forgetting curse took his single most recent memory of Mira at the Nodrum (the [[Session 1]] note he gave her for [[Aldric Garlandsson|Aldric]], its contents now lost to him — *not* his knowledge of the child), that memory was not destroyed but exiled to where the forgotten go. Because a piece of their shared past now resides in that between-space, **part of Mira resides there too** — and that is the true source of her unnatural ear for the stones: she hears them as words rather than grinding earth because she is already half-present in the place they open onto. This binds her gate-craft, the Witch Stone network, the [[God of Forgetting]], and the Xan-Kor into a single thread. (GM-held; surface gradually. The live tension Garland carries — that he cannot recall what the note said — is the player-facing tip of this.)
- **Held card — the "unborn" (Session 7):** Garland privately recognized the sigil of [[Paxton Lumnus|Paxton]]'s resurrection as the mark of what his youth called the *unborn* — those reborn through the Light — and deliberately said nothing to Paxton. This is a player-chosen secret to spring when dramatically appropriate; the recognition is recorded on [[Garland yn Greenholt|Garland]]'s page, but the timing and the full meaning of the "unborn" are GM-held.
- **The far Witch Stone ring (Session 7):** the Bonewall ring is twin to Garland's but carved in an unreadable runic language, his bond to it faint — a hook into a wider stone network. He intends to study it and reproduce Mira's grasping-gate as a ritual to send her home; a natural lever for both the network mystery and the life-sworn debt to return Mira to Crownvale.
- **The stones as the last witnesses (GM):** the [[Witch Stones]] stand slightly outside linear time, so the **erasure** cannot reach what they hold — they still remember [[Sariel]] when scripture, busts, and clergy do not. The grinding Garland deciphered for centuries was the stones replaying history the world had already lost. (Terminology: this is *the erasure* / the [[The Xan-Kor|Xan-Kor]]'s work — do **not** call it "the Unraveling.")
- **The erasure's clock against the stones (GM):** because the rings are the last witnesses, the [[The God of Forgetting|God of Forgetting]] — through [[The Xan-Kor|the Xan-Kor]] and its other reach — has reason to hunt and erase the stone rings one by one. This sets the forgetting directly against the stones' own creed (*the land remembers*).
- **What the stones may be (GM, open):** working idea — the Witch Stones channel a *higher-order* Old God, a "Zeus of the Old Gods" with other Old Gods (the Forgetting among them) arrayed against it. Leave open; it also feeds the Slate's "stone god" / Syvämaa buried-sun question ([[the-slate]]) and Mira's emerging deity ([[Mira]]).
- **The restoration ritual shape (GM, loose):** to restore [[Sariel]] — a place that remembers (the stone rings) + a person who remembers (Roderic) + a voice that can reach across (Mira) + a body anchor (a surviving relic of Sariel, TBD).
- **Who is in the forgotten place (GM, open):** the Displaced, Sariel, and possibly [[Edric Bloom|Edric]] (vanished beneath the Chryseum, which resonates with the stones). Portal travel passes through that place — glimpses already in play (Castor's wind-form vision of the vale before the Beacon, Session 7); a misfire or deliberate ritual could strand the party there properly.
- **Garland's culpability for Mira's wound (GM, the reveal):** Garland was a herald of ruin, and the heralds spread ruin through southern [[Beaconhold]], [[Marrow County]], and the [[Ashen Vale]]. In [[Crownvale]] it took the form of a *ruin of apathy* — the guards' indifference, the lingering fog over the town, the children daring each other to leap from Aldric's parapets ([[Session 2]]) — the same wound that drove [[Mira]] into the depression that opened her ear to the stones ([[Mira]]). He is not singled out as personally to blame, but as a herald he bears real responsibility. Play the reveal order: emotional gut-punch first (the party, Garland among them, caused the harm), cosmic turn second (her wound is why she can hear, and why she may be the key to restoring Sariel).

## Sir Roderic Lightbearer

- Prior experience working alongside the [[Order of the Eclipsed Sword]] — his own order, the Redeemers of Light, once supplemented Eclipsed Sword operations; respects their tactical efficiency but fears their rigid adherence to orders with no room for judgment
- His faith proved effective against mortal authority — divine decrees command obedience from both Aureate soldiers and Harlequin prisoners
- His herald curse — the herald of failing wards — causes protections to crumble; ruin struck at his core identity as a protector (door lock crumbled, ley line energy passing through him emerged corrupted)
- Articulated a nuanced theological position: ruin and renewal are forces of nature subordinate to [[Lucifer]], not gods — those who worship them are ignorant, but not wrong to believe these forces must exist
- Discovered the [[Shrine of Renewal]] and confirmed [[Luminary Severin Morrow|Severin]], [[Count Albrecht Marrow|Marrow]], and [[Dawnwarden Brenn]] as heralds of renewal
- **Names resolved (Session 7):** the tomb Roderic's battalion entered is the [[Tomb of the Spinewright]], and the relic they sought is [[The Spinewright's Lantern]], a portable ward that could carry the Light past the wall. The tomb is now buried under a fresh rockslide. (The inquisitor from the Aurelion vault was named [[Inquisitor Voss|Voss]] earlier.)
- **Roland — seeded antagonist (Session 7):** [[Roland]] was improvised at the table (not pre-named) as a deliberate antagonist who sends [[The Spinewright's Lantern|lantern]]-marked men to keep Roderic from the tomb, fearing his return would jeopardize something. Open canvas: who Roland is, what the lantern necklace/cult is, what's now in the tomb, and how it ties to the night the battalion died. A [[Carnforth]] chaplain reputedly wears the same necklace — a thread toward a hidden order on the failing northern spine. **All Roland specifics stay GM-only** (the player-facing [[Roland]] entry keeps only what the party can see).
- **The tomb was a betrayal from inside (GM, now canon):** the massacre of Roderic's company in the [[Tomb of the Spinewright]] was not the tomb's trials but a betrayal worked from within. The bodies were mutilated as *bookkeeping*, not rage — destroyed so no one could count the dead, because someone walked out unaccounted for. Roderic surviving was by design: a massacre needs a culprit, and a lone survivor with no memory is a ready-made scapegoat. He wasn't spared — he was *cast*.
- **Griswald's true role (GM):** making Roderic a knight-errant was protection disguised as punishment — getting the scapegoat beyond the Church's reach before a tribunal could finish the framers' work. ([[Primus Griswald]] is the vanished mentor; [[Vicar Lucis Gregory]] is the one with the missing [[Sariel]] bust.)
- **The lantern-cult is the conspiracy (GM):** Roland and the [[The Spinewright's Lantern|Hiillos-lantern]] necklaces are a hidden faction within/around the Church, the same hand behind the tomb betrayal. **Roland was spared the erasure** — he still remembers [[Sariel]] — and is using that knowledge and this moment to make a move against [[The Light|the Light]] at large. Possibly tied to the vanishing faithful ([[Primus Griswald|Griswald]] and the others — see the group hook *The Vanishing Faithful*). Their precise ends and the faction's name are still open.
- ⚠️ Predates the Slate/Syvämaa canon — this church-conspiracy layer coexists with **Vaalo** / the GM note *The Slate*; it does **not** replace the Spinewright's Deep-earth origin. And per Joe: **[[Sariel]] has no connection to the Spines** — drop the brainstorm's "Sariel as the spiritual half of the wards" idea. (The relic-can-*unraise*-a-ward notion is brainstorm only, not canon.)

## Paxton Lumnus

- **Reborn wrong (Session 6):** His drive (act callously/recklessly about death) already cost innocent lives on Senna's barge; a recurring engine for tragedy as he treats his own survival as expendable without counting others' cost
- **The cost made personal (Session 7):** wading back to the barge, he could save only one side of a hidden family — pulled a woman and child free, left the husband Roy to burn, and realized the marsh corpse he'd found was the woman's son. The player leaned into the lesson that he can't keep spending lives so cavalierly; whether it actually changes his recklessness is the live thread. A natural place to test his drive again, and to surface the debt-of-favor he owes a party member his Light has burned.
- **Resurrection vs. Senna (Session 7):** established in play that Paxton's spontaneous resurrection is genuinely rare and divine (no ritual), while Senna was not raised from death but metaphysically transformed/"reborn" in Moldrex's vision. Keep the two distinct — Paxton is Light-made, Senna is Moldrex-made.
