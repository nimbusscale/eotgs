## The Bonewall

The eastern arm of the L-shaped border range. Cold, dry, and windswept, with far less snow than the [[Cinderwall]] despite the chill. What sets it apart is the stone itself, and the fact that it was not always this empty.

**Sight.** The Bonewall is built of pale shale, bone-white where it is freshly broken and weathering down through ashen greys to near-black in its oldest, most sheltered hollows. It is brittle stone that splits and flakes into loose plates and shards, and it erodes into shapes the eye reads as skeletal: ridges running like exposed ribs along the spurs, knuckled outcrops, smooth hollows worn into the cliffs like empty sockets. The land behind the first slopes is deep and broken, a country of ravines, blind valleys, and stacked outcroppings where sightlines are short and travel turns maze-like. The only stubborn growth is a hardy woody vine, grey-brown and near-leafless, sprawling in thick knotted tangles across the shale and choking the mouths of ravines, like old grapevine swollen to a wilder scale.

**Sound.** Everything underfoot crunches and clatters. Loose shale plates slide and break with each step, so no one crosses the Bonewall quietly. Above that runs the wind, which never fully stops, moaning through the ravines and the hollow shells of ruined structures. Beneath both is a deep silence: no birdsong, no insect drone, none of the ordinary noise of a living landscape.

**Smell.** The defining note is dust. Dry, chalky, mineral, the smell of powdered stone and old plaster, with a cold flinty edge where fresh rock is split. It settles on the tongue and dries the throat, and the wind keeps it moving. Just as distinctive is what is absent: no loam, no rot, none of the green or wet smell of living things. The one exception is the vine, which gives off a faint bitter resin where it is crushed or broken, a thin living scent that only sharpens how dead everything around it smells. Travelers often say the Bonewall smells like a sealed tomb, which, given the warden mausoleum beneath the Spine of Hallowreach, is not far wrong.

**A wall that was lived on.** The Bonewall was never true wilderness. Under the [[Imperium Lucis Aeternae|Imperium]] it was settled and sustained, its harsh ground made livable by imperial engineering and magic. There were no great cities, but there were towns, quarrying camps cut into the shale, waystations strung along the roads, and remote sites given over to magical research and to supplying the [[Thirteen Spines]]. When the Imperium fell, the support that held all of it in place fell with it, and the wall emptied with startling speed. What remains is a ghost country: abandoned settlements, worked-out quarries, and the derelict shells of imperial works, standing dry and dust-choked where the people simply walked away.

**The roads.** A few roads are still kept open. These are the supply lines [[Beaconhold]] and the [[Spinewardens]] rely on to provision the Spines that remain manned, and they are the exception. Most of the old imperial roads are abandoned and failing, their pale paving plates heaved and scattered, half-swallowed by shale and vine. Along them sit the bones of the old order: toppled mile-markers, the foundations of dead waystations, the empty husks of border checkpoints. The ancient military road still runs precariously along the base of the wall toward [[The Reaches]].
