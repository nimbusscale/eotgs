## 1. Summary

**Top 3 strengths (system-specific)**
1. **Think Dangerously — the best session yet.** A PC condition landed (Garland tackled
   by Sergeant Iyer, line 378-383, narrated cause-first, player chose STR + "my back"),
   and Paxton's choice to torch the barge killed Senna *and* innocent stowaways hidden in
   the cargo — irreversible, and pointed at a PC's own reckless act rather than ambient
   scenery (804). This directly cashes the S5 carry-forward.
2. **Leave Things Open / Ask Then Gloss — the bedrock of the whole session.** Nearly all
   the fiction was co-authored: Roderic's tomb backstory (86-118), the Reaches geography
   and the barge (508-563), the beaver politics, Mira's portal. The GM repeatedly
   retconned to improve on player input ("I'm okay adjusting based on what you destroyed
   to reveal," 92) and asked the right open questions ("why Aurelion and not Beaconhold?"
   497; "how does Mira know where these are?" 185).
3. **Portray a Lively World.** Captain Vasque's crown-on-a-mountain broach, three named
   hostile family heads (Cecil Thornby, Dural Beck, Walthallis in his beaver furs), the
   ravens-that-aren't-ravens veering off the witch stones (332-333), Gregory's darting
   eyes and ceremonial dagger ("I sleep with this now," 935), Senna's scaled healing hand
   and reptilian eyes (714-720). Sharp, specific, self-driven.

**Top 3 growth areas (ranked by impact)**
1. **Spotlight cadence across the split — three vignettes run fully sequentially.** Each
   player was "on" for ~30 minutes then idle; non-active players disengaged (Roderic went
   AFK and missed Paxton's entire scene, 951; a long out-of-game tangent filled Paxton's
   barge fight). The GM self-diagnosed it ("I got to play the entire thing and everyone
   just got to listen," 886). The CA fix is intercutting at beats — see §3.
2. **One live threat to a PC dissolved without a Move — the demand for Castor.** Armed men
   and angry trappers came explicitly to seize Castor ("we need to start with this one,"
   316) and the scene resolved by everyone fleeing through Mira's portal; nobody made a
   Move *at* Castor. Fix in §5.
3. **Encourage Exciting Risks — weak in the exposition-heavy Beaconhold thread.** The
   Gregory scene was pure investigation with no jeopardy or invitation to act. Fix in §4.

**Quick wins for next session**
- When threads are split, **cut away on a cliffhanger** ("the tentacle yanks you under —
  hold that — Roderic, you're staring at the empty plinth where Sariel's bust stood")
  instead of finishing one player's whole arc before starting the next.
- Keep leading 7-9 Defy with the **ugly choice** (Paxton's "drop the sword or stay in the
  water" at 668 was textbook — do that every 7-9 Defy).
- When the fiction puts a helpless community or an outcast PC in armed reach, **fire a
  Move at the PC** even with no dice out (the demand-for-Castor, §5).

**Notable moments (why, in CA terms)**
- **Paxton drops his sword to reach the woman (668-673).** A 7-9 Defy resolved as a clean
  ugly choice — sacrifice the weapon or stay flanked — and the player chose, generating
  real fiction. Model 7-9 Defy handling.
- **The Sariel bust with no dust (906-923).** *Introduce Something New + Foreshadow an
  Ominous Force*: the missing bust with "a layer of dust like it never existed" makes the
  forgetting physical and points Roderic at the arc. Excellent slow-burn dread.

## 2. Continuity — am I improving?

**Open recommendations checked:**

- **[S3→S5, top priority] Make a Move *at* a PC at non-roll trigger points — ◐ partial,
  trending up.** In the *combat* threads the GM fired Moves cleanly and unprompted: the
  scaled vine grapples Paxton's foot on his 6- Examine (590), a second creature flanks him
  on the Unleash Radiance "unwanted attention" (693), the tentacle hauls him back in
  (743), and Sergeant Iyer tackles Garland into a condition the instant he lunges for the
  portal (378). That's the habit landing. The **one** uncashed trigger is the
  demand-for-Castor: armed men and trappers openly threaten him and it dissolves into the
  portal escape with no Move at Castor (309-352). Net: improving, with one social-threat
  miss. Fix in §5.
- **[S5, new] Point the world-breaking *at* a PC — ◐→✅, trending up.** This is the
  session's biggest step. The grim, irreversible consequence (Senna + hidden stowaways
  burned to death, 804) is *caused by Paxton's own reckless choice* — his Drive in action,
  not ambient scenery. The refinement: the innocents' screams were revealed as **aftermath
  after** he was already in the water, not foregrounded as a choice *before* he committed.
  Fix in §6.
- **[S4] When a player declines a 7-9 ugly choice, escalate — still untested, but the
  underlying habit is adopted.** No ugly choice was *declined* this session, but the GM
  **offered** one on a 7-9 Defy (drop the sword or stay, 668) and Paxton took it. The
  "lead 7-9 Defy with an ugly choice" texture is now a demonstrated habit.
- **[S3→S5, partial] Re-anchor Castor's ill-fitting drive — ◐ ongoing.** At End of Session
  Castor again can't claim his drive ("I don't feel like I committed mine... I didn't feel
  like I could commit my drive," 1009/1024). The *line* is still unsettled. Castor also had
  the thinnest focus this session (5.3% mic, a shared scene), so the re-anchoring hook was
  thinner — but his wind-form leap to follow Mira and his giving up anonymity to argue for
  the beavers (337-348) are the arc moving in fiction.

**Recurring strengths confirmed:** Portray a Lively World (Vasque's retinue, Gregory, the
ravens), Leave Things Open / collaborative "Ask Them" worldbuilding (the whole session),
and — newly — Think Dangerously actually landing on a PC.

**Recurring growth area — refined.** Two threads had clean, varied roll-by-roll (ugly
choice, a Favor debt, traded conditions). The edge that remains is **spotlight rotation
across split threads** and the lone uncashed social threat — not the dice handling, which
was the strongest of the four sessions.

## 4. The five GM Principles

1. **Encourage Exciting Risks — Weak (this session's weakest principle).** The Beaconhold
   thread was investigation with no risk: Gregory hands over information freely and the
   scene ends on a quest acceptance. _Fix: frame the info as an invitation to danger.
   **"Gregory lowers his voice — 'The ones who went looking for the missing never came
   back, and the last was a scholar who started asking about Sariel by name. If you pull
   this thread, whatever took them will know your face. Do you still want it?'"** Now the
   lead points toward a dangerous course instead of a free briefing._
2. **Portray a Lively World — Strong.** Vasque's retinue, the three family heads, the
   ravens-that-aren't, Senna's reptilian transformation, Gregory's dagger.
3. **Think Dangerously — Strong, best of the four sessions.** A condition on a PC and
   irreversible innocent deaths tied to a PC's choice. See §1 and §6.
4. **Leave Things Open — Very strong; the session's bedrock.** Co-authored backstory,
   geography, and politics, with the GM retconning to improve the game throughout.
5. **Ask, Then Gloss Over — Strong on Ask.** Dozens of well-placed questions built the
   world. The Gloss between vignettes was clean; the one drag is that some worldbuilding
   ran long while three players sat idle — see the cadence note in §3.

## 5. GM Moves — usage, variety, and missed opportunities

**Used well**
- **Introduce Something New / Foreshadow an Ominous Force** — the missing Sariel bust with
  no dust (906-923); Aldric's ravens that "know all that happens in the veil" and veer off
  the witch stones (332-340); Senna reborn "in the vision of Moldrex" (744). Strong
  threats-in-the-world planted for the arc.
- **Inflict a Condition (cause-first) + a Move at an exposed PC** — Iyer tackling Garland
  the moment he lunges for the portal (378). A clean Move at a vulnerable PC.

**Missed / under-used — the demand for Castor.** When the trappers and soldiers arrive
they explicitly want Castor seized over the beaver problem, and Walthallis (furs, beaver
pelts among them) is the instigator pushing to "start with this one" (316, 322). Castor is
a hunted outcast standing in armed reach — a live threat *and* his Druid drive (protect
the beavers, endanger yourself for outcasts) — and the scene resolved through banter and
Mira's portal without a Move firing at him. _Here you could **Set Up an Immediate Danger**:
"Walthallis swings down off his horse, that beaver-fur cloak flaring, and slides a skinning
knife from his belt — 'Lord Garlandson said bring the beaver. He didn't say unharmed.'
He's three steps from you, Castor, and Vasque isn't stopping him. What do you do?"_ Or,
aimed at the community, **Make Them Choose**: _"Vasque is plain about it — hand over the
upstart beaver Tamarack and the families let the rest of the colony be; refuse and the
trappers start at Beaver Lake tonight. Which do you protect?"_ Either turns the political
threat into a decision in the moment instead of an offstage thread.

## 6. 6- and 7-9 handling

_Clean session — lots of varied 7-9 texture. The only fix below is timing, not softness._

- **Paxton — Examine/Scrutinize the body (+WIS) → 6- (total 4, 582).** The GM made a Move
  on the miss — a scaled vine grapples his foot (590) — which is the right instinct on a
  6-. _Verdict: handled adequately._ Mildly generous in that the answer ("the body is
  dead") was still given for free alongside the complication; on a true 6- you can withhold
  the read entirely and let the grapple *be* the answer.
- **Paxton — Engage the vine (+STR) → 7-9 (595).** Hacks free, trades a condition (DEX
  ankle, absorbed by armor). Cause-first, player chose. _Handled well._
- **Paxton — Defy to leap onto the barge (+DEX) → 7-9 (668).** Ugly choice: drop the sword
  or stay flanked in the water. Player chose to sacrifice the sword. _Handled well — model
  7-9 Defy; this is exactly the ugly-choice texture the ledger has been chasing._
- **Paxton — Unleash Radiance (+CHA) → "choose one" → unwanted attention (691-694).** He
  picks the cost and a second creature flanks him. _Handled well._
- **Paxton — burning the barge to escape (Engage/Defy, success, 743-804).** Mechanically a
  success — he burns a trait, keeps his armor-against-fire, and escapes — and narratively
  the boat and everyone on it burns. The **consequence is excellent** (Senna + innocent
  stowaways dead, irreversible, his Drive in action). The only refinement is *timing*: the
  stowaways' screams were revealed as aftermath *after* he was clear (804). To make it land
  on him as a choice, foreshadow before the final burn — **Make Them Choose**: _"As the
  first beams catch the cargo, you hear it under Senna's voice — other people, children,
  stowed in the crates between you and the door. The fire's already between them and the
  water. You can drive it higher and run now, or try to reach them and lose your own way
  out. What do you do?"_ Same grim outcome, but now it's a decision he owns rather than a
  reveal he learns about from the riverbank. _Verdict: strong consequence, handled well;
  foreground the choice next time._
- **Garland — Compel with Particular Set of Skills (+CHA, threat, Advantage) → 7-9
  (414-420).** Iyer yields but Garland now **owes him a Favor** — a relationship/debt cost,
  not a condition. _Handled well — varied, fiction-forward 7-9 cost, and it seeded a
  lasting thread (the Iyer bond)._
- **Garland — tackled into a condition (378-388).** A GM Move (no roll), narrated
  cause-first, player chose STR + "my back," then spent Battle Momentum. _Handled well._

## 7. Conditions, consequences & fictional positioning

- **Conditions narrated cause-first, player choosing stat + manifestation** — Garland's
  tackle (back, STR) and Paxton's ankle (DEX) both done cooperatively.
- **Consequence variety stayed broad:** a condition on a PC, a **Favor debt** (Garland→Iyer)
  as a 7-9 social cost, lost/abandoned equipment (Paxton's sword, by choice), and
  irreversible **collateral deaths** (the stowaways). Across four sessions this is the
  widest, most fiction-driven palette — keep it.
- **Fictional positioning respected** — the GM steered Paxton's attempt to "charm the
  tentacle" back to the fiction ("narratively it doesn't work," 659) rather than allowing a
  bespoke roll, and let the fiction pick the stat on Garland's grab (STR to break the hand,
  not the DEX the GM first reached for, 369-376). Good self-correction.
- The one positioning slip toward *too safe* is the Beaconhold thread (no jeopardy) and the
  uncashed Castor threat — both covered in §4 and §5.

## 8. Missed character moments

- **Castor (Druid — Drive: endanger yourself for wildlife/outcasts).** The whole
  beaver-conflict scene sits on his drive — armed men have come over *his* community and
  want to seize *him* — yet it never became his decision (309-352). Engage it with the
  *Set Up an Immediate Danger / Make Them Choose* in §5. Even a one-line Ask would surface
  it: _"Castor — these men are about to ride off and 'deal with' the colony you spent
  centuries protecting. The beaver in you knows what comes after armed men and trappers.
  Do you let them leave?"_ A Druid playbook Move (tempt his Instinct, or introduce a beaver
  from the threatened colony in need) would do the same work.
- **Roderic (Paladin — test his Virtues; corrupt/rival authority).** The inquisitor he met
  in S5 returns, openly admits he comes from "not a good place" (109-110), and recruits
  Roderic into an alliance — a ready-made Virtue test that stayed a quest hand-off. _Press
  it: "He's the man who nearly branded you a heretic, and now he wants you as his only
  ally. Does a Lightbearer take a corrupt man's commission to chase a righteous end — and
  what does that cost your purity?"_ Engages the Paladin's "test his Virtues" Move instead
  of a clean accept.
- **Paxton (Immolator — have his fire burn something valuable; act on his Drive).** Not a
  miss — strongly engaged. His fire burned valuable lives and his Drive drove it; the only
  refinement is foregrounding the choice (§6).
- **Garland (Fighter/Wizard) — well-served, with a Wizard hook teed up.** His magic
  returning (he recognizes the portal as a far more powerful version of what he once did,
  366), the second *silent* ring of witch stones on the Bonewall, and Mira's untranslatable
  booming tongue are a Wizard mystery planted for next session — good. No notable miss.
