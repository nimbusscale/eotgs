## World Geography (Established)

### Historical Periods

**The Old Empire (Ancient Past)**
- A vast empire that spanned the land (think Rome)
- Ruled by Lucifer — the god-emperor figure the Luciferians now worship
- The empire eventually fell, leading to a "dark ages" period

**The Middle Kingdoms (Post-Empire)**
- A confederacy of kingdoms and city-states that formed after the empire's fall
- Eventually this confederacy also fell apart
- **Edric's yellow scarf** bears the symbols of these Middle Kingdoms — the kings and kingdoms that made up this confederacy

**The Modern Kingdoms (Current Era)**
- More independent and disorganized than the Middle Kingdoms
- Each kingdom is essentially its capital city + immediate surroundings
- Some kingdoms have a main city and a few minor cities
- Outside the cities and main roads = **the wild**

### The Nature of "Civilization"
This is a **points of light** setting:
- Kingdoms may *claim* mountains, valleys, and regions, but they only truly *control*:
  - The capital/cities
  - Main roads
  - Inns along those roads
  - Checkpoints and fortresses
- Go a mile or two off the beaten path and you're in the wild
- Maps are not very good
- The roads are relatively secure; off-road is dangerous

**What's in the Wild:**
- Remnants of the Old Empire
- Ruins of the Middle Kingdoms
- People who don't fit civilization
- Monsters that have cropped up
- The unknown

### Specific Locations

**Roderic's Kingdom (Beaconhold):** The kingdom Roderic grew up in. The Luciferian faith is the **state religion** here — supported but not mandated. Other religions are tolerated but not supported (perhaps frowned upon).
- The **Ashen Vale** and **Eastern Rivers** are border/hinterland regions of this kingdom
- The capital city is where Roderic was raised
- Details to be fleshed out in play

**Other Political Entities:** Multiple other kingdoms and city-states exist in the setting. None have been established yet — these will be developed in play as political intrigue unfolds. (Ken and Ramsey are both interested in this element.)

**The Eastern Rivers:** Border/hinterland region on the eastern edge of the kingdom. Edric's homeland. Remote enough that tax collectors don't visit.

**The Ashen Vale:** A border region to the south of the kingdom, where Garland and Castor live. Associated with curse-related phenomena and home to the legendary Witch Stones (Shepherd's Teeth).

**The Cursed Ruins:** South of the kingdom, near the Ashen Vale. Originally was a functioning town that Garland had relatives in, but has been reduced to ruins by the aging curse. Sits atop a spring whose water now carries the curse downstream.

The party was hired to retrieve a twisted holy symbol from ruins in the mountains above the Ashen Vale. What should have been a straightforward job turned into something far worse. The ruins were not ancient—they had been a thriving settlement where Garland's grandchildren lived, now aged centuries in moments by a spreading curse. The symbol itself was a seal, and by removing it, the party unwittingly released the god of ruin it contained. Now the curse spreads through the mountain springs, flowing downward toward the valley. Only Castor's beaver dam holds it back, but the dam and the beavers maintaining it are aging rapidly. The party realizes they were used—whoever hired Edric knew exactly what would happen. They are no longer just adventurers; they are the heralds of ruin, and the only ones who can make things right.

A modern city-state kingdom; one of many that emerged after the fall of the Middle Kingdoms. The Luciferian faith is the state religion (supported but not mandated).

### Beaconhold (Capital City & County)

- Built around the last intact Farus Lucis  
- The dormant beacon tower is a symbol of ancient connection and inherited legitimacy  
- Seat of the crown; ruled by the King of Beaconhold *(name TBD)*

### Marrow County

- A minor but strategically placed county between Beaconhold county and the Ashen Vale  
- Ruled by Count Albrecht Marrow  
- Deeply entwined with the Church; the Count is a major donor with several sons holding influential clerical positions  
- Those who cross Marrow find their reputations quietly eroded through sermons and homilies referencing their "moral failings" until common folk turn against them  
- Marrow is a well-known collector of whiteglass

### Port City *(Name TBD)*

- Located on the border between the kingdom, the Eastern Rivers region, and the great lake  
- Provides Beaconhold vital water trade with other lakeside peoples

## The Ashen Vale

A fertile valley enriched by volcanic ash from the Cinderwall. An independent region bordering the Kingdom of Beaconhold.

- Home to Garland and Castor  
- Location of the Witch Stones (Shepherd's Teeth)

### Crownvale

- Seat of power in the Ashen Vale  
- Ruled by Aldric Garlandsson, Garland's grandson  
- Aldric styles himself as a would-be petty king of the vale

### Lower Ashen Flow

- The portion of the Ashen Flow that runs through the Ashen Vale  
- Clean and calm river, filtered by the great beaver dam upstream  
- Feeds the fertile farmland of the vale

---

## The Cinderwall (Southern Range)

The southern mountain range forming part of the L-shaped border.

- Wet and cold climate  
- Volcanic; source of the Ashen Vale's fertile ash  
- Wooded slopes, mossy, shrouded in mist and steam

### Ashbrook (Cursed Ruins)

- Once a town high in the Cinderwall, near one of the few mountain passes  
- Location of the reemergence of the God of Ruin  
- Now a cursed ruin; the spring beneath feeds the Upper Ashen Flow, carrying the curse downstream  
- Garland's great-grandchild Rowan yn Greenholt lived here and perished when the town fell

### Upper Ashen Flow & The Great Beaver Dam

- The Upper Ashen Flow originates from a spring beneath Ashbrook  
- Fed by several other rivers and springs from the Cinderwall  
- Wide, dirty, and perilous waters  
- The great beaver dam blocks the Upper Ashen Flow, forming a lake  
- The dam has existed as long as anyone remembers; meticulously maintained, never grown rotten  
- This was Castor's society and home during his years as a beaver  
- The dam filters the water; below it, the Lower Ashen Flow runs clean into the vale

### Dire Coyotes

- A tougher and more aggressive breed of coyote dominates the lower elevations toward the Ashen Vale  
- Humankind is sparse where they roam, so few know of these keen predators  
- Far more dangerous than their pasture-roaming cousins

# The Old Gods and the New

### The World Before Lucifer

Before the rise of the Luciferian faith, humanity honored the **Old Gods**—not as beings to be worshipped in temples, but as forces to be acknowledged in their proper time. The Old Gods weren't "good" or "evil" in a moral sense. They simply *were*. The turning of seasons. Growth and decay. Birth and death. The hunt and the hunted.

The old religion was **cyclical and situational**. You didn't choose a patron deity—you honored whichever force was relevant. Farmers honored growth when planting, decay when composting. Hunters honored the chase, then honored the fallen prey. The dying honored endings; those who mourned honored memory. No force was rejected, because all were necessary.

The Old Gods existed in **balance**. They weren't at war with each other—they were parts of a whole. Day needed night. Summer needed winter. Endings needed beginnings. The world turned, and the gods turned with it.

### The Luciferian Ascent

The New Gods—Lucifer chief among them—offered something the Old Gods never had: **the promise that things could be preserved**.

Where the Old Gods accepted the cycle, the New Gods fought against it. Lucifer brought light to push back darkness, wards to hold boundaries, whiteglass that never decayed. The faithful could build things that *lasted*. They could protect what they loved from the turning of the wheel.

This was genuinely appealing. The old ways accepted loss as natural; the new ways promised you could *keep* things. The Luciferian faith spread not through conquest but through hope.

### The Suppression

As the New Gods rose, the Old Gods were gradually **reframed**. What was once honored as natural became something to be feared. The god of ruin wasn't a necessary part of the cycle anymore—it was a demon, a corrupter, an enemy of all that was good. The wild hunt wasn't sacred—it was a danger to be warded against.

Some Old Gods were actively **sealed away**—imprisoned in whiteglass, the very substance of Luciferian permanence. Others were simply **starved**. Without acknowledgment, without the small rituals that kept them present, they faded. Not dead, but dormant. Diminished. Waiting in the forgotten places.

Most people today don't know there ever *were* Old Gods. The Luciferian faith presents itself as eternal truth, as if it has always been this way. The Old Gods are dismissed as "pagan superstitions" or "dark spirits" or simply never spoken of at all.

### What Was Lost

The Luciferians brought real benefits—safety, stability, the foundations of civilization. But the suppression of the Old Gods created **imbalances** that have been building for centuries:

| Suppressed Force | What It Governed | The Imbalance Created |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Ruin** | Endings, letting go, release | Things that should end don't. Institutions calcify. Old grudges fester. The world becomes cluttered with what has outlived its purpose. |
| **Renewal** | New growth from what has ended | Nothing truly new can emerge. Innovation stagnates. The same patterns repeat. |
| **The Wild Hunt** | Culling, natural selection | Weakness spreads unchecked. Things survive that shouldn't. |
| **The Fallow One** | Rest, dormancy, the necessary pause | Exhaustion everywhere. Endless labor. No respite. |
| **The Forgetting** | Release of memory, moving on | Obsession with history. Ghosts. Inability to heal from the past. |

The world has been slowly sickening for centuries. The Luciferians call it "the darkness pressing in" and redouble their wards. They don't realize they *caused* it.

## The Shape of Things

**What the party believes:** They accidentally freed a god of ruin. The curse is spreading via the river. They need to stop the curse before it reaches civilization.

**What's actually happening:** The river curse is a ruse—dramatic, visible, something heroes can "solve." The real vehicle for the plague is the party themselves. They've become unwitting Heralds of Ruin, spreading decay through every interaction, every act of heroism, every moment of connection with others.

**The cruel irony:** The more they try to help, the more they spread ruin. The more heroic they are, the wider the plague reaches.

## The Forces in Play

### The God of Ruin

**Titles and Epithets** (true name unknown/unknowable):

- **The Unmaker** — Luciferian term, pejorative  
- **The Gentle Collapse** — old honorific, still used by hedge traditions  
- **Lord/Lady of Endings** — formal old religion term  
- **The Patient Dark** — poetic name  
- **Terminus** — scholarly term  
- **The Release** — used by those who honor endings  
- **The Final Kindness** — whispered by those who tend the dying

Still wounded, still potentially sympathetic. Centuries of imprisonment in whiteglass—the substance of preservation—has changed them. What was once gentle release has become something rawer, less controlled. The plague spreading through the Heralds may be unintentional—an overflow of long-suppressed power, not malice.

**Potential angles:**

- The god doesn't know they're spreading plague through the Heralds, and would be horrified to learn it  
- The god knows but can't stop it—their control is shattered  
- The god is too disoriented to understand what's happening at all  
- The plague is the god "remembering" their purpose, but doing it wrong after so long  
- The god's wounds make them unstable—sometimes the old gentleness surfaces, sometimes something harsher

**What the god might want:**

- Acknowledgment that endings are necessary, not evil  
- A place in the cosmic order again  
- To understand what happened to them  
- To find Renewal (if they remember)  
- Simply to rest, to be left alone, to stop hurting

**The tragic possibility:** The god of ruin isn't the villain. They're a victim whose trauma is now causing harm they never intended. Healing them might be how you stop the plague—not defeating them.

**Those Who Honor Ruin**

Ruinous cultists aren't cackling villains. They come to the god of ruin from different places:

*The Grieving* — People who lost everything and found peace only in accepting that loss is natural. The god of ruin doesn't mock their loss—it honors it as the way of things. "When my children died, only the Gentle Collapse offered comfort. Not promises of reunion, not platitudes. Just... permission to let go."

*The Exhausted* — People tired of the endless Luciferian labor of preservation. Why struggle so hard to keep things that want to fall? Let them fall. Let us rest. "Every day we shore up walls, mend what's broken, fight the tide. I'm so tired. The Patient Dark offers something the Light never does: an end to the struggle."

*The Philosophical* — Those who believe the natural order has been disrupted. The old king should have died decades ago. Ancient families hoard wealth across generations. Nothing new can grow because nothing old is allowed to end. "The world is constipated with things that have outlived their purpose. The Unmaker isn't evil—they're necessary."

*The Converted* — Once victims of ruin who came to see it as revelation. When they lost everything, they understood. They were clinging to things that were already gone. "The fire took my home, my shop, my life's work. And in the ashes I finally saw clearly. I had been holding on so tight. The Release showed me I could let go."

*The Desperate* — Those who want something specific to end: a tyrannical ruler, a corrupt institution, an abusive relationship, their own suffering. They come to Ruin not from philosophy but from need. Some find wisdom; others find only the ending they sought.

### The God of Renewal

**Titles and Epithets** (true name unknown/unknowable):

- **The Beloved** — in relation to Ruin, used by those who know the old stories  
- **The Green After Fire** — poetic name  
- **The First Shoot** — old honorific  
- **The Dawn After** — scholarly term  
- **Hope** — sometimes, simply  
- **The Returner** — used by those who await the awakening  
- **The Second Chance** — whispered by those seeking redemption

Dormant. Waiting. When Ruin was sealed, Renewal went into a kind of hibernation—a cocoon state, waiting to emerge when their counterpart was free again. The sealing of Ruin broke something in Renewal too; without endings, there can be no true beginnings.

**Current state possibilities:**

- Physically cocooned somewhere—a sacred grove, a hidden shrine, beneath the earth  
- Beginning to stir as Ruin's power flows again  
- Still dormant but their followers sense the change coming  
- Dreaming, and those dreams are bleeding into the world in strange ways

**What Renewal's awakening might mean:**

- The cure to the plague (Renewal balances Ruin)  
- A new complication (Renewal is also changed by centuries of dormancy)  
- A reunion that could heal both gods—or go terribly wrong  
- A restoration of the old balance that threatens the Luciferian order

**Followers:** Think Celtic/druidic aesthetic. They've kept the old ways in secret—hedge witches, hermits, rural communities that never fully converted, families with long memories. They know Renewal sleeps and have been waiting for the awakening. Some operate openly in the wilds; others hide within cities, appearing devout while keeping the old traditions privately.

### The God of Trickery

The mastermind. Not evil—none of the Old Gods are. A force of chaos, mischief, change, disruption. They embody the truth that plans go awry, that certainty is illusion, that the universe has a sense of humor (and it's not always kind).

**Possible names/epithets:**

- The Laughing One  
- Lord/Lady of Misrule  
- The Crooked Path  
- The Motley  
- The Unexpected  
- The Reversal

**Nature:** Amoral, not immoral. The Trickster doesn't serve good or evil—they serve *change*, *disruption*, *the puncturing of certainty*. They lie, but sometimes their lies reveal deeper truths. They deceive, but sometimes their deceptions free people from comfortable illusions. They cause chaos, but sometimes that chaos breaks stagnant systems.

**Why they orchestrated this (possibilities):**

- The cosmic order has calcified. The Trickster is introducing chaos to a system that's become too rigid, too controlled.  
- They genuinely believe freeing the imprisoned gods is right—they just can't do it straightforwardly. Deception is their nature.  
- They're bound by their essence: even when they want to do something good, they *must* work through misdirection, layers, complications.  
- They find the Luciferian order unbearably *boring*. All that preservation, all that stability. Where's the drama?  
- They have their own wound—perhaps they were the one who wasn't sealed because no one took them seriously enough to imprison. That dismissal festers.  
- They want to see what happens. That's it. They're genuinely curious.  
- They owe Ruin or Renewal something from before the sealing, and this is how they repay it.

**The river ruse:** This is classic Trickster work—give the heroes something obvious to fight, something they can "win," while the real game plays out invisibly. The Trickster knows how heroes think. They need villains, threats, clear objectives. So the Trickster gave them one. Meanwhile, the party becomes the true vector without ever knowing it.

**Possible relationship to events:**

- Directly manipulated Marrow (appearing as advisor, vision, mysterious benefactor)  
- Set up the circumstances for Marrow to find them, making it seem like Marrow's own idea  
- Has agents who did the actual work while the Trickster watched  
- Was the one who ensured the party specifically would be hired (perhaps through reputation manipulation, ensuring they'd be in the right place)  
- Set up the "seal" to infect whoever broke it, knowing eventually someone would

### The Harlequins

Followers of the Trickster. Not a formal cult—more like a loose network of those who've been touched by the Laughing One's influence.

**Aesthetic:** Motley, masks, bells, patchwork. The classic harlequin imagery—but it can range from theatrical and almost playful to deeply unsettling. A Harlequin might be a travelling performer who seems harmless until you realize they've manipulated an entire town into conflict. Or a masked figure in noble courts whose jokes carry poison. Or a wild-eyed prophet speaking truths no one wants to hear.

**What they do:**

- Spread chaos, disruption, change  
- Puncture certainties, expose hypocrisies  
- Test people—are you as virtuous as you claim? Let's find out.  
- Manipulate, misdirect, arrange circumstances  
- Sometimes genuinely help people—but never straightforwardly

**Possible encounters:**

- A Harlequin who seems to be helping the party but is actually steering them toward greater spread of the plague  
- A Harlequin who knows what the party has become and finds it *hilarious*  
- A Harlequin who wants to tell the party the truth—but only in riddles, because that's their nature  
- A Harlequin who is themselves a pawn, not knowing who they truly serve  
- A Harlequin who has doubts about the Trickster's plan but can't act directly against their god

**Tone options:** Harlequins can be played light (mischievous, theatrical, almost likeable despite being untrustworthy) or dark (sinister, alien, their laughter wrong somehow). Mix both for unsettling effect—charming one moment, deeply creepy the next.

### The Church of Lucifer

The status quo. The Luciferians genuinely believe they're protecting humanity from darkness. Their faith has brought real benefits—stability, safety, the foundations of civilization. They don't see themselves as suppressors of the old ways; they see themselves as having *transcended* them.

**What they know (at various levels):**

- Common faithful: The Old Gods are myths, demons, or "pagan superstitions." The Light protects.  
- Local clergy: There are dangerous forces that the wards hold back. Don't ask questions, trust the rituals.  
- Regional leadership: The old powers are real but contained. The church's sacred duty is maintaining those containments.  
- Highest levels: Some may know the full history—the deliberate sealing, the suppression. They believe it was necessary. Perhaps some have doubts.

**Potential responses to current events:**

- Send inquisitors to investigate the curse, possibly targeting the party  
- Attempt to reseal the god of ruin (repeating the original wrong)  
- Declare the party heretics or plague-carriers (ironically correct)  
- Some reformers within the church might question whether the old ways were right  
- Political factions might try to use the crisis for their own advancement

**Tension with Roderic:** A Luciferian paladin discovering the truth about his faith's history. What does he do with that knowledge? This is Roderic's player's story to tell, but the material is there for drama if he wants it.

### Count Marrow

A believer in Renewal, operating within a Luciferian society. His family has kept old traditions secretly—or perhaps framed them as "acceptable" Luciferian mysticism while actually serving older purposes. He wants to awaken Renewal, believing this will heal something broken in the world.

**What Marrow knows (possibilities):**

- Knows about Renewal, knows (roughly) where they sleep  
- Knows the Seal of Unmaking held a god  
- Believed breaking the seal would somehow facilitate Renewal's awakening  
- May or may not know about the Trickster's involvement—he might think he's the one running things  
- Doesn't know about the Heralds or the plague spreading through the party

**How Marrow was used (possibilities):**

- The Trickster appeared to him directly—as a vision, a mysterious advisor, a voice in dreams  
- The Trickster manipulated circumstances so Marrow would "discover" the plan himself  
- Marrow found old family records that were actually planted or altered  
- The Trickster worked through intermediaries and Marrow never knew there was another player

**Marrow's position now:**

- He got what he wanted (the seal broken, Ruin freed, Renewal stirring)  
- He probably didn't want the plague, the chaos, the beaver dam crisis  
- He may be trying to manage the situation, limit damage, stay hidden  
- He may be horrified and looking for ways to fix things  
- He may be doubling down, believing it will all be worth it when Renewal wakes

**Potential grievance with the party:** Keep this in your back pocket. If you need to complicate things, Marrow has some history with one of the characters—a past slight, a grudge, a debt unpaid. Could explain why he specifically chose this party (or ensured they'd be chosen). Not defined until you need it.

**Possible Marrow beats:**

- The party discovers who hired them and confronts him  
- Marrow reaches out, claiming he can help them  
- Marrow becomes an unexpected ally against the Trickster when he realizes he was played  
- Marrow betrays them (if he has reason to)  
- Marrow's position collapses as the church investigates the crisis

## The Heralds of Ruin

When the party broke the seal, they didn't just free the god of ruin—they became carriers of that power. The plague doesn't spread through water or air. It spreads through *them*.

**The mechanism (keep vague):** Don't over-explain how the plague spreads. It happens through interaction, through connection, through the party being *present* in people's lives. The more meaningful the interaction, the stronger the infection. Saving someone's life is more contagious than passing them on the street.

### Each Herald's Burden

The cruel design: each Herald spreads ruin that mirrors their greatest virtue. The thing they're best at becomes the thing that harms.

**Roderic — Herald of Failing Wards**

His gift is protection. His presence makes people feel safe.

*What he spreads:*

- Protections fail. Walls develop cracks. Wards flicker. Locks stick then break.  
- Faith wavers. Certainties crumble. The things people relied on feel less solid.  
- Guardians falter. Those who protect others begin to doubt, to hesitate, to fail at crucial moments.  
- Safety itself becomes suspect. "I thought I was protected, but..."

*The irony:* People seek his protection, and he genuinely protects them—but he leaves behind a world where protection is harder for everyone.

**Garland — Herald of Forgetting**

His gift is knowledge, wisdom, the long view of a man who has seen so much.

*What he spreads:*

- Memories blur. People forget important things—names, faces, why they came into this room.  
- Knowledge becomes uncertain. "I'm sure I knew how to do this..."  
- Understanding fades. Lessons unlearn themselves. Wisdom feels distant.  
- History becomes muddled. Records contradict. Traditions lose their meaning.

*The irony:* He seeks to pass on knowledge before his death, and those he teaches slowly lose the ability to retain what they've learned.

**Edric — Herald of Souring Tales**

His gift is story, reputation, the ability to shape how people see things.

*What he spreads:*

- Stories twist. The tale told becomes a little darker, a little less true.  
- Reputations decline. Good names acquire whispers. Trust erodes.  
- Deals go bad. What seemed fair becomes contested. Trade relationships sour.  
- Words fail. What you meant to say isn't what they heard. Communication breaks down.

*The irony:* The storyteller who trades in words leaves behind a world where words can't be trusted.

**Castor — Herald of Crumbling Foundations**

His gift is building, community, the deep connection between creature and home.

*What he spreads:*

- Foundations crack. Buildings settle wrong. Dams develop weaknesses.  
- Communities fragment. Neighbors become suspicious. Bonds fray.  
- Nature unbalances. Ecosystems destabilize. The harmony between creature and environment breaks.  
- Homes become unhomely. The place you lived feels less like yours.

*The irony:* The builder whose beaver-nature drives him to create and maintain—he leaves behind a world where nothing holds together.

### The Herald Legend

There's a legend about Heralds who come before an age of ruin. Edric knows it—this is Ken's territory to define when the time comes. The party will initially assume the legend refers to external threats. The dawning realization that *they* are the Heralds is the emotional gut-punch of this arc.

**When to introduce:** Drop hints that the legend exists before revealing its relevance. Edric might tell it around a campfire, thinking it's just a story. NPCs might reference it when strange things happen. Let the players start to suspect before confirmation.

**What the legend might contain (for Ken to develop):**

- Four figures (or a number that matches the party)  
- Each brings a different kind of ruin  
- They come in the wake of something freed/awakened  
- They don't know what they are  
- There's probably something about how they can be stopped, cleansed, or redeemed—but interpretations vary

## The River Ruse

The curse on the river is real—but it's a deliberate misdirection. The Trickster (or their agents) set it up to give the party something to fight, something visible and dramatic that heroes can address.

**Why it works:**

- Heroes need villains, threats, clear objectives  
- The river curse is all of those things  
- While the party focuses on the dam, the plague, the racing-against-time drama, the real threat spreads invisibly  
- Every person they meet while "saving" the valley gets infected  
- Their heroism becomes the vector

**What happens when they "solve" it:**

- They probably can solve it—save the dam, stop the water, protect the valley from *that* threat  
- They'll feel like heroes  
- And then strange things start happening to everyone they helped  
- The plague is already everywhere they've been

**The Trickster's fingerprints:**

- If the party looks closely, they might notice the river curse is *too* convenient, too theatrical  
- A Harlequin might have been seen near the spring  
- The curse behaves strangely—almost like it's putting on a show rather than truly threatening  
- Someone might point out that curses don't usually work this way
